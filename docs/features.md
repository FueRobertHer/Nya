## Connecting accounts

Log in, then pick a way to connect, on the Accounts tab or on the empty state before anything is connected. Use it again for each additional institution. Each one is added to your dashboard with a running net worth total.

- **Connect a bank or card**: checking, savings and credit cards with their transactions, plus loans.
- **Connect a brokerage or retirement account**: 401(k)s, IRAs and brokerage accounts, with their holdings and investment activity, including plans and brokerages the bank option can't find. Shown only where the deployment turns it on (`PLAID_BROKERAGE_LINK=1`, see [deployment.md](deployment.md#brokerage-and-retirement-connections)); until then the bank option is the only one.

There are two because one Plaid connection request can't cover both. Plaid's Link lets you pick only an institution that offers every product the request asks for, and a request must ask for at least one. The bank option asks for Transactions, so a 401(k) recordkeeper that offers Investments but not Transactions never appears in its list. The brokerage option asks for Investments instead, which plain banks don't offer, and lists Transactions only for your consent, which Plaid's reference says is "not billed until you start using them by calling the relevant endpoints". Nothing about transactions starts when you link.

Link also "will only display account types that are compatible with all products supplied in the `products` parameter", in Plaid's words; only a bank's own sign-in window lists its accounts as the bank chooses. So a brokerage or retirement connection is for investment accounts, and may not offer a checking account or card at all: connect those with the bank option. Unless it does hold a bank (depository) account or a card, whose transactions Nya then asks Plaid for (see [Keeping Plaid costs down](#keeping-plaid-costs-down)), it brings in no spending transactions, with no warning about it. Its balances, holdings, daily snapshot and estimated history work as for any other institution, and Activity, the Budgets tab and the Plan say when none of your connections brings in spending ([When an institution can't be reached](#when-an-institution-cant-be-reached), and [the Plan's inputs](#where-the-inputs-come-from)).

Bank connections through Plaid work for US institutions only: both link-token routes ask Plaid for `CountryCode.Us`. Beside the Plaid buttons, **Connect with SimpleFIN** connects your own SimpleFIN account instead, which may reach banks Plaid doesn't; Redbark, a separate paid service that speaks the same protocol, covers Australia and New Zealand. Where the deployment turns it on, **Connect with Lunch Flow** connects your own Lunch Flow account by its API key, which reaches banks in many countries through the providers Lunch Flow uses ([Bring your own sync](#bring-your-own-sync)). The app says so beside the ways to connect and on the login and sign-in pages, and points anything else to a [manual account](#manual-accounts), saying that its balance is entered in its own currency.

### More accounts at an institution you already have

To add (or remove) accounts at an institution that's already connected, tap **Manage** on the Accounts tab, then **Add or remove accounts** on its card. This opens Plaid's account picker on the connection you already have, so it stays one connection: no duplicate accounts, and no second connection for Plaid to bill.

If you start connecting and pick an institution you've already connected, Nya stops before you sign in and offers to add the accounts to the existing connection instead. Choose **It's a different login** for a genuinely separate login (a joint or business login, say); that one gets its own connection, made the way you started.

When the connection already there was made the other way (as a bank or card when you started a brokerage or retirement connection, or the other way round), Plaid may not offer the accounts you came for on it, since Link shows only the account types compatible with the products a connection was made with. So the sheet says so: try adding them to it first, and if they aren't offered, choose **Connect it again as** the way you started, and pick only the accounts that aren't connected yet, so none is counted twice. A checking account or card that is added to a brokerage or retirement connection brings its transactions, where Plaid provides them.

When Plaid notices new accounts at a connected institution (it needs webhooks, below), the card says so and offers **Review accounts**, which opens the same picker.

Removing an account in the picker stops Nya receiving new data for it. Its balance history and stored transactions are kept, as they are when you disconnect a whole institution. Recorded past totals don't change, but the estimated part of the chart (before Nya started recording) is rebuilt from the accounts connected now whenever an account is added, so a removed account drops out of those estimates then. Chase doesn't let accounts be removed this way; hide the account, or disconnect Chase, instead.

If Plaid Link asks you to reconnect an institution (shown as a **Reconnect** button on its card), the bank's credentials or MFA changed on their end. Click it and log back in through Plaid to fix it; there is no need to disconnect and relink from scratch. The [Connection health](#connection-health) card says which institutions need this, and why.

In `sandbox` mode, Plaid Link shows fake test institutions. Search for any name (for example "Chase") and log in with username `user_good` and password `pass_good`.

## Removing an institution and adding it back

Sometimes a connection has to be removed and added again (it broke badly, the bank moved platforms, or you are coming back after a while). Plaid then gives every account a new id, so on its own the re-added account would start a new, empty history beside the old one.

Instead, the Accounts tab (under **Manage accounts**, in **Reconnected accounts**) offers to link each new account to the one it replaces, with the evidence and a chart preview. Nothing is linked until you say so. Once linked:

- its **balance history** continues from the old account's, and so does the record of what an investment account held each day;
- if you had **hidden** the old account, the new one is hidden too (a hidden account stays hidden after you disconnect it, listed as disconnected in the Hidden card, where you can still unhide it);
- **categories you set** on the old account's transactions show on the same transactions under the new account (matched by date, amount and the bank's own description of the transaction), and the link says how many carried over. A category you set on a new transaction wins. Two identical transactions on the same day that you categorized differently (or only one of) can't be told apart, so they carry nothing;
- transactions you **excluded** from budgets and reports are excluded again, matched the same way, and ones you put back stay put back (a rule doesn't hide them). Excluding or including a new transaction yourself wins, and two identical transactions you excluded only one of carry nothing;
- the **tags** you put on (or took off) the old account's transactions, and your **notes** on them, show on the same transactions under the new account, matched the same way. Tags you put on or take off a new transaction go on top, and a note you write on it (or clear) wins; two identical transactions you tagged or noted differently carry nothing;
- transactions you **imported** into the old account from files (your bank's, or [another app's](#switching-from-another-app)) are the new one's, and where the new connection brings the same transactions, its own take their place. So does one the bank had changed since it took an imported one's place (a tip added, say), matched by its day, amount and the bank's own description as they were when you disconnected, from the new connection's next sync after you link the two (the next load). Those you said were the old connection's own count again where the new one doesn't have them, so none is lost with it ([Importing history into a linked account](#when-the-bank-sends-the-same-transactions)).

[Splits](#splitting-a-transaction) and [groups](#grouping-transactions) don't carry across: disconnecting takes them with the transactions they were made on (a group keeps its transactions on other accounts, and says one went), so split or group the re-added transactions again. Those on transactions you [imported into an account from its bank's files](#importing-history-into-a-linked-account) stay with them, as the rows do, and count again once the accounts are linked. The disconnect confirmation says how many of the institution's transactions are split or in groups before anything goes, and the Reconnected accounts card says they didn't carry. Groups can't carry, since a group can hold transactions from several accounts, only some of which come back; splits could, matched as categories are, but this release doesn't carry them yet.

Anything not offered can be linked with **Link an earlier account by hand**: there is no time limit, and it works even after you said an offer was not the same account. **Unlink** undoes a link completely; nothing stored is changed by linking.

### What is kept, and forgetting it

After you disconnect an institution, Nya keeps each of its accounts' balance history (and, for an investment account, the record of what it held each day), name, mask and institution, the categories you set, the transactions you excluded and the tags and notes on them, and the transactions you imported into it from files, so a re-added account can pick them up, even months later (splits and groups go with the bank's transactions, [above](#removing-an-institution-and-adding-it-back), and stay with the ones you imported). You decide how long: **Earlier accounts** lists the accounts of institutions you disconnected, and **Forget** deletes one's balance history, holdings history, name, saved categories, exclusions, tags and notes, and imported transactions with their imports, their splits and their places in groups, for good.

- Your past net-worth totals don't change (they were your net worth on those days).
- A hidden account stays out of them: forgetting it takes its amount out of each stored total, so the chart looks as it did while it was hidden, and nothing about the account is kept. Two differences: a day the chart left out because it couldn't tell what the account held that day is deleted, if the account existed then; and a day it left out from before the account existed or after it was last seen comes back, since the account had no part in it.
- While that is under way the account can't be unhidden; if it stops part way, Forget again finishes it.
- Unlink a linked account first.
- An account of an institution that is still connected (a card you closed, say) is listed there once you disconnect the institution.
- An account that [ended](#accounts-that-end) is taken out of the totals after its end by its own balances, so while any are recorded after its end, forgetting it is refused, saying why: it would put the account back into those totals. One not reported after its end, the usual closure, is forgotten as any other, its end and its note on the timeline with it.

Forget can't reach copies made before it: a backup you downloaded (see [operations.md](operations.md#backups)) still has the account, and restoring that backup brings it back, and a file from [Download my data](data-export.md) still lists it. A goal that pointed at the account keeps pointing at it (as an account id only) until you change the goal.

Disconnecting ends the connection at Plaid as well as in Nya, but it can't reach the records Plaid keeps itself, under its own privacy policy. The confirmation links to the [Plaid Portal](https://my.plaid.com), where you can see and delete them.

## Payment details

Credit cards and loans show what they actually cost: purchase APR, minimum payment, and next due date on the account row, with statement balance, last payment, accrued interest, escrow and payoff or maturity date when the row is expanded. A payment coming due within a week, or one already overdue, also surfaces as an alert on the Home tab.

**A student loan's accrued interest and net worth** (awaiting the owner's sign-off). Plaid reports a student loan's balance as its principal, and the interest accrued on it apart (Sallie Mae's aside: their balances include it, and Plaid reports no separate figure). The net worth total on Home stays the balances, as every other figure of it is (the chart and every day Nya records, the Accounts tab's subtotals, the [API](#the-api-and-the-mcp-server), [the download](#downloading-your-data) and a net worth share), and a line under it says how much accrued interest it leaves out, on how many loans, in the loans' own currency, or, once you have chosen a home currency, converted into it at the newest rates as the total is (saying from which currency; one with no rate stays in its own, marked as not converted). The [payoff plan](#debt-payoff-plan) counts the interest as owed, unless you leave it out there (`lib/accrued-interest.ts` holds the rule for both). The interest comes from a call of its own, which can fail while the balances come back, and a servicer may send a loan with none on it: then a line in amber names where those loans are and says their interest couldn't be read, rather than saying nothing, and the total doesn't move. When the reason is known, the line says it instead, and what to do: payment details that aren't enabled for an institution (tap **Enable payment details** on its card on the Accounts tab), payment details still importing, or a bank that couldn't be reached, whose loans show their last known balances until it answers. The other way, counting the interest in the Home total, was built first and set aside: that total would then be a figure no other part of Nya has, and a failed call would raise it by the interest with no word.

This comes from Plaid's Liabilities product, which has to be enabled on an institution before it will return anything. Newly connected institutions get it automatically, through either way of connecting. Institutions you linked before this existed don't, so they show an **Enable payment details** button on their card: tap it, log back in through Plaid, and the terms appear.

That button goes through Link's *update mode*, which re-authenticates the institution you already have rather than adding a second one: the item keeps its id, and its stored transaction history survives. Not every institution supports the product; where it isn't supported the button says so rather than failing silently, and where there's simply nothing to report (no cards or loans) no button appears at all.

## Debt payoff plan

**Payoff plan** on the Accounts tab (shown whenever a credit card or loan is on screen) works out when your cards and loans are paid off and what that costs in interest, paying a steady amount each month.

Each debt starts from its balance and the terms Plaid supplies, and every figure says whether it came from Plaid, was worked out, or was typed. You can type over anything Plaid supplied, and go back to Plaid's figure. The plan waits until every debt has a rate and a payment, or has been left out of it: it never assumes either. Hidden accounts aren't included, and a card or loan with nothing owed is listed but not planned. Each debt in the plan shows roughly what it costs in interest a month at its current balance.

Where the figures come from, and where Plaid's aren't used as they are:

- **Rates.** A card's purchase APR (its highest rate when it reports no purchase APR), and a loan's interest rate. When a card reports the part of its balance each of its rates applied to at the last statement, Nya blends those rates by their parts instead, so a 0% promotional balance isn't charged the purchase APR, and labels the result as its own blend of Plaid's rates rather than a figure from Plaid.
- **Cards paid in full.** A card whose last payment covered its last statement, and was made on or after the day the statement was issued, was paid in full at that statement. No interest is charged while that continues, so it starts out of the plan; put it in to plan it as a balance you carry. A payment made before the statement was on an earlier one, so it doesn't count, and a card whose payment or statement date isn't reported is planned with interest.
- **Mortgages.** Plaid reports the whole monthly payment, which usually includes escrow (taxes and insurance) that doesn't pay down the loan, and it gives no split. So a mortgage's payment is needed, with the reason on the mortgage and by the headline: type the principal and interest from a statement. For a fixed-rate loan the planner offers the payment worked out from the original amount, term and rate, and where Plaid reports no escrow balance you can say there's none and use Plaid's figure. Planned as the payment, escrow would also move on to the next debt once the mortgage was paid off.
- **Student loans.** For loans, Plaid's balance is the principal, and accrued interest is reported separately. Both are owed, so the plan starts from the two together and says so ("includes $612.40 of accrued interest"); you can leave the interest out. Plaid documents one exception: Sallie Mae's balance already includes the interest and it reports no separate figure, so nothing is added there. The accrued interest is then charged interest with the rest, which overstates a federal loan's interest slightly, since those use simple interest.
- **A $0 minimum** from Plaid usually isn't a real payment: Plaid notes that some servicers show $0 under autopay, and it also appears when nothing is due this cycle or a loan is in deferment. It's needed rather than planned as $0. A 0 you type yourself is planned as it is.
- **Shared payments.** Plaid notes that some student loan servicers bill one payment across all of an account's loans and show that same payment on each loan, and names them (Great Lakes, Firstmark, Commonbond Firstmark Services, Granite State and the Oklahoma Student Loan Authority). At those, loans showing the same minimum share one payment, split across them by balance, which the rows and the headline say; if the loans really are billed separately, use each loan's own figure. Anywhere else loans can simply have the same minimum, so the planner asks whether it's one payment for all of them or one each, and the answer applies to the whole group.
- **No terms.** Manual accounts have none, and neither do cards and loans at an institution without [payment details](#payment-details) or one that can't be reached right now, or loans Plaid doesn't cover (auto and personal loans). Type the rate and payment from a statement.

Add an extra amount each month and pick an order:

- **Avalanche** pays the highest rate first, which costs the least interest.
- **Snowball** pays the smallest balance first, which clears debts sooner and can cost more.

Either way the total paid stays the same every month: each debt gets its own payment, the rest goes to the debt at the front, and when a debt is paid off its payment moves on to the next one. Both are shown beside **minimums only**, where each debt gets just its own payment and that payment stops once it's paid off. The plan shows the month everything is paid off and the total interest; what the extra alone saves (the same order with and without it); what the plan saves against minimums only (the extra and the moved-on payments together); the order the debts are cleared in, with each one's month and interest; and a chart of what is owed over time. The headline says "Debt-free" only when nothing owed may be outside the plan: nothing left out, no card or loan without a reported balance, no other currency, and no institution the plan can't see. Otherwise it says "Paid off", with what is outside it.

The plan also says what it can't see, as the Home total does: an institution that couldn't be loaded (any cards or loans there aren't in the plan), accounts that couldn't be recovered or that an institution stopped reporting, and the date of balances recovered from an institution that couldn't be reached.

How the numbers are worked out:

- Interest is charged monthly at the APR / 12 on what is owed and rounded to the cent, and everything is counted in cents, so what is paid always equals what was owed plus the interest, to the cent. Card issuers charge interest daily, which comes to slightly more, so a card's real interest runs a little higher than shown.
- Payments stay at today's amounts. A card's required minimum usually falls with the balance, so paying only what is required takes longer still. New charges and fees aren't modelled, and a promotional rate is taken as lasting, since when it ends isn't known. The first payment is a month from now.
- A payment that doesn't more than cover a debt's interest never pays it off on its own, and the planner says so on that debt. A plan that hasn't cleared everything within 50 years is reported as not paid off within 50 years rather than given a date, and then has no total to measure savings against.
- Debts in different currencies are planned one currency at a time: nothing is converted between currencies. Debts with no standard currency code (Plaid reports an unofficial one) are planned together without a currency symbol, and may not all be in the same currency.

Nothing you type is saved, on the server or on the device. It lasts until the app is reloaded.

## Hiding accounts

Some accounts you want synced but not counted: a joint account, a business card, an old account kept linked for records. On the Accounts tab, tap **Manage accounts** and then **Hide** on any account, linked or manual.

A hidden account keeps syncing and keeps being stored. It's left out of net worth, the balance sheet, the Activity tab, budgets, insights, recurring-bill detection, and the goal picker. Hidden accounts collect in a collapsed **Hidden** card at the bottom of the Accounts tab, where **Unhide** restores them.

Hiding is **retroactive**: the net-worth chart redraws as though the account was never counted, rather than showing a cliff on the day you hid it. That works because nothing is deleted. Snapshots keep recording the true total and every account's balance, and hiding is applied when the chart is read, so unhiding brings back the full history including the period while it was hidden.

Hiding is not a security feature: the data is still fetched and stored, it just isn't shown or counted. To actually remove an account, disconnect it (Plaid) or delete it (manual). A disconnected account that was hidden stays hidden (its history is kept, so unhiding it would change past totals) until you unhide it.

Hiding leaves an account out of every day, past ones too. To stop counting an account only from a day on, because it closed, isn't yours any more, or you stopped following it, give it an end instead ([below](#accounts-that-end)): every day before it still counts.

## Hiding amounts

For using Nya in public, on a shared screen or in a screenshot: the eye in the header, beside Refresh (and on the Reports page), hides every amount on screen and keeps everything else. Balances and net worth, transactions and their day totals (a split transaction's parts and a group's net among them), the month's totals and top spending, the cash flow, budgets and what their meters say, goals, the income pool, the forecast, the calendar and recurring bills, the payoff plan, the Plan, the sharing preview of what someone sees, reports on screen, and every chart's axis, readout and label show **$•••**. The sign and the currency stay where they were (money in still reads "+$•••", a euro account "€•••", an amount in a currency without a symbol "••• DOGE"), the figures go, and one mask stands for any size, so not even a compact "$1.2K" on an axis says how much. A holding's quantity and the amount in a rule are hidden too, and so is an amount in a message from the server, such as a balance that changed since a form opened or what a split's parts came to when it was refused (its counts stay, such as how many parts a split can have), and every figure from a file while it is being imported (a CSV file's lines, where its column names may be, and an amount quoted because it couldn't be read). A field holding an amount shows the same **•••**, however large the amount, until you are in the field (an empty one shows its example, or nothing; a rate or a share in percent shows as it is).

What shows proportions stays: meters and bars keep their lengths, the charts their lines, bands and markers, and percentages stay (a budget at 95%, a card's utilization, a change over a range, a goal's share, the Plan's success rates), since a percentage says no amount. A screen reader hears "hidden" for each hidden amount, in the text and in the labels of charts, meters and buttons, rather than a row of bullets. A choice in a list (a category to budget, with what was spent there) gives its words the same way, as its label, which most screen readers say, though one reading a phone's own list may read its bullets. The switch is a button named Hide amounts, pressed while amounts are hidden.

It is remembered on this device, in the browser's own storage (beside the dashboard's last-known snapshot), and never stored on the server: it follows you to another tab on the device, not to another device. Where the browser keeps nothing (a private window, blocked site data), it lasts as long as the page is open, and a page that can't read the choice shows amounts.

It never changes what leaves Nya: the read-only API and the MCP server, the data download (JSON, CSV and OFX), a report's CSV and every answer from the server carry the figures. A report printed, or saved as a PDF, has its figures too: printing is a deliberate act, so the Reports page shows them while the browser prints and hides them again after. Printing any other page prints what is on screen. Where it applies in the code is in [architecture.md](architecture.md#hiding-amounts).

## A home currency

Accounts in more than one currency (a euro account beside dollar ones, a bitcoin balance Plaid reports) are each shown in their own currency. Until you choose otherwise, net worth adds them up as they are, unconverted, labelled with the currency most of your accounts are in, and says so.

**Choosing one.** When your accounts' balances are in more than one currency, or the last year's transactions are, Home offers to show your totals in one, suggesting the currency your budgets are read in today, and the one most of your accounts are in when that differs. Nothing changes until you choose: Nya never picks one for you. You can also choose, change or stop it on the **Currency** card, under **Manage** on the Accounts tab. The sheet lists each manual account you typed with its currency, so one that is in another can be corrected before you confirm: only its currency changes, never the balance you typed. Beside a correction, the sheet (and the account's own form) offers to move the transactions you entered on it to the new currency too, with how many; only if you tick it, and never one imported from a file, split or in a group, nor one more precise than the new currency allows (12.34 can't be in yen). Each keeps the number you typed.

**What it converts.** Your net worth: Home's total, its change over recent days and its chart, an institution's total on the Accounts tab when its accounts are in different currencies, and the share of net worth Connection health names. Your spending and income: the Activity tab's totals, the month and cash flow charts, tags and places, and the insights on Home, each transaction at its own day's rate. Reports, in the home currency unless you pick another, each row in the appendix with its own amount and what it counts as. The weekly summary's week against a usual one (still never an amount), and what you share (below). The API's new fields ([below](#the-api-and-the-mcp-server)). Every account, transaction and holding is still listed in its own currency, and a manual account stays in its own, its balance converted for the total.

**What keeps its own currency.** What you typed is never relabelled. Budgets stay in the currency they are typed in (the one most of the last year's transactions are in, as before), and spending in another currency counts against them converted into it, so the meters, rollover carries, the income pool and suggestions count everything with a rate; the tab says so. The Plan stays in the currency most of its accounts are in, which what you typed there is in, and converts the year's spending, income and savings (each at its day's rate) and your invested assets and allocation (at the newest rates) into it, so the FI number and your assets compare. The cash forecast stays in its cash accounts' main currency, which its warning is in, and converts other accounts and expected amounts at the newest rates (a day to come has no rate of its own); the calendar counts each entry in another currency converted, listing it in its own. Recurring bills' monthly total converts each at today's rates. A goal stays in its account's currency; one that tracks no account says none, as before. The debt planner plans each debt in its own currency.

**Shares.** Once you choose a home currency, a net worth (and its history), spending or holdings share is in it, and a budgets share counts spending in other currencies against the budgets' own currency. A share converts at the European Central Bank's daily rates alone, never at your own rates, so nothing in it can give one of yours away, beside an account's own balance or otherwise. What only a rate of your own would convert (a currency the ECB doesn't publish, such as ARS or BTC) is left out of the converted figures and named, "1 account in ARS isn't in this total: there is no public rate for it", and the account still shows in its own currency wherever its balance is shared. So a share's converted total can differ from your own where your rates differ from the ECB's. If your home currency itself is one the ECB doesn't publish, a share converts nothing, as before a home currency. The person you share with is told it is converted at the European Central Bank's daily rates, never a rate, and the preview in Sharing shows exactly what they get.

**The rates.** The European Central Bank's euro reference rates, published each working day around 16:00 CET for about 30 currencies, which Nya fetches once a day. A balance now converts at the newest; each day of the chart at that day's, a weekend or a holiday at the last working day's. They are reference rates, published for information: a bank's own rate can differ. The note under the total says what was converted, at which day's rates, each currency's part and what it came to, with the rate (rates show even while amounts are hidden: they are no amount of yours). A currency the ECB doesn't publish (a cryptocurrency, the Argentine peso) is left out and named, never guessed, until you set a rate for it, and when Nya couldn't fetch newer rates every converted figure says from which day the rates are.

**Your own rates.** On the Currency card: "1 BTC = 61,000 USD", from a day on or for every day, in the euro or a currency the ECB publishes (converted through the ECB's rate for it each day). It is used instead of the ECB's on those days, and the card shows the ECB's beside it, so a bank's markup is visible. Changing or removing one changes every figure it reaches, past days included, since nothing converted is ever kept.

**The change over recent days** is worked out from the converted chart, each day at its own rate, so a move in rates is in it; when that is more than rounding, it says how much: "€310.22 of it from exchange rates".

**Nothing converted is kept by the server.** Each figure is worked out again when it is shown, from the balances as recorded, so choosing, changing or stopping a home currency changes nothing stored: the history keeps recording the sums it always has (your device keeps the last figures it showed, as it keeps the rest of what the app last showed, for opening offline). Your choice and your own rates are kept in your encrypted settings, and are in your data download; the ECB's rates are public, kept once for everyone and left out of Nya's nightly backups. In the API, accounts, net worth and balance history give the converted figures in fields of their own, beside the ones they always had ([below](#the-api-and-the-mcp-server)). How it works is in [architecture.md](architecture.md#exchange-rates).

## Accounts that end

Accounts end: a card you closed, a loan paid off, a cash jar emptied (**closed**); a brokerage account transferred, a joint account divided, a car sold with its loan (**no longer yours**); or one you simply stop following (**stop following**). On the Accounts tab, tap **Manage** and then **Close…** on any account, linked or manual: it asks which, from which day (up to today), and, for the first two, a note if you like ("Moved to Chase"). When the account still shows a balance, the sheet says what ending it does to net worth now ("It still shows $310.00 owed. From Mar 12 that debt stops counting, so your net worth rises by $310.00"), and a card or loan that still owes waits for a tick, **Stop counting what it owes**, before it is saved: a debt that stops counting must never look like money gained by accident.

- **Every day before the end is untouched.** The money was there, and the history is a record of what was measured.
- **From the end on, the account doesn't count**: not in net worth now, not in any point of the chart after it (measured and estimated alike), not in allocation, the FI figures or the Plan's inputs, not in budgets or spending from that day (its transactions before the end stay), not in what you share, and not in the API. **Closed** and **no longer yours** are the same to all of these and differ only in what they say: you know which happened, so Nya keeps it.
- **Stop following is not hiding.** A hidden account is left out of every day, past ones too; one you stopped following still counts on every day before the one you chose. The sheet says so beside the choice.
- **Nothing is deleted.** The end is applied when history is read, the way hiding is, and nothing is ever written to the recorded history for it. Moving the end further back recomputes the later points; **Reopen** puts every point back exactly. Reopening an account its bank no longer returns can't make the bank return it: it is held at its last known balance and asked about, as any missing account is ([below](#when-nya-asks)), until the bank does or you say it closed.
- Ended accounts collect in a collapsed **Accounts that ended** card on the Accounts tab, with when each ended and its note, to change or reopen. An end follows the account across a [re-link](#removing-an-institution-and-adding-it-back), and stays after its institution is disconnected.
- **An account SimpleFIN or Lunch Flow keeps up to date** ends the same way, with **Close…** under **Manage**. Once it has ended it holds nothing at its bank: its going missing from the service's answer, or anything the service says about it alone, holds neither the bank's total nor its older history, and its balance is recorded only while an answer measures it. Ending it doesn't stop the service updating it; stopping tracking it does ([disconnecting](#disconnecting)), and an account you let go keeps its end, whether kept as a manual account or removed.
- **The connection.** Plaid charges for a connection until it is removed, so when the last open account of a connection ends, Nya asks whether to remove the connection, through the same confirmation as any removal. Disconnecting is about the connection and closing is about an account: disconnecting doesn't say an account closed, and its confirmation says so.
- The change under the Home total says when it includes an account that ended in its days, so the account leaving isn't read as a gain or a loss.
- **Sharing.** An account you share that ended shows as ended to the other person from its day, with no balance, and only its transactions from before it.
- **Reports.** A [report](#reports) leaves its transactions out from its day, as the Activity tab does, and names it among the sources: closed, no longer yours or not followed, from its day, with how many of its later transactions are in Nya and left out. One you stopped following is still yours, so its later transactions are a gap the report names; so is an assumed closure, and the days an account was missing before it came back.
- The [data download](data-export.md) carries each account's end and every note, an ended bank account's or card's OFX statement says when it ended and what of it is dated from then, and [the API](#the-api-and-the-mcp-server) gives each account a `lifecycle` field.
- **Transactions imported from its bank's files** count as the bank's do, before its end only, and it still takes a file, from the Accounts that ended card ([An account that ended](#an-account-that-ended)).

### When Nya asks

Banks don't say that an account closed. Some stop returning it; others return it at zero. Either way, Nya asks rather than guesses.

- **An account missing from an otherwise good answer.** Until you answer, it is held at its last known balance, labeled as such ("Last known balance, from Mar 12"), or shown without one and not counted when that is older than 35 days, and no total is saved, exactly as when a connection fails: a closed card dropping out would otherwise read as good news. The question is on the institution's card and in Connection health: "Chase Savings ••4821 hasn't appeared since Mar 12. Closed?", with **Closed** (the day after it was last seen, prefilled), **Still open** and **Stop following**.
- **If nobody answers** within three days, Nya assumes it closed the day after it was last seen, so totals are saved again rather than held forever. Nothing moves that was measured: the days it was seen keep it, and the days after hold no balance of it. The assumption is shown as one, with its note on the chart, in the Accounts that ended card (opened, with **Yes, closed** and **Still open**). An answer you gave about it going missing, or an end you set, is never replaced by an assumption; an answer to another question (Still open about a zero balance) says nothing about this one, and is.
- **If it comes back after that** (a bank's glitch that outlived the three days), the assumption is dropped and the account counts again. The totals saved while it was assumed closed are short of it, and stay as they were saved; the chart leaves those days out, as days with nothing recorded, rather than draw a dip that never happened, and a note on the timeline says so ("Ally Savings ••4821 was missing at Ally from Mar 13 to Mar 18, so the totals saved then, short of it, are left out"). That is the cost of the way out: a few dashed days instead of a pause with no end.
- **Still open** keeps it held while it stays missing, for two weeks from your answer: no total is saved meanwhile, for any institution, so one answer can't pause your history for good. Then Nya asks again ("You said … was still open, but it still hasn't appeared"), and, unanswered for three more days, assumes it closed as above. Reconnecting may bring it back, or you can say it closed. Connection health and the note under the Home total say it is your answer that holds it, and for how long.
- **A balance at zero for four weeks**, measured on at least fourteen of those days, **with no transaction since** (from its bank: a row you imported from a file is never one), raises the same question, with the first day of the zeros prefilled. Nothing is held for it: a zero was measured, and it counts the same whether the account is open or closed, so the answer only tidies the account list and marks the chart. Still open stops the question until a new run of zeros begins.
- **A closed or left account its bank still returns**, if its balance moves from the one it showed when you ended it (a closed one's taken as zero when it wasn't known), or a transaction its bank sent for it is dated after then, is asked about again on its institution's card and in Connection health: "Chase Freedom ••9902, closed Mar 13, shows $450.00 owed. Still closed?", with **Still closed** (or **Still not mine**) and **Reopen**. It counts in nothing meanwhile, so a card used again, or a fee that lands, never moves net worth unseen. On a debt it says is owed, Still closed waits for the same tick as closing one, with the amount named ("Stop counting the $450.00 it owes"). Still closed asks no more until it moves again. Its transactions are checked once a day, so one is asked about the day after it lands at the latest; a balance that moves, at once. An account you stopped following is expected to move, and is never asked about.
- A hidden account that goes missing holds every total too (the recorded totals include it), so it is asked about as well, though it counts in nothing either way.
- None of these is asked about a manual account, nor about one SimpleFIN or Lunch Flow keeps up to date: when the service stops returning one, its bank's card is held and says what to do (stop tracking it with **Choose accounts**, or, if it closed, say so with **Close…**).

### Notes on the timeline

A step in the chart can be alarming: an account that closed, a house bought, a 401(k) rolled over. Nya doesn't smooth the line: it marks the day. Each note draws a dotted line and a small marker on its day, and scrubbing to the day shows the note beside the figure. The notes in the range are also listed under the chart, where a keyboard reaches each one (choosing it selects its day) and a screen reader announces it.

Nya writes a note when an account ends ("Ally Savings closed: Moved to Chase", or "assumed closed: it stopped appearing at Ally"), and the note follows the account: changing the end moves it, reopening removes it. Add your own in the **Notes on the timeline** card on Home: a day up to today, and up to 200 characters, up to 500 notes in all. Notes are stored encrypted, like everything else.

## When an institution can't be reached

Plaid connections fail: a bank has an outage, a login expires, an item needs re-authenticating. When that happens Nya shows the institution's **last known balances** with the date they were taken ("Could not fetch balances, balances as of Aug 7") rather than a $0.00 card.

That's not cosmetic. Dropping a failed institution from the total silently understates your debts as well as your assets, and a credit card falling out makes net worth go *up*: a broken connection that reads as good news. Showing the last real figures keeps the total honest while the connection is down.

Two stores back this. Balances come from the most recent daily snapshot, which is only recorded on days when **every** institution answered. Alongside it, each institution's account list is recorded every time **that** institution answers, so a card you closed drops out on the next successful load rather than lingering. Those two have different conditions, so they drift, and an account can be in one and not the other. When that happens the card can't show every row, and it says so: *"2 accounts couldn't be shown, so this total is incomplete."* That matters because a missing row is usually a missing debt, and a missing debt makes net worth look better than it is. Everything is scoped per institution, so one bank's problems never affect another's recovery.

If the balances are older than **35 days** the card stops showing them and names the date instead. An institution broken for months shouldn't quietly revert to zero, and it shouldn't drag a months-old figure into today's total either. Any institution that can't be reached and can't be recovered is called out under the Home total by name, with when it was last seen ("Chase needs reconnecting, last seen Sep 12, and isn't counted in this total"), so a total that's missing a whole bank never looks complete. The other notes under the total name their institutions too, rather than only counting them.

Recovered balances are **display-only**. They're never written to the net-worth history, and no snapshot is recorded on a day when any institution failed, so a stale figure can never be mistaken for a measured one in the chart. The consequence is a real gap: if a connection stays broken through the end of the day, that day gets no point at all and nothing back-fills it later. That's deliberate, because a fabricated flat line in the real history layer would be permanent: nothing ever rewrites a past date.

An institution that needs re-authenticating keeps the red warning and its **Reconnect** button, and only says the balances are dated. The softer amber note is reserved for failures that usually clear on their own, so a dead connection can't hide behind plausible-looking numbers.

A related case: an account that disappears from an otherwise successful fetch (a closed account some banks simply stop returning) is held at its last known balance and holds the snapshot, called out under the Home total, while Nya [asks whether it closed](#when-nya-asks). Unanswered for three days, it is assumed closed from the day after it was last seen, shown as an assumption you can correct. A glitch resolves itself inside that window; a real closure costs a few days of gap rather than a wrong total written permanently, and a glitch that outlives the window costs the days saved without the account, which the chart then leaves out, with a note saying why.

The same honesty reaches **Activity**. When an institution's transactions couldn't be loaded, or its connection is broken and last synced before a month ended, that month's totals say so under the figures ("Chase hasn't synced since Sep 12, so this month may be missing some of its transactions"). A month that ended before the connection last synced is left alone: nothing it holds is missing. The month's budgets on the Budgets tab say the same, since spending that is short makes a budget look safer than it is, and so does Home under its budget alerts, spending pace and biggest purchase, which read the same month.

Connections that bring in no transactions are said for what they are. When none of yours can bring in spending (every one holds only investment accounts, say) and there are no transactions from anywhere else, Activity says "Your connected accounts are investment accounts, so there are no bank or card transactions to show" rather than "No transactions in the last 12 months", and each budget shows its limit and why there is nothing against it rather than $0 of it. Transactions [entered by hand](#transactions-on-a-manual-account) or [imported from a file](#importing-files) are spending all the same: with any, they are shown and counted as usual, and the connections that bring in none are named under the month's totals and budgets ("Empower holds no bank account or card, so no transactions come from it"). A bank account or card that Plaid doesn't provide transactions for is named under each month's totals, the month's budgets and Home's budget alerts and spending pace, since its spending is missing from them; so is one whose transactions you didn't allow Nya to see when you connected it, with the way to bring them in: its card on the Accounts tab offers **Allow transactions**. Neither is named for a month that files you [imported into its accounts](#importing-history-into-a-linked-account) cover for every one of its bank accounts and cards (an account that ended only up to its end, a hidden one not at all): their transactions are there in the bank's place, as a [report](#reports) and what [rolls over](#budget-rollover-and-income-carryover) count them. A connection that holds no bank account or card is never named as missing transactions, not even when its sign-in lapses: it never brings any in.

### Connection health

On the Accounts tab, the **Connection health** card lists every linked institution in one place. It stays collapsed to one line while everything works and opens on its own when something doesn't. For each institution it shows:

- its state: working, reconnect soon (with the date), needs reconnecting, not updating (an outage or a temporary error), needs connecting again, no open accounts, or missing accounts;
- when it last synced, as a date. Every load that reaches the institution and the daily snapshot record it;
- whose side the problem is on: your sign-in at the bank, the bank, Plaid, SimpleFIN or Lunch Flow for a bank it feeds, or Nya itself. A broken link looks like a Nya problem from the outside, so it says which;
- which accounts are affected, and how much of your net worth is shown from last known balances rather than measured now, labeled as such. Accounts with nothing to recover are named as not counted;
- the one thing to do about it.

Plaid's error codes decide the state, in one mapping (`lib/connection-state.ts`), and the action follows from it:

| What happened | Plaid's codes, for example | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Your sign-in needs redoing: a changed password, a new security step, a consent that ran out | `ITEM_LOGIN_REQUIRED`, `INVALID_CREDENTIALS`, `ACCESS_NOT_GRANTED`; `ITEM_LOCKED` and `USER_SETUP_REQUIRED` need something done at the bank first | **Reconnect**, which goes through Plaid's update mode: it keeps the connection and its history, and takes a minute. |
| The bank or Plaid is having trouble | `INSTITUTION_DOWN`, `INSTITUTION_NOT_RESPONDING`, `INTERNAL_SERVER_ERROR`, rate limits, no answer at all | Nothing: it usually recovers on its own. |
| Plaid refuses Nya itself: what a deployment mistake looks like, such as a `PLAID_ENV` or `PLAID_SECRET` for another environment | `INVALID_ACCESS_TOKEN`, `INVALID_API_KEYS`, `UNAUTHORIZED_ENVIRONMENT`, any `INVALID_REQUEST` or `INVALID_INPUT` error; or Nya can't read the access token it stores | Nothing for you: whoever runs Nya puts the settings or the keys right, and the connection then works as before. Never removal, which would delete its stored transactions and leave the connection live at Plaid. It is never emailed; the daily job's log tells whoever runs Nya. |
| The connection is gone and can't be repaired | `USER_PERMISSION_REVOKED`, `ITEM_NOT_FOUND` | **Remove** it and connect the bank again. The new accounts get new ids, and [linking them to the old ones](#removing-an-institution-and-adding-it-back) carries the history over. Where Plaid can't reach the institution any more (`ITEM_NOT_SUPPORTED`, `INSTITUTION_NO_LONGER_SUPPORTED`), connecting again won't help, so only removal is offered; the history is kept. |
| Accounts were closed at the bank | `NO_ACCOUNTS`, or an account missing from an otherwise good answer | If you closed them, remove the connection (history is kept). For one missing account, answer the question beside it ([When Nya asks](#when-nya-asks)): until then it is held at its last known balance; unanswered, it is assumed closed after three days, and Still open holds it for two weeks at most before Nya asks again. Removed on purpose? Check with **Add or remove accounts**. |

A code Plaid adds later reads as "not updating" with the code shown, never as a guess at one of the others. The **Reconnect** button on an institution's card follows the same mapping, so the card and the health view always agree.

If Nya can't read some of what it keeps about a connection (a damaged entry, or one a later version wrote), the card says so, open or closed, rather than looking fine: a warning that the connection is about to end may be missing. Nya leaves such an entry as it is; reconnecting or removing the connection clears it.

A bank whose accounts come through your own SimpleFIN account has a row here too, marked **via SimpleFIN**, in SimpleFIN's words and with what to do about it there ([When SimpleFIN can't be read](#when-simplefin-cant-be-read)), and one through your own Lunch Flow account is marked **via Lunch Flow**, in Lunch Flow's ([When Lunch Flow can't be read](#when-lunch-flow-cant-be-read)).

### Reconnect soon

Plaid warns about a week ahead when a working connection is going to end: `PENDING_EXPIRATION` when the consent the bank gave runs out (with the time it does), and `PENDING_DISCONNECT` when the bank is ending connections, for example while it moves to a new connection method (with no time; Plaid says it sends it seven days ahead, so the date shown is an estimate, said as "around"). Nya records each warning for its connection, encrypted, and the institution's card shows a **Reconnect soon** badge with the date and a **Reconnect** button. Three days before the date, Home raises it too. Some banks' consent has an end date Plaid reports on every fetch; within a week of it, the card says Reconnect soon even when no webhook arrived (webhooks need `PLAID_WEBHOOK_URL`, [below](#keeping-plaid-costs-down)).

Reconnecting through update mode clears the warning, and so does Plaid's `LOGIN_REPAIRED` (a repair made in another app) or removing the connection. A warning a repair outran, such as one Plaid delivered again afterwards, ends by itself once the connection answers past the date it named, or Plaid reports the consent renewed.

### Email notices

When a connection breaks, the daily snapshot sends one email, and one reminder a week later if it is still broken. Nothing more: an email every day is an email nobody reads. A break begins at the first daily run that finds it and ends at the first run that finds the connection working again, or when you reconnect or remove it; the next break gets its own email.

- **What is worth an email, and when.** At once, what plainly needs you: a sign-in to redo, a connection to remove and make again, a bank reporting no open accounts, and Plaid's warning that a connection will end. Only once the connection has gone three days without answering: an outage, which needs nothing from you, and anything on Plaid's side or that Nya can't place, which one fault can bring to everybody at once, so whoever runs Nya has days to see it first. Never: a problem on Nya's side (the row above), since there is nothing you can do about it. A missing account is asked about on its card and in Connection health, and is not emailed: unanswered, it is assumed closed after three days, and an answer of Still open holds it for two weeks at most before Nya asks again, so it never holds your history for longer without asking.
- **Again only if what it needs from you changes.** A break first emailed as an outage ("nothing to do yet") that then needs you, a sign-in say, gets its own email at once, and its own reminder a week later. Each state is emailed at most once a break, so a connection that flaps between two never repeats itself.
- **One email per run**, naming each connection that needs you and what to do, with a link that opens the Connection health card (`APP_URL/?view=connections`). The link works signed out too: you come back to the card once signed in.
- **Held back, for three days, when it looks like a fault many people share.** A deployment mistake or a fault at Plaid gives many accounts the same Plaid code at once. So when emails about a cause on Plaid's side or one Nya can't place first become due on the same run for three or more accounts with the same code, those emails wait three days, and the log tells whoever runs Nya, with the day they go. If the problem is still there then, each goes, once, and is never held again; fixed in time, nothing goes. Only emails first due on that run, and with that code, are held: a different problem that matures the same day, or a later one, goes as usual. A hold is best effort: one the run had no time left to record only means that email isn't held, and it goes with the next run. An email about a sign-in or the bank's own problem is never held: no deployment mistake makes a bank ask for a new sign-in, and a bank asking everyone at once is exactly when each person should be told.
- **Never twice.** Each break is recorded before its email goes and marked sent only once the email service accepts it, so a run again (the catch-up two hours later, or a cron delivered twice) sends nothing new, and a failed send is tried again by the next run. Each email also carries an idempotency key, so a retry after an answer that never arrived isn't delivered twice.
- **What an email says:** the institution's name, what to do, and for a connection about to end, around which day. Never a balance, an amount or an account number.
- **Who gets it:** with Clerk, the primary email address of the account that owns the data, once Clerk has verified it, and only while the account is still allowed in. With the shared password, `NOTIFY_EMAIL`, and only for the deployment's own data.

Email is sent through [Resend](https://resend.com) and needs `RESEND_API_KEY` and `MAIL_FROM` ([deployment.md](deployment.md#email-notices)). Without them nothing is sent, the log says so once, and the Connection health card is the only place a broken connection shows. A download of your data and a new API token each send an email of their own, to the same address ([below](#downloading-your-data)). So does the [weekly summary](#the-weekly-summary), if you turn it on.

Only Plaid connections are emailed about. A problem with a bank SimpleFIN or Lunch Flow feeds shows on the Connection health card, on the connection's own card and under the Home total, but sends no email yet.

## Manual accounts

Plaid's coverage is wide but uneven: small credit unions, HSAs, some 401k recordkeepers (try **Connect a brokerage or retirement account** first, where it is turned on), foreign banks, and anything that isn't a financial institution at all (property, crypto held off-exchange) may simply not be linkable. Those get tracked by hand, or, for a bank your own SimpleFIN or Lunch Flow account reaches, through it ([Bring your own sync](#bring-your-own-sync)).

Click **Add a manual account** (on the Accounts tab, or on the empty state before anything is connected), give it a name, an institution, a type, its currency and a balance. The currency starts as your home currency if you chose one, else the one most of your accounts are in; an account added before a currency could be chosen is in US dollars until you change it under **Update**. Changing it never changes the number you typed: the account and its history are counted in the new currency from then on, and its row says the currency when it isn't the one your totals are shown in. The transactions already on it keep the currency they were entered in, unless you tick **Also move the transactions you entered on it** (shown beside a corrected currency with how many, counted by the server, so older ones the page hasn't loaded count too): then the ones you typed move to the new one, each with its number as typed, and never one imported from a file, split or in a group, and once saved the form says how many moved. There is no undo, and the offer says so: which ones moved isn't kept, so changing the account back offers to move every transaction you typed on it in that currency, not only those. The next file imported into it that doesn't say its currency is read in the new one. Accounts sharing an institution name group into one card. Choose **Cash on hand** for the cash in your wallet (it is kept like a checking account, marked as cash), so the [Plan](#where-the-inputs-come-from) can tell the cash you take out of the bank and the cash spending you enter on it are the same money, and so a wallet running low raises no **Low balance** alert on Home, which is for an account that could be overdrawn; an account added before that choice existed is a checking or savings account until you say otherwise under **Update**. From then on it behaves like a linked account: it counts toward net worth, appears in the Accounts tab, is selectable as a savings-goal source, and gets its own balance history chart.

The balance holds flat until you change it, and each update is recorded on the timeline, so the chart shows a step at each update rather than a pretend-smooth curve. Credit and loan balances are entered as the **amount owed** (a positive number) and subtract from net worth. Deleting a manual account is not reversible: re-adding it creates a new account with a fresh id and an empty history, and the transactions entered on it are deleted with it.

### Transactions on a manual account

Spending in cash, or at a bank Plaid can't reach, can be entered by hand. Tap **Add a transaction** at the top of the Activity tab, or **Add transaction** on a manual account's row on the Accounts tab. The form is built for a phone: the amount first, on the decimal keypad (a comma works as the decimal point), **Spent** or **Received** instead of a minus sign, then who was paid, the date (today), the account (the one whose row you started from, or the one you used last on this device), a category from the ones you already have (filled in from the last time you entered the same payee) and an optional note. Any currency works; it starts at US dollars. The amount is rounded to the currency's smallest unit (a cent, a yen, a fils), and the button shows it before anything is saved; a doubled or stray separator ("12..50", "1.2.3") is not read as an amount at all, so a typo can't become a hundredfold one. The date is today or earlier: a transaction is something that happened, and one dated ahead would count in this month's totals before it did (the server allows one day ahead, for a phone in a time zone already in tomorrow). Cash taken out of another of your accounts and put in a wallet is a transfer, not income: the form suggests the **transfer in** category when you tap **Received**.

A transaction entered by hand shows in the Activity tab with its account's name and institution and "entered by hand", and counts everywhere a bank's transaction does: the month's totals and chart, budgets, insights, recurring-bill detection and the Plan's spending. Tap it to change its category, to **Edit or delete** it (or move it to another manual account), or to exclude it. Its category and payee are its own, so changing the category edits the transaction itself; vendor renames, which relabel a bank's merchant, don't apply to it. Transactions on a hidden manual account are left out, like any hidden account's. Shared at **Balance and transactions** with someone you're connected with, a manual account's transactions from the last 30 days are shared like a bank's, each in its own currency, without your notes or categories ([sharing](authentication.md#sharing-between-people)). A save shows at once, without reloading the tab or asking any bank again.

**Other currencies.** Until you choose a [home currency](#a-home-currency), nothing is converted between currencies, so a total adds up only the currency most of your transactions are in, and says how many in others it left out ("1 transaction in JPY isn't in these totals, which are in USD"); each one is still listed, in its own currency. Once you choose one, every total here is in it, each transaction in another currency converted at its own day's rate, and the note under the totals says how many were converted; only one with no rate is still left out, and named with why. That goes for every total, a bank's transactions in another currency included: the month's money in and out, its chart, trend, categories, places and channels, each day's subtotal (one figure per currency), the budgets, the bills' monthly total, the Home insights (no budget alert, pace or biggest purchase is taken from another currency) and the Plan. A transaction with no currency code at all, which only the oldest stored history has, is taken to be in its account's currency, or, when that isn't known either, in the currency most transactions are in, and the note under the totals says how many were taken that way.

**The balance stays what you typed.** A manual account's balance feeds net worth and its history, and transactions you enter beside it need not add up to it, so adding one doesn't move it. The form says so, and offers **Also update the balance**, showing the balance before and after. Ticking it records the new balance exactly as the account's **Update** form would (and the estimated history is rebuilt around it), and only from the balance the form showed to the one it said: if the balance has changed since the form opened, or the account changed so that the money would move it the other way, nothing is saved and the form says so. It is offered only for a transaction in US dollars, the currency manual balances are kept in, and never when it would take an amount owed below zero. Editing or deleting a transaction never moves the balance. The estimated history behind the chart keeps holding manual accounts flat: it never walks a typed balance back through transactions typed beside it.

**Saved once.** The form gives the transaction its id when it opens, so tapping **Add** again after an answer that never arrived (a phone losing its connection) finds it already saved: one transaction, and the balance moved once. If you changed the amount or the account before tapping again and the balance moved (or was to move) with it, nothing more is changed and the form says what was saved, so the transaction and the balance never disagree; edit the transaction, then update the balance from the account. Two transactions of the same amount added at the same moment on two devices each move the balance once: the second is saved, and says its balance wasn't updated. Moving a transaction to another account changes both accounts in one step, so it is never on both, or on neither, whatever fails.

Each account's transactions are stored together, encrypted and compressed, on the storage seam (`lib/manual-txns.ts`); one account holds some 80,000 of them, and a save that would not fit is refused whole rather than trimmed. Each carries where it came from and that source's own id, so a [file import](#importing-files) can tell a transaction it already has from a new one, and the import it came in, so one import can be taken out whole. They are in the [download of your data](data-export.md#manual-transactions).

### Updating balances from a script

Retyping balances gets old. If you set `INGEST_SECRET`, anything that can make an HTTP request can push balances into your manual accounts:

```bash
curl -X POST https://your-app.vercel.app/api/ingest/balance -H "Authorization: Bearer $INGEST_SECRET" -H 'Content-Type: application/json' -d '{"updates":[{"account_id":"manual_...","balance":1234.56}]}'
```

The `account_id` is shown in the account's edit dialog. The response reports each id's outcome (`updated`, `not_found`, or `invalid`) so a script pointed at a stale id fails loudly instead of looking healthy. A successful push also records a net-worth snapshot immediately, so the chart doesn't wait for the app to be opened. The endpoint only *updates* accounts that already exist. It can't create them, so a leaked token can't invent accounts.

This is the escape hatch for filling Plaid's gaps however you like. Some options, roughly in order of how well they hold up:

- **[SimpleFIN Bridge](https://beta-bridge.simplefin.org/)** (about $15 a year, read-only, daily refresh) is purpose-built for personal aggregation and is what Actual Budget and Firefly III use. It sometimes covers institutions Plaid misses, and Nya now connects to it directly, with no script: see [Bring your own sync](#bring-your-own-sync).
- **OFX Direct Connect**, the pre-Plaid standard, is still enabled at many credit unions (often needing a separate enrollment and PIN) and is scriptable with [`ofxtools`](https://github.com/csingley/ofxtools). Check the [GnuCash bank list](https://wiki.gnucash.org/wiki/OFX_Direct_Connect_Bank_Settings) for a given institution. The industry is migrating away from it, so treat it as a bonus where it exists.
- **Other aggregators** (Teller, MX, Akoya, Finicity) generally have *narrower* long-tail coverage than Plaid, so they rarely help with the exact institutions Plaid is missing.
- **Scraping your own account** is possible but a maintenance treadmill: MFA and device binding break it, bank logins from datacenter IPs get flagged (so it can't run on Vercel), most bank terms prohibit automated access, and the failure mode is a locked account rather than a stale number. If you do it, run it on your own machine and push the result here rather than storing bank credentials in this app.

## Bring your own sync

Plaid reaches US institutions only, and not every one of them. Bring your own sync connects a service you pay for yourself, which may reach banks Plaid doesn't (nobody here has checked bank by bank), and keeps their accounts up to date like any other. There are two: SimpleFIN, below, and the [Lunch Flow Personal API](#lunch-flow-and-what-it-costs), with your own key, after it, offered only where the deployment turns it on (`LUNCHFLOW_ENABLED`, [deployment.md](deployment.md#turning-on-lunch-flow)). Both sit on one interface ([architecture.md](architecture.md#connectors)), so what this section says of SimpleFIN's accounts once they are tracked (where they live, how they count, transfers, letting them go, what they don't do) holds for Lunch Flow's too, and the Lunch Flow part says only where it differs.

### SimpleFIN, and what it costs

[SimpleFIN Bridge](https://beta-bridge.simplefin.org/) connects to your banks, through the provider it uses to reach them, and lets apps you choose read your accounts, balances and transactions, read only. Actual Budget and Firefly III use it too. You pay SimpleFIN for it, about $15 a year when this was written. Whoever runs Nya pays nothing for it, unlike Plaid, which bills the deployment for each connection. Your bank login stays with SimpleFIN, and Nya never sees it.

Any server that speaks the [SimpleFIN protocol](https://www.simplefin.org/protocol.html) works the same way: Redbark, for banks in Australia and New Zealand, is one, a separate service with its own subscription. Nya never assumes SimpleFIN's own address: it reaches only the host in the token you paste, to claim it, and the host of the access that claim returns, to read your accounts.

It brings balances and posted transactions, read about once a day. It brings no pending transactions, no holdings for investment accounts (only their balances), no categories and no account kinds; the connect sheet says so before you paste a token.

### Connecting

1. On the Accounts tab, or on the empty state before anything is connected, choose **Connect with SimpleFIN**, beside the Plaid buttons. Nothing about SimpleFIN happens until you do.
2. In SimpleFIN Bridge, create a Setup Token for Nya, copy it, and paste it into the sheet.
3. Nya claims the token at once, and SimpleFIN answers with an Access URL: the read-only access Nya keeps, encrypted. **A token works once.** Once it is claimed, SimpleFIN refuses it to anyone, Nya included, which is what makes pasting it safe. If Nya says a token was already used or isn't valid, create a new one; if you never used it yourself, someone else may have, so check the apps listed in your SimpleFIN account. A token is spent the moment it is claimed, so if anything fails after that, the sheet says the connection is made, and its card lets you carry on.
4. Nya asks SimpleFIN once for your accounts, balances only, and lists them by bank, each balance as SimpleFIN reports it with its sign in words ("-$432.10, owed", "+$500.00, held"). Choose which to track, and what each one is: checking or savings, credit card, loan, investment or other. The protocol doesn't say, so Nya guesses from each name and the sign of its balance, and asks you to check. An account in a currency Nya doesn't know (a server can have its own, such as points) can't be tracked.

Nothing is tracked until you choose, and the balances of accounts you don't track are never stored: only their names, so **Choose accounts** on the connection's card can offer them later. Each account you choose gets its last 89 days of transactions at once (a window that runs to now stays under the 90 days SimpleFIN allows), and older ones over the following nights.

**A card or a loan reported as money held.** SimpleFIN's own rule is to report what is owed as a negative balance, and SimpleFIN Bridge follows it, so a card or a loan normally comes negative and is counted as the amount owed; with that rule, its contribution to net worth is the reported balance whatever you call it. But the protocol doesn't pin the sign of a debt, and some servers report what is owed as positive, which read the protocol's way would add the debt to your net worth. So a card or a loan that comes with a positive balance isn't counted at all until you say, once for that account, which it is: money held (a card paid past zero) or what you owe. Until you answer, its bank is held out of the day's total, its card and Connection health ask (**Answer**), and Home says the bank has a card or loan waiting for your answer. A share of it is paused meanwhile, as one of an account still waiting for its first balance is: Sharing lists it, marked as waiting for its balance, where you can keep it or set it to Not shared, and a save that leaves it out keeps it. The answer is kept, and applies to every balance after.

You can have two connections (SimpleFIN Bridge, and another server such as Redbark or a Lunch Flow account) with 100 accounts between them, since the day's requests are for every connection together, and try 10 tokens or keys an hour; something that isn't a token at all is refused before it counts. An account another connection already tracks through the same service on the same server (the same SimpleFIN id) isn't offered again, and tracking it is refused with the reason: tracked twice, it would be counted twice. Accounts are never matched by name there, so two people's own "Checking" at one bank, each in their own SimpleFIN account, are two accounts. Through another service or on another server (Lunch Flow's own SimpleFIN server beside its API, say, which may give the same account other ids) only a likely match can be told, the same bank, account name and balance, which the list says beside the account, leaving it untracked unless you choose a kind for it anyway.

### Where its accounts live

Each account you track is kept the way a manual account is: an id of the same kind (`manual_` and a random id), and its transactions in the same per-account book that typed and imported transactions use, each marked as from SimpleFIN, with SimpleFIN's own id for it. Its own record (`connector-accounts`) is what makes it SimpleFIN's. It is never written to the directory of Plaid's accounts, so nothing Plaid-shaped (reconnecting through Link, linking history across a reconnect, Plaid's warnings) reaches for it, and keeping it as a manual account when you disconnect is the same account carried on.

It counts everywhere an account does: net worth and its history, the Accounts tab (its bank's card says **via SimpleFIN** where a manual one says Manual, and each balance says the day the bank gave it), Activity ("from SimpleFIN"), the month's totals, budgets, insights, recurring bills, the Plan, allocation (as an account with no positions: SimpleFIN reports none), savings goals, hiding, sharing (its transactions are shared as a manual account's are) and the [download of your data](data-export.md). An account in another currency follows the [currency rule](#transactions-on-a-manual-account): nothing is converted.

Its balance and transactions are the bank's, so **Update**, **Add transaction** and **Import a file** aren't offered on it, and a transaction's date, amount, payee, account and note stay as the bank gave them; nor can one be deleted. Its category is yours to change, and it can be [excluded](#excluding-a-transaction).

**Transfers and categories.** SimpleFIN gives no categories, so nothing says a row is a card payment or a move between your own accounts. When two of your SimpleFIN accounts hold an exact opposite pair, the same amount out of a checking or savings account and into another account (a card payment, a loan payment, a move between cash accounts), in one currency, within four days, neither categorized by you, both are shown as a bank shows the same pair, and filed into your categories as a bank's are: a payment into a card as a card payment and into a loan as a loan payment (both under loan payments), and any other as a transfer (transfer out of one account, transfer in to the other). Each is left out of every total because its category is in a transfers group, as a bank's are ([what a category counts as](#what-a-category-counts-as)): rename the category and it still is; move the category into a spending group (loan payments, say) and it counts as spending there, a bank's and these alike. A card's or a loan's payment that repeats is a bill the [cash forecast](#recurring-bills-and-the-cash-forecast) counts as the money leaving checking, as a bank's card payment is, and a loan's is a loan payment in the Plan, whatever group holds loan payments; and each row says what it is, worked out from the amounts, and, while it is left out, how to count it. A purchase on a card is never one side, so a card charge and an unrelated deposit of the same amount stay counted. The amounts can't tell everything, though: a purchase paid from checking and an unrelated refund to a card of the same amount, within four days, look just like a card payment and are paired too, which their rows say. This is worked out each time the rows are read, never stored, so a category you give either one wins and undoes the pair: give a wrong pair a spending category and both sides count again. A [rule](#rules) never files either side of a pair: it would make one side spending while the other stayed a transfer, counting half of it; a rule's name, tags and hide still apply to them. Every other row from SimpleFIN comes without a category and counts as spending or income until you give it one, a payment to a card at another service included; the month's totals on the Activity tab and the Plan's spending say how many such rows they count.

### Keeping them up to date

SimpleFIN updates from your banks about once a day and allows an app 24 requests a day. Nya makes at most 8 a day for all your connections together, counted before each is sent.

- **Every day** the daily snapshot ([deployment.md](deployment.md#4-scheduled-jobs)) asks each connection for the balances and the transactions posted since five days before the last day fetched (never 90 days back or more), so a transaction that posts late is still caught. Only an answer that holds every tracked account, with no problem reported about its bank, moves that day on; after any other, the next one asks from where the last whole one ended. Its catch-up run two hours later skips a connection only when its last answer is under 12 hours old and was whole and current for every account it tracks; a bank held out of the day's total is always asked again. Opening the app never asks SimpleFIN anything: it shows what the last answer brought.
- **Older history** arrives 90 days at a time going back, up to two windows at each daily run, each overlapping the next by five days, until two whole answers in a row bring nothing older (one quiet window could be a quiet stretch of an account with more before it) or two years have come. An answer missing an account, or reporting a problem with its bank, ends nothing: that window is asked again the next night. The connection's card says how far it has come, and while it is coming the Activity tab says the bank is still importing older transactions under each month, as for a Plaid bank. The backfill always leaves three of the day's requests for you.
- **Refresh**, on the connection's card, asks at once, from the same day's requests; once they are spent it says when they come back. SimpleFIN itself updates about once a day, so asking more often seldom brings anything new.
- **Nothing twice.** A transaction is matched by SimpleFIN's own id for it, so a window fetched again, or one that overlaps, adds nothing, and one the bank has since changed (its date, amount or description) is brought up to date, keeping the category you gave it. Two identical purchases with two ids stay two.
- **Pending transactions are left out.** Nya never asks for them, and skips any an answer holds all the same, counting them in the connection's latest request. The protocol doesn't tie a pending transaction to the posted one that replaces it, which often has another id, so keeping both would count a purchase twice. A purchase shows once it posts.

### When SimpleFIN can't be read

A request that fails changes nothing: no balance and no transaction. A bank's accounts count as measured only when their latest word is a whole, current answer. When the latest request failed, when SimpleFIN reported a problem that concerns that bank, when one of its tracked accounts is missing from the answer, before any answer held a balance or since you connected again, when a card or a loan waits for your answer about its sign, when the bank's own balance (its `balance-date`) is more than three days old, or when no answer has come for a day and a half, its balances are shown as last known with their own date, no net-worth total is recorded that day (as when a Plaid bank can't be reached), and nothing is written to its history as measured or recovered from it. A balance that is current counts in the day's total and its breakdown like a Plaid balance, which can be up to a day behind too, and is shown with the day the bank gave it. SimpleFIN's messages name the bank they are about, so a message naming one of your banks holds that bank only; one naming no bank could be about any, so it holds every bank on the connection; one naming only banks you don't track holds nothing. An account that [ended](#accounts-that-end) holds nothing either: whatever its latest answer says of it, its bank is held only for the accounts that still count.

[Connection health](#connection-health) has a row for each bank SimpleFIN feeds, marked **via SimpleFIN**, with SimpleFIN's own words as it sent them, whose side the problem is on, and the one thing to do. Plaid's codes and buttons never apply to it.

| What happened | Whose side | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SimpleFIN reports a problem with a bank ("Connection to My Bank may need attention") | SimpleFIN's: between it and your bank | Fix it in SimpleFIN, often by signing in to the bank there again (**Open SimpleFIN** links to it), then **Refresh**. |
| SimpleFIN's latest balance from a bank is more than three days old: its link there has stopped updating (unless Nya's daily update stopped first: that is Nya's row below) | SimpleFIN's | The same: fix it in SimpleFIN, then **Refresh**. The row says the balance's date. |
| A card or a loan comes with a positive balance | Yours | **Answer** whether it is money held or what you owe, once. |
| SimpleFIN no longer accepts Nya's access: removed or reset there, or the subscription ended | Yours | **Connect again** with a new Setup Token: the same accounts carry on, with their history. |
| SimpleFIN wants the subscription paid | Yours | Renew it at SimpleFIN; Nya picks up at its next update. |
| A tracked account is missing from SimpleFIN's answer | SimpleFIN's | If you removed it there, stop tracking it with **Choose accounts**; if it closed, say so with **Close…** ([accounts that end](#accounts-that-end)). |
| SimpleFIN doesn't answer, or answers with something Nya can't read | SimpleFIN's | Nothing: Nya asks again at its next update. |
| Nya can't read what it keeps, its daily update didn't run, or SimpleFIN refused how Nya asked or said it asked too often | Nya's | Nothing for you: whoever runs Nya looks at it. |
| Nya can't use the access it keeps | Nya's | **Connect again** with a new Setup Token. |
| The server's address isn't one Nya connects to (a private or internal one) | Not known | If you didn't expect this, connect again with a new Setup Token. |

A month whose transactions may be incomplete because a SimpleFIN bank stopped answering says so under its totals, as for a Plaid one. Problems with SimpleFIN aren't [emailed](#email-notices) yet.

**Connecting again** with a new Setup Token forgets what the old access came to: until the new one answers, the accounts wait for it, so a refusal of the old access is never shown as the new token's. If the day's requests are used up, the sheet says when more are available, and Refresh on its card then brings the accounts up to date.

**Backups never hold the access.** The Access URL works on its own, so it is kept out of the nightly backups (as the counts of requests are): restoring one ends every SimpleFIN access, and each connection then asks to be connected again with a new Setup Token, its accounts, history and transactions carrying on.

### Disconnecting

**Disconnect**, on the connection's card, deletes the Access URL and the connection, so nothing is fetched again, and asks what becomes of its accounts, as removing a Plaid connection does. You type its name to confirm.

- **Keep them as manual accounts** (the default): each becomes a manual account under the same id, at the balance SimpleFIN last reported and in its currency, with its transactions, history, goals, shares and whether it is hidden. From then on you update it by hand. A card or a loan still waiting for your answer about its sign can't be kept until it is known whether its balance is owed or held, so the sheet asks it in the same step, and nothing is disconnected until you answer. An account no answer ever held a balance for has nothing to keep, and goes: the sheet names it.
- **Remove them**: the accounts go, with their transactions and what you said about them. Their past balances stay in your net-worth history, which is never rewritten, as a disconnected bank's do.

**Choose accounts** lets one account go the same way, kept or removed (with the same question for a card or a loan waiting for its answer), while the rest stay connected. Nya can't tell SimpleFIN: Nya stays listed as an app in your SimpleFIN account until you remove it there, and the sheet says so.

Either way, Nya keeps each account's name, bank and the day you let it go, so a report on a period it was tracked in can say what may be missing ([SimpleFIN in a report](#reports)). Both sheets say so, with a link to **Accounts you stopped tracking** on the Reports page, where **Forget** deletes one, and so does Manage accounts, under Reconnected accounts, for as long as any are kept.

### What it doesn't do

- **Holdings.** SimpleFIN reports balances, not positions, so an investment account has a balance and no holdings, and allocation counts its money as unclassified until you give the account a split, as for a manual investment account. With no subtype from SimpleFIN, its tax bucket is unclassified too until you set one.
- **Account details.** No account number digits, credit limits, rates, minimum payments or due dates, and no account kinds: you say what each one is.
- **Categories.** SimpleFIN gives none: a payment out of a SimpleFIN checking or savings account into another of your SimpleFIN accounts is left out as a transfer, and every other row counts as spending or income until you set one, said under the totals.
- **Days in other time zones.** A transaction's day is the UTC day of SimpleFIN's posted time. SimpleFIN Bridge stamps noon UTC, which gives the right day for banks from UTC-11 to UTC+11; a server that stamps local midnight east of UTC (a day in Australia starts the afternoon before, in UTC) would have each transaction dated a day early. Unverified for Redbark: Nya doesn't guess a server's time zone from its stamps, since the same hours are real posting times in the Americas.
- **Pending transactions**, as above.
- **Estimated history.** Before its first snapshot, an account is held flat at its balance in the estimated history, as a manual account is; its transactions aren't walked back yet.
- **Earlier history from Plaid.** A bank you used to connect through Plaid isn't joined to its SimpleFIN accounts: account links are for Plaid's reconnects.
- **History from your bank's files.** [Importing history into a linked account](#importing-history-into-a-linked-account) is for accounts linked through Plaid: an account SimpleFIN (or Lunch Flow) keeps up to date brings its older transactions itself (up to two years), matched by the service's own ids, which a file's rows can't be matched against, so it isn't offered, and the import refuses one, saying so.
- **Email notices**, as above.
- **Other services.** Lunch Flow is the other one (below); more can be added behind the same interface.

### Lunch Flow, and what it costs

[Lunch Flow](https://www.lunchflow.app/) connects to banks in many countries, through the providers it uses to reach them (GoCardless, Akahu, MX, Finicity, SnapTrade, Pluggy and others), and gives apps you choose read-only access to your accounts, balances and transactions through its Personal API, with an API key you make in your own Lunch Flow account. Sure, Firefly III and ZeroSum read it the same way. You pay Lunch Flow for it: $34.99 a year ($2.92 a month) for two bank connections, and $10 for each more, with a 7-day trial, as its site listed it when this was written; Lunch Flow sets its own prices. Whoever runs Nya pays nothing for it. Your bank login stays with Lunch Flow and its providers, and Nya never sees it.

It brings balances and posted transactions, which Lunch Flow refreshes from your banks about once a day. It brings no pending transactions, no categories and no account kinds, and no holdings (its API serves positions for some brokerages' accounts, which Nya doesn't read yet: an investment account has its balance, as with SimpleFIN). With no categories, a payment between two of your accounts it or SimpleFIN keeps up to date is found from the amounts, as between two of SimpleFIN's ([transfers and categories](#where-its-accounts-live)). And it gives no date with a balance, so each balance is shown as of the day Nya received it ("Balance as Lunch Flow gave it on Oct 17"), never as the bank's own day. The connect sheet says all of this before you paste a key.

Lunch Flow's own documentation says its Personal API is for your own connected accounts and points apps that serve other people's data to its Platform API, a partnership. Here each person connects their own Lunch Flow account, with a key they make and can delete, to see their own data, as Sure, Firefly III and ZeroSum's Plus plan do; nothing in Lunch Flow's terms that could be found forbids it. All the same, **Connect with Lunch Flow** is off until whoever runs Nya turns it on, which they do only once Lunch Flow has said yes in writing ([deployment.md](deployment.md#turning-on-lunch-flow)). Turned off again later, it stops offering new connections, and those already made keep working.

Lunch Flow can also act as a SimpleFIN server (its SimpleFIN Bridge destination, made for Actual Budget and "other compatible apps"), so a Setup Token from it may work through **Connect with SimpleFIN** too. That route hasn't been tried with Nya.

### Connecting with Lunch Flow

1. Choose **Connect with Lunch Flow**, beside **Connect with SimpleFIN**.
2. In Lunch Flow, open Destinations, add an API destination, and copy its API key into the sheet. The field hides what you type.
3. Nya checks the key with Lunch Flow once, by listing your accounts, and keeps it encrypted, as SimpleFIN's access is kept: never in a log, an error, your download or a backup, and never sent back to your browser. A key isn't spent by being checked, unlike a Setup Token: if Lunch Flow doesn't answer, try again. If it says the key isn't valid, copy it again, or make a new destination; a key that is deleted in Lunch Flow, or a subscription that lapsed, reads the same way.
4. Nya lists your accounts with each one's balance and currency (Lunch Flow's list of accounts often has no currency, so Nya asks each account's balance for it), and you choose which to track and what each one is, as for SimpleFIN. Each account you track gets the last two years of its transactions at once (whatever Lunch Flow has of them), which is as far back as Nya keeps history: nothing older is asked for. Its card then says which day that history starts from, not the day of the oldest transaction, since Lunch Flow may hold fewer days than were asked. An account whose balance doesn't come while Nya lists them isn't offered yet: the connection's card says why, and Nya lists it again at its next update.

The key is sent only to Lunch Flow's own address (`lunchflow.app`, and the same site at `www.lunchflow.app`, to which a redirect between them is followed), never to any other host, and never to a private or internal address ([architecture.md](architecture.md#connectors)).

**Backups never hold the key**, as they never hold SimpleFIN's access: restoring one ends every Lunch Flow connection, and each asks to be connected again, its accounts, history and transactions carrying on. The key in your API destination still works, so you can paste it again, or make a new one.

### How Lunch Flow is kept up to date

As for SimpleFIN ([Keeping them up to date](#keeping-them-up-to-date)), within the same 8 requests a day for all your connections together, where a request is one fetch: Lunch Flow has no one answer that holds every account, so a fetch asks for the list of accounts, then the balance and transactions of each account you track, then the balance of any account you don't track that Lunch Flow has added since (to offer it with its currency), at most four at a time. An account you don't track that is offered already is only listed, never asked about again, so it costs nothing at each update and stays offered under **Choose accounts**. Lunch Flow documents no rate limit, so Nya keeps to its own.

A transaction is matched by Lunch Flow's own id for it; one whose date, amount or description changed is brought up to date with your category kept; one Lunch Flow stops returning stays, as SimpleFIN's do. Lunch Flow dates a transaction by its own day, and a card's held purchase (a hotel, a car hire) only comes once it posts, which can be weeks later, still with the day of the purchase. So each update asks from 45 days before the last day fetched (a longer window costs no extra request, only a larger answer), and a transaction Lunch Flow sends dated before that is kept all the same, back to two years: it is matched by its id, so it is never counted twice. Pending transactions are never asked for, and any that come all the same (marked pending, or with no id) are left out and counted: a purchase shows once it posts, with the id Lunch Flow gives it then.

### When Lunch Flow can't be read

As for SimpleFIN ([When SimpleFIN can't be read](#when-simplefin-cant-be-read)): an answer that isn't whole and current holds its bank out of the day's total, and its balances are shown as last known, never recorded as measured. Each bank Lunch Flow feeds has its row in [Connection health](#connection-health), marked **via Lunch Flow**, in Lunch Flow's words, and problems with it aren't emailed yet either. The differences:

| What happened | Whose side | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lunch Flow lists an account's connection as disconnected or in error ("The connection to Monzo is disconnected") | Lunch Flow's: between it and your bank | Fix it in Lunch Flow, often by signing in to the bank there again (**Open Lunch Flow**), then **Refresh**. This is how a link at Lunch Flow that broke shows, since its balances carry no date to grow old; one that stops updating without a word from Lunch Flow can't be told ([below](#what-isnt-known-about-lunch-flow)). |
| One account's balance or transactions didn't come: no answer about it, Lunch Flow's own error (such as "Bank data is temporarily unavailable", when a provider behind it is busy), an answer Nya can't read, or no balance there yet | Lunch Flow's | Nothing: the card and the bank's row say so ("Credit Card: no balance came. … Nya asks again at its next update"), and Nya does. Only that account's bank is held; if nothing came for any account you track, the request failed. |
| Nya's own time for the update ran out before it asked about an account (many accounts on a slow day) | Nya's | Nothing for you: Nya asks again at its next update. |
| Lunch Flow says it no longer has an account ("Account not found") | Lunch Flow's | As for an account SimpleFIN stops returning: if you removed it at Lunch Flow, stop tracking it with **Choose accounts**; if it closed, say so with **Close…**. |
| Lunch Flow no longer takes the key: deleted or replaced there, or the subscription isn't active | Yours | **Connect again** with an API key from Lunch Flow (once a lapsed subscription is renewed, the same key works again): the same accounts carry on, with their history. |

**A card reported as money held.** As for SimpleFIN, a card or a loan whose balance comes positive isn't counted until you say which way it goes: Lunch Flow's convention, as other apps read it, is that what you owe is negative. Some banks give Lunch Flow a card's credit left to spend in place of its balance (Sure has seen it from Halifax, through Lloyds); then neither answer is right, and the sheet says to stop tracking that card instead.

**Disconnecting** deletes the key and asks what becomes of the accounts, as for SimpleFIN. Nya can't tell Lunch Flow: the key works there until you delete its API destination in Lunch Flow, and the sheet says so.

### What isn't known about Lunch Flow

Lunch Flow's documentation pages couldn't be read in full when this was built, so some of what its API does was read from its own clients and from other apps' (`lib/connectors/lunchflow.ts` names each source). Where they disagree or say nothing, Nya takes the reading that can't put a wrong figure in your totals:

- **The window's parameters.** Nya asks for transactions `from` and `to` a day, as two clients do (another sends `start_date` and `end_date`), and keeps every transaction an answer holds from the last two years, matched by its id, so a parameter Lunch Flow ignores costs a larger answer, never a wrong one.
- **How late a transaction can come.** Forty-five days of overlap, and keeping what comes from before the window, cover a held purchase that posts late. How Lunch Flow filters a window (by the day it dates a transaction, or the day it got it) isn't documented.
- **Pages, and `total`.** None is documented, and no client asks for a second page or reads an answer's `total`; one sample answer has a `total` of 2927 beside 11 transactions, so it may count every transaction the account has. Nya doesn't read it: an answer is taken as it comes. Whether one is ever cut short needs a check with a live key ([deployment.md](deployment.md#turning-on-lunch-flow)).
- **Signs.** A transaction's amount is taken as money out negative, as Lunch Flow's own client hands it to Actual Budget, and a balance with what is owed negative, as above.
- **Dates.** A transaction's day is the calendar day Lunch Flow gives, as the bank gives it; a time after it is dropped, never moved into a time zone.
- **A balance's time.** None is given: shown as the day Nya received it. So a link at Lunch Flow that stops updating while Lunch Flow still lists the account as active can't be told from one that is up to date: its last balance would be recorded each day as it came. A link Lunch Flow says is disconnected or in error holds its bank, as above. Lunch Flow plans a last sync time for each account; until then, nothing plays the part of SimpleFIN's three days.

## Importing files

A way in without bank logins: the file your bank lets you download. Import it into a [manual account](#manual-accounts) with **Import** on the account's row (Accounts tab) or **Import a file** on the Activity tab. A linked account's older history, from before what its bank gives Plaid, is imported from the account's own screen, through a review of what the bank already gave ([Importing history into a linked account](#importing-history-into-a-linked-account)).

### Formats

- **OFX and QFX** are best, and most US banks offer one beside CSV. Each transaction carries the bank's own id (FITID), so importing a period again, or one that overlaps, adds nothing twice, and every field has a defined meaning, so there is nothing to map. OFX 1.x (SGML) and 2.x (XML) are read, bank and credit card statements alike, including files that bend the standard (a whole file on one line, a bank's own extra tags, and end tags left out, even each transaction's own `</STMTTRN>`). Whatever had to be repaired is said in the preview, and a transaction the file holds but that still can't be read is listed as a line that can't be read, never lost without a word. A file holding several statements (checking and savings in one download) asks which to import. The preview names the statement's account by its last four digits, and a file for another account than the one this account's last OFX file was for is caught: nothing is imported until you say it's the right file. A statement without its currency (CURDEF) is read in the one you choose, as a QIF file is. Investment statements aren't read.
- **CSV** needs a row of column names. You say which column is the date, the description and the amount (or separate money-out and money-in columns), and optionally a category, notes and a currency; with one amount column, whether money out is written negative or positive. The first rows show how they read as you choose. Dates are read in ISO (2026-09-30), US (09/30/2026) and UK or European (30/09/2026, 30.09.2026) form, or with the month named in English (30 Sep 2026), and the sheet says which form it found; when every date in the file fits both the US and the UK order (a short file early in a month), it asks which, and never guesses. Fields can be separated by commas, semicolons, tabs or vertical bars, and quoted with commas, quotes and line breaks inside; a byte order mark, decimal commas with grouping (1.234,56) and a bank's summary lines above the table are fine. The account remembers the mapping by column name, so the next file from the same bank imports in one tap.
- **QIF**, Quicken's older format: `!Type:Bank` and `!Type:CCard` (and cash and other accounts, written the same way), with Quicken's dates ("1/2'26", "1/ 2'26", two-digit years) and the same question when every date fits both orders. A transfer to another of your accounts ("L[Savings]") is a transfer in or out, so it stays out of spending; Quicken's opening balance isn't a transaction and isn't imported. A file of several accounts asks which. QIF says nothing of its currency: the preview says which one it is read in (the account's own until you choose another, then the one this account's last QIF file was read in; when you correct the account's currency, one that was its old currency follows it) and **Change** reads it in another.

A file is read as UTF-8, UTF-16 or Windows-1252, the old Western encoding OFX 1.x and many European banks still use, so accented names come through.

**The order of a date's numbers** comes from the file whenever its dates settle it: one date that can only be day first (13/01/2026) puts every date of the file day first, whatever an earlier file from the same account was. Only when every date fits both orders is the order asked, or, for an account that has imported such a file before, its answer used, shown beside the rows and changeable (in the column step for a CSV file, in the preview for a QIF file). Dates that contradict each other (one only fits month first, another only day first) are asked about, unless the account has an answer from before: then that order is used, shown and changeable, and the dates that don't fit it are listed as lines that can't be read.

### Getting an OFX file from your bank

On your bank's website, open the account and look for **Download**, **Export** or **Download transactions** (often beside the statements or the transaction search). Choose the dates, then **Quicken (QFX)** or **Web Connect**, **Microsoft Money** or **OFX**: they are the same format. Without one, choose **CSV**. Some banks offer downloads only on their full website, not in their app.

### The preview

Before anything is stored, the sheet shows how many transactions are new, how many the account already has, how many the file lists twice, and the lines that can't be read, each with its line number and why (no date, an amount that isn't one, a date in the future, an amount more precise than its currency allows); then the first rows as they will be read, the dates covered, the currency, the money in and out of the new rows, and what the file marks as transfers, ATM withdrawals, card payments and bank fees, with how each counts. Rows whose bank id is already on another transaction here are listed for you to decide ([below](#matching-what-is-already-there)). **Import** adds the new rows in one step: a file is never half imported, and rows that arrived meanwhile from another device are matched against too.

Imported transactions are ordinary manual transactions. They show on the Activity tab ("imported from OFX"), count in totals, budgets, insights, recurring bills and the Plan as a linked bank's do, follow the currency rule, and can be recategorized, edited, moved or excluded. Shared at **Balance and transactions**, they are shared as a typed one is ([sharing](authentication.md#sharing-between-people)). Their category is the file's own (a CSV category column, a QIF category), lower-cased. An OFX file has no categories, but its bank's own transaction type is a fact where its meaning doesn't depend on the bank, and is used: XFER is a transfer in or out and ATM cash taken out (or paid in) at a machine, which count as neither spending nor income on the Activity tab (the Plan counts the cash as spent, as it does a linked bank's withdrawals); FEE and SRVCHG are bank fees, which are spending; and PAYMENT on a credit card statement is a payment toward the card, a loan payment, never income. Any other type (DEBIT, CREDIT, a PAYMENT from a bank account, which may pay any bill) gets no category, so it counts as spending or income until you give it one (transfer in or out, loan payments). A description longer than a typed one may be (100 characters) is shortened, and the full text is kept with the import.

### Matching what is already there

- An OFX transaction is already there when the account has one with the same FITID, from an earlier OFX or QFX import, that is the same transaction: exactly what was imported for it, however you edited it since, or what it says now; or, once every row has found its exact match, the same amount dated within five days of it (a pending charge posts a few days after it was made). That last, looser match comes last so that a bank that renumbers its downloads can't have a FITID now on another purchase of the same amount take a transaction from the row that really is it. A FITID the file lists twice on the same transaction (the same payee and amount) is the bank listing it twice: it is imported once.
- A FITID is never trusted on its own, since banks reuse them: some number each download's transactions from 1, so October's "1" is another purchase than September's, and some keep a pending charge's FITID when it posts at another amount. A row whose FITID the account has on a transaction that isn't this one is listed in the preview with both versions and what differs, for you to choose: **Import as new**, **Replace** the one already here with the file's version (its date, amount and payee; its category and note stay, and the file's fill in only one it lacks), or **Skip**. When the payee, amount and date all differ, it is another transaction under a reused id, and **Import as new** is chosen for you; when only some differ (the same payee at another amount), nothing is chosen, and nothing is imported until you say. **All as new**, **Replace all** and **Skip all** answer every one at once. A row reusing a FITID that is already in the same file for another transaction is imported as its own, and the preview says which lines share it.
- Otherwise, and for CSV and QIF, a transaction is already there when the account has one with the same date, amount, currency and description (lower-cased, letters and digits only), transactions you typed in included, and an OFX transaction whose FITID changed (a bank that renumbers) is found the same way. Two identical transactions on one day (two coffees) are two: each stored one matches one in the file, and the rest are new. One imported from a CSV or QIF file is recognized either by what the file said when it was imported, so editing it doesn't make the next file bring it back, or by what it says now, so correcting it to what the bank later says (a pending amount that posted at another) makes that file's row match it; still once.
- Matching is exact. A bank's CSV and its OFX describe the same transaction differently, so they can't be matched against each other: import one format into an account.

### Undo

The account's past imports are listed under the file picker, each with its file, when it was imported, how many transactions it added and the dates they cover. **Undo** removes every transaction the import added, wherever it is now (one moved to another manual account too) and whether you edited it since or not, and puts back any it replaced with its file's version as they were before it. A transaction it added that a later import found already there and relied on (or whose own version of it you skipped there) stays, for that later import: undo January to March after importing February to April, and February and March stay, since the second file holds them and didn't add them again; undoing that one takes them out. The confirmation says how many go, how many were changed or moved, how many stay and for which file, and how many go back. Exclusions on the transactions it removes are forgotten with them. The balance stays as it is. Deleting a manual account deletes its imports with it.

### The balance

An import never changes the account's balance by itself. An OFX statement carries its ledger balance and the day it is as of; when that day is today or yesterday, the preview offers **Also set the balance** to it, unticked. Ticked, it is recorded as the account's **Update** form records a balance, and only from the balance the preview showed: if that changed since, nothing is imported. An older statement's balance is shown, never set, since setting it would record a past figure as today's. It is offered only in US dollars, which manual balances are kept in, and only onto an account of the statement's kind (a card statement onto a credit card), since its sign means opposite things on a card and a bank account.

### Limits

- A file of up to 3 MB and 10,000 transactions (four years of a busy account is about 7,000). A larger one is refused whole: export a shorter period and import it in parts.
- At most 200 columns in a CSV file, and 50 statements in an OFX file or accounts in a QIF file: a bank's export has far fewer, and a file with more is refused before anything is built from it.
- An account holds some 80,000 transactions; an import that would take it past the size a stored value may have is refused whole, never trimmed.
- 100 previews, imports and undos an hour, per person, and 300 reads of the list of past imports.
- Every field is checked as a typed transaction's is: dates from 1900 to tomorrow, amounts in whole minor units of their currency (a cent, a yen), and lengths.

### What is kept

Each import keeps the file's records as the file had them (an OFX transaction's fields, a CSV line's cells, a QIF record's lines), what became of each (and, for a transaction it replaced, how that one was before) and why a line couldn't be read, with the file's name, the format, the account and when: if a transaction looks wrong years later, what the bank's file said settles it. The records, not the file: they are what can be set beside a row, and a file's other statements and accounts aren't this account's. They are encrypted, in your [download](data-export.md#imports), never shared, and go with the import's undo or the account. The list of past imports reads a small summary kept beside each, never the records, which are read only to undo an import and for the download. A file is never written to a log.

## Importing history into a linked account

A bank gives Plaid only so much history (often two years, for some accounts 90 days), but lets you download years of it as files. Tap a linked account on the Accounts tab and choose **Import history from a file** under its chart. It is for accounts linked through Plaid: a manual account imports a file with its own **Import**, and an account SimpleFIN keeps up to date brings its history itself ([What it doesn't do](#what-it-doesnt-do)). The files are the ones a [manual account](#importing-files) imports (OFX or QFX, CSV with its column mapping, QIF), read the same way, up to the same 3 MB and 10,000 transactions a file, and counted against the same hundred imports an hour. Unlike a manual account's, such a file overlaps what the bank already gave Nya, so nothing is kept until you have seen what would happen, with every likely duplicate beside the row it looks like, and said what each one is. The sheet says on which day the bank's own transactions begin, so you can export a period that reaches up to it, and, for a connection made in the last day or so, that the bank is still sending its history (Plaid sends about a month first, then the rest), so the overlap may grow: what it sends later of what you import takes the imported rows' place ([below](#when-the-bank-sends-the-same-transactions)).

### Reviewing the file

Before anything is stored, the sheet shows:

- How many transactions are new, and how many of them are older than the bank's own (from the day its oldest transaction for the connection is dated): history it never gave. New ones that fall inside the bank's period, though it has nothing like them, are counted apart, since that is where a file read the wrong way round, or a wrong file, shows.
- **Likely already there**: each row of the file with exactly the same amount, in the same currency, as a transaction the account already counts, dated at most five days from it (a bank's file often dates a purchase the day it was made, Plaid the day it posted). What the account counts is every posted transaction the bank gave for it, and every row an earlier file brought in that counts: a bank's CSV and its OFX file describe one transaction differently, so importing both of one period finds each row of the second beside the first's, named by that file. Each is shown beside the row it looks like (the bank's name for it, and the bank's own description where Plaid renamed the merchant, or the earlier file's), with how many days apart they are, whether their names are alike, and how sure that makes it: **very likely the same** (the same day or a day apart, or up to three days with alike names), **likely the same** (up to three days, or alike names), or **possibly the same** (four or five days apart, with nothing alike in the names). Each is answered **The same** (that row is the transaction; the file's row is kept beside it and never counted while that row is there) or **Its own** (another transaction, counted). The first two are answered **The same** for you, and any answer can be changed; a "possibly" has no answer until you give one, and **Import** waits for it. **All the same** and **All their own** answer every row at once. Pairs are one to one, so two identical coffees in a week are two, and as many as can be, then the closest, so no row is left without the one it looks like because a closer pair took it. Pairs inside both sides' days come first, and a pair across an edge (below) is only offered for the rows left over, so a file exported up to the bank's first day has that day's fare proposed as the bank's own, never pushed out by lagged pairs across the edge. What that costs: when the file dates a daily fare by purchase and the bank by posting two days later, which their dates can't show, the fares inside both sides' days pair with the bank's of the same day, and the file's fares of the two days before the bank's first count as well as the bank's: two too many. The names only break a tie and move the confidence, since a bank's file says "SQ *BLUE BOTTLE 1234 OAKLAND CA" where Plaid says "Blue Bottle Coffee": they are least reliable exactly where they would be needed. Nothing is merged without your answer.
- A pair across the edge of either side's days (the file's row dated before the bank's first transaction, or the bank's dated after the last day the file covers) is the same only if one of them was dated days later than the other: always **possibly the same**, with no answer until you give one, and the sheet says why ("Chase has nothing before Mar 3: the same only if it posted days later"). An OFX file dates each transaction the day it posted, as the bank does, so for one such a pair isn't made at all.
- A transaction still pending at the bank is never a match, since its amount can still change. The sheet says how many rows look like one: once it posts, the bank's takes their place ([below](#when-the-bank-sends-the-same-transactions)).
- Rows already imported into this account, found exactly as a manual account's import finds them ([Matching what is already there](#matching-what-is-already-there)): an OFX transaction by its FITID, so importing a period again adds nothing. A row whose FITID an earlier import kept on another transaction is listed for you to **Import as new** or **Skip**.
- The period the file covers: an OFX statement's own, otherwise its first to last day. **Change** gives a longer one (a statement whose first or last days had no transactions), which must hold every row. The chart's estimated history is drawn only across days a file or the bank covers ([below](#the-chart-reaches-further-back)).
- As a manual account's preview does: the first rows as they will be read, the lines that can't be read, the currency, and what the file marks as transfers, ATM withdrawals, card payments and bank fees. An OFX file for another account is caught, and nothing is imported until you say it's the right file: one for another account than this account's last OFX file was for, or, for the first, one whose account number doesn't end as the bank gives it to Plaid.
- When some lines can't be read, the chart is drawn through the file's period only if you tick that they aren't transactions (a total, a title): otherwise the period may lack theirs, so it isn't used, and the chart says so ([below](#the-chart-reaches-further-back)).

**Import** stores it in one step, all or nothing, and only as reviewed: if the bank has sent a transaction since that changes what the review showed, or another import into this account went in, nothing is stored and the sheet asks you to look at it again. An import that would take the account's imported transactions past the size a stored value may have is refused whole, never trimmed, and says so.

### Where the imported rows count

The rows are the account's, beside the bank's: on the Activity tab, marked "imported from OFX" (or CSV, or QIF), with each month's totals saying how many of its transactions were imported from files rather than sent by the bank (the same of rows [imported from another app](#switching-from-another-app) into the account), and the [cash flow](#cash-flow) the same of its period; in budgets and what they [roll over](#budget-rollover-and-income-carryover), the spending totals and the Home insights; in what goes into an account for a savings goal's [pace](#when-it-will-be-reached); in recurring-bill detection, so a yearly renewal the bank has once and a file has a year earlier is found; in the Plan's spending; in the API and MCP server (with `source` `import:ofx`, `import:csv` or `import:qif`); in what you share at **Balance and transactions**; and in your [download](data-export.md#imported-transactions). They follow the rules every row does: the currency rule, what counts as spending, and a merchant you rename and an [exclusion](#excluding-a-transaction) work on them as on the bank's, and so do a [split](#splitting-a-transaction) and a [group](#grouping-transactions): their parts and a group's net count in every total, and a month's note counts a split one once. They are filed into [your categories](#categories-and-groups) by the one rule: a category you choose for one, else the file's own (a CSV's category column, a QIF category), which gets a category of its own the first time the Activity tab shows the row, else the uncategorized one; so they count in a category's budget and its group's, in the Plan by their category's kind, in the API by its id and group, and in a category's usage: one an imported row uses, of any date, can't be deleted, and a merge takes them along. They can't be edited or moved: they say what the bank's file said. A row you answered **The same** isn't counted or shown while the row it named is there: that one is. Once it isn't (the bank no longer has it, or the earlier file is undone), the row counts again, so no change can lose a transaction you imported. Like everything on the Activity tab, only the last twelve months are listed; the older ones are in recurring-bill detection, the chart and the download.

### When the bank sends the same transactions

The bank's own rows win. When a later sync brings a transaction an import holds (a row the bank hadn't sent yet, a pending one that posts, or more history after a reconnect, or as the bank's first pull of a new connection's history lands), the imported row retires: it stops counting, and the bank's counts in its place, in the very load that brings it. The rule is the one the review proposes by (exactly the same amount and currency, posted, at most five days apart, as many as can be, then the closest, one to one), against the bank's rows none of your answers holds: a row you said is the bank's holds that bank row, and a row you said is its own is never matched again with the one it was shown beside, which stays free for its real twin. A pair across the edge of either side's days is never made this way: only a lag would make it one, which is yours to say in the review, so an answer that a row is its own stands. Earlier imports are matched first. Which rows retire is worked out each time they are read, so if the bank removes its row, the imported one counts again, exactly as it was. Once a bank row stands for an imported one, it keeps standing for it while the bank has that row, whatever the bank changes on it later: a tip added, a currency settled, a merchant's name corrected (Plaid keeps a transaction's id when it changes it). The sync that brings the change keeps the pair before it saves it, so the month never counts the imported row beside the bank's at its new amount; a sync that only brings new transactions has nothing to keep and reads none of your imported ones. The pair carries across a reconnect as well (Across a reconnect, below). The bank's row counts, at the bank's amount, and the Activity tab says under it that it is the transaction you imported at the file's amount and that the bank has changed it since. A pair the bank had already changed before Nya began keeping pairs can't be told any more: both rows count, as they did, until you [exclude](#excluding-a-transaction) the imported one, undo its import, or the bank removes its row. What you said about the imported row carries to the bank's row that takes its place: a category you set on it, that you excluded it or put it back, its tags and your note, unless you said otherwise on that row yourself (a category or a note of its own, an exclusion set or cleared, a tag taken off or put on), field by field. So do how you [split](#splitting-a-transaction) it and its place in a [group](#grouping-transactions): the bank's row is split into its parts, or counts in its group in its place, and a change made from it (a part's category, the split, taking it out of the group) changes them, unless the bank's row is split or in a group of its own, which comes first; a pending one takes neither until it posts, and the group says it is pending at the bank. A split or a group of the bank's row's own is refused while it shows the imported row's, as it would hide it. Either way the transaction counts once, as its parts or in its group, never as its parts and the bank's row too, and if the bank removes its row, the imported one is split and grouped again as it was. When the bank has changed the amount since, the split shows what it changed as a part of its own, filed as the transaction would be, as for any split whose amount the bank changed, and an edit of the split adds up to the bank's amount now; a group counts the bank's amount in its net. A split you edited that way stays at the bank's amount if the bank then removes its row: the imported row counts at the file's amount again, with the difference as a part of its own, and says that you split it at the amount of the bank's transaction that stood for it, until you edit the split. While a pending bank row looks like an imported one, the pending row is shown in its place, so the month never counts it twice. When it posts, the bank sends it again under a new id, dated the day it posted (often after the last day of your file, which the rule alone never pairs across) and sometimes at another amount (a tip): when the bank names the pending transaction it replaces (most do), the sync that brings it keeps the pair, so the posted row takes the imported one's place all the same, counting once at the bank's amount, said under it as any amount the bank changed is, with what you said about the imported row. When a bank doesn't name it, a posted row dated after your file's last day, or at another amount, counts beside the imported one, and so does one that posted before Nya kept these pairs: both count until you exclude the imported one or undo its import. (SimpleFIN sends no pending transactions, and its accounts take no file of history, so none of this concerns them.) The list of past imports says, for each, how many of its rows count, how many are held as transactions already there, how many the bank has sent since, and how many it held as already there count again.

Across a reconnect, the rows follow the account. They are kept under the account's id, and linking the re-added account to the old one ([Removing an institution and adding it back](#removing-an-institution-and-adding-it-back)) makes them the new one's, where the new connection's rows (which may reach further back) retire those they match. A row you answered **The same** names the bank's row by its day, amount and the bank's own description, which survive a reconnect where its id doesn't; where the new connection doesn't have that row (its window may start later), the imported row counts again. A bank row that stood for an imported one is kept the same way when you disconnect, by its day, amount and description as the bank had them then, a tip the bank added since the import included, and once the two accounts are linked, the new connection's sync pairs the imported row with its row that has exactly those, as the next load after the link does; one that hasn't come yet is paired when it comes. One the bank changed again before you added the institution back can't be told any more, and both count, until you exclude the imported one or undo its import. Until the two are linked, the old account's imported rows show nowhere. Disconnecting keeps them, with their splits and their places in groups; forgetting the earlier account deletes them, with their imports, what you said about them, their splits and their places in groups, but for rows a later import into another account found already there (made while the two were linked): those stay, as that import's.

### The chart reaches further back

The net-worth chart's estimated history ([Net-worth history](architecture.md#net-worth-history)) stops where the bank's transactions stop. Imported transactions take it further: a checking, savings or credit card account is walked back through them from its balance on the chart's earliest day, as the bank's transactions walk it, to the oldest imported transaction, and so is its own chart on the Accounts tab. Only bank accounts and cards: an investment account's imported rows don't say how its market moved, nor a loan's how its interest built up, so theirs show as activity only. Every account not walked is held at its balance on that earliest day.

The estimate's own year counts them too: an account whose bank gives Plaid fewer days than another's (a card with 90 days beside a bank with a year) has its imported transactions between the two walked with the bank's, so the balance the drawing starts from holds them. Only across covered days, here too: a file cut off from the account's own days by days nothing covers isn't walked, before the chart's earliest day or after it, and the note says what isn't drawn. An import, an undo, a link, an unlink or a forget that changes which of those count works the estimate out again first.

It walks only across days something covers, from the chart's earliest day back: the bank's own period, and the period of each file imported. Import 2019 and 2023 without 2020 to 2022, and the chart never draws a line across the missing years: it stops where they begin, and the note under the chart says what isn't drawn ("Not drawn: Chase Checking's import from Jan 1, 2019 to Dec 31, 2019, since nothing covers Jan 1, 2020 to Dec 31, 2022. Import that period to join it up."). An account is walked only if what covers its earliest day reaches on to today (when that day's point is an estimate; a recorded one was measured, so what covers that day is enough), and not if the estimate held it at one balance on that day though it has transactions since: then what it held isn't known. A file with lines that couldn't be read (unless you said they aren't transactions), or with transactions in another currency than the account's, isn't used, and the note says so. The note also says, for each account, how far back the estimate follows the bank's own transactions and how far the imported ones ("Chase Checking: the estimate follows its bank's own transactions back to Oct 10, 2024, and those you imported before that, back to Jan 1, 2019."); a hidden account is never named. At most thirty years are drawn.

It is drawn once the chart's first estimate has been made (an import before then waits for it), and again after anything that changes it: another import, an undo, linking or unlinking accounts, forgetting one, or a new estimate after linking an institution. Only estimated points are written, and only for days before the chart's earliest one: a recorded balance is never changed, and undoing an import puts the estimated history back exactly as it was. If what it needs can't be read (a file's rows, a connection's accounts), nothing is drawn again: what was drawn is kept, and the note says why, until it can be.

### An account that ended

An account that [ended](#accounts-that-end) counts its imported rows as it counts the bank's: those dated before its end count wherever the bank's do, and those from its end on count in nothing (the Activity tab, budgets and spending, the Plan, what you share, the API, and a report, which counts them among the transactions in Nya it left out of the account), whichever of its ids it had when you imported them or set its end. They are kept, and count again if you reopen it or move its end later. Your download keeps them, as it keeps the bank's, and the account's OFX statement counts them with the bank's among those dated from its end. A bank that stops returning an account once it closed doesn't take its imported rows with it: those before its end count for as long as its connection is there.

It still takes a file: **Import history from a file** is in the **Accounts that ended** card, for an account of a connection that is still there. The review says how many of the rows the file adds are dated from its end, which count in nothing ("Checking closed on Mar 12, 2026: 3 of the transactions this file adds are dated from then, and count in nothing, as its bank's own from then don't. They are kept, and count again if you reopen it."). Undoing an import into it is as for any account. Forgetting an earlier account that ended deletes its imported rows with the rest, and when forgetting it is refused ([What is kept, and forgetting it](#what-is-kept-and-forgetting-it)), they stay with everything else.

What is drawn of it counts in the chart as its measured balances do: before its end only. The note under the chart says nothing of the days from its end on, which the chart marks: one that ended before its bank's own transactions begin is said to follow the transactions you imported, and neither one that ended before the oldest day drawn of it nor a file of it that starts from its end is named. For net worth, the API's `from_imports` stops at its end, while the account's own history keeps every day. A [report](#reports) needs it covered by files only up to its end, and, ended before the period, not at all.

The questions Nya asks about an account ([When Nya asks](#when-nya-asks)) read only what its bank sends: a row you imported from a file is never a transaction dated after its end, nor one that breaks a run of zeros.

### Undoing an import

The account's imports are listed under the file picker, each with its file, when it was imported, how many transactions it added, the dates it covers, how many of them the bank has sent since, and how many it held as already there count again. **Undo** takes out everything the import added (the rows kept as the bank's own too) and the categories and exclusions you set on them, their splits and their places in groups (a group keeps its other transactions, and says one went), and draws the chart again: the account is as it was before. A bank's row that took one's place loses what it took from it, as it loses its category. A row it added that a later import found already there stays, for that later import, as a manual account's does ([Undo](#undo)). The confirmation says how many go and how many stay, and for which file.

### What an import keeps

Each import keeps the file's records as the bank had them, always (an OFX transaction's fields, a CSV line's cells, a QIF record's lines), and what became of each: the row it became, the transaction it was kept as (the bank's row, or a row an earlier file brought in, with the proposal you answered, how sure it was, and why it was only possibly the same), the row it was found already imported as, or why it couldn't be read. With them, the file's name, the format, the period it covers, the day the bank's own transactions began when it was reviewed, how the file was read, and the counts. Each row carries its source, the source's own id (an OFX file's FITID) and its import. The rows are kept per account, apart from the bank's transactions, which every sync rewrites and a disconnect deletes. All of it is encrypted, in your [download](data-export.md#history-imports), never shared but for the rows themselves at **Balance and transactions**, and goes with the import's undo, or with the account when you forget it.

## Switching from another app

Bring your history from Monarch, YNAB, Lunch Money, Actual Budget or ZeroSum: its accounts, categories, tags, notes, transfers and split transactions, in one review. Under the Accounts tab's buttons (and on the first screen, before anything is connected), **Switching from another app?** has the way in: choose **Bring your history from Monarch, YNAB, Lunch Money, Actual Budget or ZeroSum** and pick the app's export, its CSV or the zip it gives you. Nothing is stored until you have said where each of its accounts goes and checked the review, and one **Undo** takes all of it out again.

### Exporting from each app

- **Monarch**: Settings, Data, **Download transactions**, for every account at once (or one account: Accounts, the account, Edit, **Download transactions**).
- **YNAB**: in the web app, the menu under your plan's name, **Export plan** (in older versions **Export budget data**). It downloads a zip of two CSV files, the register and the plan: import the zip, or the Register file in it. YNAB 4's register export is read too.
- **Lunch Money**: Transactions, with the view set to every transaction you want (it exports what is in view, as filtered), then the **Export to CSV** icon above the table.
- **Actual Budget**: **All accounts** (so both sides of each transfer are in the file), the menu at the top right of the transactions, **Export**. That is the CSV; Settings, **Export data** gives a zip of Actual's database instead, which Nya can't read ([below](#what-isnt-brought-over)), and the sheet says so if you choose it.
- **ZeroSum**: **Export CSV** above the transactions (every transaction in the view), or the budget menu's data export, a zip of `transactions.csv` and `budget.csv`: import the zip, or `transactions.csv`.

The app is told from the file's row of column names. A file Nya doesn't recognise (an app that changed its export, or a file saved again from a spreadsheet) shows its columns and first rows and asks which app it is from and which column is which.

### How it is read

- **Dates** as the app writes them. When every date in the file fits both month first and day first (a short European YNAB file), the sheet asks which.
- **Amounts** in the file's own decimal mark and sign ("$21.05", "1.250,00 €"). Lunch Money's export doesn't settle which way money out is written: the review shows the direction it read (from the file's income rows when it has them, else Lunch Money's own way, money out positive), with a box to turn it round, and each account's first rows, money out and money in, to check it by.
- **Currency**: Lunch Money's rows say theirs. Other files don't, so their amounts are read in the currency of the account they go into (US dollars for a new one), and **Change** in the review reads every row that doesn't say its own in the one you give. A new account is kept in the currency most of its rows are in (a Lunch Money file in euros makes accounts in euros), as an account a connector fed in another currency is kept; nothing is converted, and totals count it as they count any account in another currency. A linked account's rows must be in the currency its bank keeps it in: rows in another could never be found as the bank's own, so each would count beside them, and the review refuses that, saying which.
- **Payees and notes**: the app's payee (Monarch's Merchant, not its Original Statement, which stays in the import's record) and its notes or memo.
- **Opening balances**: YNAB's, ZeroSum's and Actual's Starting Balance, YNAB's Reconciliation Balance Adjustment and Monarch's Balance Adjustments are filed under **Starting balances**, a transfer category: they count as neither income nor spending. A linked account takes none of them ([below](#where-each-account-goes)).
- **YNAB's flags** become tags ("Red flag").

### Where each account goes

Each of the file's accounts waits for an answer, with how many transactions it has, the dates, its splits and how many have no category, and nothing goes anywhere by itself: **A new manual account** (its name, institution, kind and balance today; the review says what its transactions in the file come to, with **Use it**, since that is the app's balance only if the file holds all of its history), one of **your manual accounts**, one of **your linked accounts**, or **Leave it out**. Several of the file's accounts can go into one. A new account's fields are yours to type at your own pace: nothing is sent while you do, and **Update the review** (or Enter) reads the review again with them, once.

- **Into a manual account**, its rows are matched exactly against what the account holds, as a file's are ([Matching what is already there](#matching-what-is-already-there)): importing the same export again adds nothing.
- **Into a linked account**, they go through the review of [Importing history into a linked account](#importing-history-into-a-linked-account): each row the bank already has is shown beside the bank's own, very likely or likely ones answered **The same** for you to change, possibly the same ones waiting for **The same** or **Its own**. Your answers stay in the sheet as you give them and go with the import; **All the same** and **All their own** answer every one at once, those not listed too. One that is the same is kept as the bank's and never counted twice. A transaction an earlier file brought is found there too, even once the bank has changed its own row since (its amount, say: [When the bank sends the same transactions](#when-the-bank-sends-the-same-transactions)): the app's row is kept as that one, and the transaction counts once, at the bank's amount. The rows before the bank's own take the chart's estimate further back, as a file's do.

  The bank's balance and rows are the account's own, so a linked account never takes what would sit beside them. The app's opening balances and balance adjustments never come in (nor are they walked as money that moved in the chart's estimate; a reconciliation adjustment from before the bank's own rows, usually cents YNAB had missed, is left out with them, so the estimate before it differs by that much). Where the bank's own transactions reach (from its first one on) or a file imported before covers the day, Lunch Money's transaction groups (each the net of transactions those have one by one) and the lines of a split transaction whose whole the file doesn't hold (a line missing) are left out too, and a split Nya can't hold with its parts comes in whole, as they have it: YNAB's with a line of no category, Lunch Money's whose parts are on other days (its parent row), Actual's whose parts don't add up (its own row's total). Before that, nothing has them, so they come in as a manual account takes them: a group at its net, a split as its lines, each under its category. And the rows of a split the export doesn't mark (Monarch's, ZeroSum's, Actual's before 24.8) that are one day's, under one statement or payee, and add up exactly to one of the account's transactions that no row of the file is by itself (a pending one too, which then takes their place once it posts), when they aren't each one by themselves, are taken together as that transaction, their categories its parts. The review says how many of each.

  The bank's rows that are the app's take the category, tags, notes and split they had in the app, so the years the bank also reaches keep your own categorizing. They take them as your own choice for them, ahead of your rules and the bank's categories, which moves their budgets and reports; the review says how many, and untick **Give the transactions your banks already have the category, tags, notes and splits they had** to leave them as they are. Undo puts them back. So does a transaction the bank still has pending when you import: the app's row comes in, its pending one stands in for it until it posts, and the posted one then takes its place, with what the app said of it only as the box says; otherwise the app's row comes in at its total, without its split or tags, so nothing of the app's reaches the bank's row. One you approved in the [review queue](#what-approving-keeps), while pending too, stays where you approved it: it takes the app's tags and notes, never its category or split, and the review says how many stay so (an approval Nya can't read counts as one). A transaction only a file imported before brought (the bank's own rows don't reach it, or don't have it) keeps what it has, and the review says how many: what the app said goes only onto the bank's own rows. To have the app's on those, undo that import before importing the app's file, which then brings them as the app has them (everything else that file brought goes with it). A linked account that [ended](#accounts-that-end) is said: the rows the file adds dated from its end count in nothing, as its bank's own from then don't.

### Categories

Each of the app's categories, with its group, goes to one of yours of the same name (in any case, or the same words), or is offered as a new one, in a group of the app's group name (yours, or a new one). The review lists them, most used first, each with where it goes and how it counts, and you can send any to one of yours or make it new as spending, income or a transfer. What the app says a category is comes with it:

- YNAB's **Inflow: Ready to Assign** (To be Budgeted in older files) and ZeroSum's Ready to Budget go to your **Income**, and the categories of a group YNAB, ZeroSum, Actual or Lunch Money calls Income (YNAB's Inflow) are made as income.
- Monarch's transfer categories (Transfer and Credit Card Payment, and Buy and Sell) are made as transfers, and its income ones (Paychecks, Interest, Business Income, Other Income, Dividends & Capital Gains) as income. Monarch's file has no groups, so this goes by its default categories' names: one you made in Monarch can't be told from the file, is proposed as spending, and is yours to change.
- Lunch Money's export doesn't carry a category's **Treat as income** or **Exclude from totals** settings, and the review says so: a group named Income is proposed as income and a category whose name says transfer or payment ("Payment, Transfer", "Credit Card Payment") as a transfer, for you to check.
- One sent to a category of another kind than the app's is said ("The app counts it as income; Food and Drink counts as spending").

New categories keep to what Nya allows: names unique in any case ("Misc" from two groups becomes "Misc" and "Misc (Fun)") and up to 60 characters, at most 500 categories (a file that would pass that is refused in the review until you send some to yours) and 50 groups (past that, a new category goes in its kind's own group).

### Transfers and rows with no category

Money moved between your own accounts isn't spending. A transfer the app marks (YNAB's and ZeroSum's "Transfer : Savings" payee, whether or not Savings is in the file; a payee naming another of the file's accounts in Actual and ZeroSum, a split's line too; Monarch's and Lunch Money's transfer categories) is filed as Nya files a bank's, "transfer out" or "transfer in", and left out of spending and income on both sides. Actual names a transfer's payee by the other account alone, so one to an account not in the file can't be told from a payee of that name: export every account. YNAB gives a transfer to an account it tracks off budget a category, since it leaves the budget there; both accounts are yours, so here it is a transfer, and the review says how many had one. Export every account for both sides of each transfer to be in the file.

A row with no category counts as spending or income until you categorize it, as a bank's does. The review counts them, for the file and for each account: an account YNAB tracks off budget has none on any of its rows.

### Tags, notes and splits

Tags and notes are kept as those you add in Nya ([Tags and notes](#tags-and-notes)). Tags are matched to yours by name, or made. Nya keeps at most 250, so a file with more new ones than you have room for is refused in the review: delete some of yours, or untick **Bring the tags** to import without them. A longer name is cut to 40 characters, and a transaction with more than 20 tags keeps its first 20, which the review counts.

A split transaction comes in as one transaction with its parts ([Splitting a transaction](#splitting-a-transaction)), adding up exactly to it, each under its category:

- **YNAB**: the lines one after another whose memos start "Split (1/3)", "Split (2/3)" and on (YNAB 4's "(Split 1/3)"), the memo after the mark kept as the part's note.
- **Lunch Money**: the parts naming their transaction's id (Parent ID). Its own row gives the payee and note, and isn't counted beside them.
- **Actual**: the transaction's own row, "(SPLIT INTO 3)", then its parts, "(SPLIT 1 OF 3)", from Actual 24.8 on. Older exports leave the transaction's own row out, so each part comes in as a transaction of its own, with the transaction's payee.
- **Monarch and ZeroSum** write nothing that says which rows were one transaction, so each part comes in as a transaction of its own under its own category, which counts the same in every total, and the review says so; into a linked account, rows that add up to one of the bank's transactions are taken together as it (above).

A split that can't be rebuilt comes in as its lines, each a transaction of its own, and the review says how many lines and why: for instance, its lines aren't together in the file, are money out and money in at once, are in two currencies, don't add up to it or are more than 20 parts, or it is past the 5,000 split transactions Nya keeps.

### The review

Before anything is stored, the sheet shows how many transactions in how many accounts, the dates, how the file was read and from which file in a zip; each of the file's accounts and where it goes; the categories; the transfers, opening balances and rows with no category; the tags; the split transactions rebuilt and those coming in as lines; then for each Nya account, its first rows, money out and money in, how many transactions are new, already there and (for a linked account) the bank's, what a linked account doesn't take, rows in another currency, the money in and out of the new ones, and its lines that can't be read, each with its line number and why (the file's lines that name no account are listed once, for the file). **Import** stays off while an answer is missing or something blocks the import, which is said: too many categories or tags, a new account's balance it can't have, too many manual accounts.

**Import** stores it all or none of it, only as reviewed: if anything changed since you saw it (another import into a linked account, a category made, a manual account added on another device), nothing is stored and the review is read again for you to check. Sent twice (a phone that lost the answer), it is made once.

### Undoing it

Every import from another app is listed under the file picker (**Imported before**), with its file, when, how many transactions it brought and the accounts they went into. **Undo** says first what it would take, as each account's own Undo says it: how many transactions go, how many of them you changed since (they go too), how many are on another account now, how many go as your banks' own, and those a later import found already there, which stay for that import. Then it takes all of it out in one action: each account's import, as that account's own Undo would (a linked account's under every id it has had, so after a re-link a later import's rows stay with it), then what it made that nothing uses now: a new manual account with no other transactions that you haven't changed since and no goal tracks, a tag no transaction or rule holds, a category no transaction, budget or rule uses, and a group left with no category and no budget, this month's or a past one's. What something uses now is kept, and the answer says what and why. Each account's import is also listed on that account, with an Undo of its own. An import that fails part way is taken out again at once; one the server stopped in the middle is listed, and Undo finishes taking it out.

Nothing is written to your balance history: a new account's balance is the one you typed, and a linked account's estimated history is drawn back through its new rows as a file's are, as an estimate.

### What isn't brought over

- **YNAB's and Lunch Money's APIs.** Either would need your personal access token, a key to everything in that app's budget, to change as well as to read, and Nya couldn't be tested against either without real credentials. The exports carry the same transactions, categories, tags, notes and splits, so the importer reads those. What an API would add is Lunch Money's category settings, and YNAB's splits as its own structure rather than the marks in their memos.
- **Actual's full export** (Settings, Export data), a zip of its SQLite database. Reading it would take an SQLite reader, which Nya doesn't have without a new dependency, and Actual's schema followed from release to release. Its CSV holds the same transactions.
- **Budgets and goals** (YNAB's plan file, ZeroSum's `budget.csv`): they aren't transactions. Set yours in Nya ([Budgets on categories and groups](#budgets-on-categories-and-groups)).
- The apps' balances over time, investment holdings, recurring items and rules. Lunch Money's transaction groups come in as one row at their net, as its export writes them, but not into a linked account where its bank's own transactions, or a file imported before, already have them one by one.

### Limits, and what isn't confirmed

- A file of up to 3 MB (a zip's files at most 64, and 6 MB each unpacked) and 30,000 transactions, at most 10,000 of them into one account, 100 accounts and 1,000 of the app's categories in a file. A larger one is refused whole: export a shorter period, or some accounts at a time.
- At most 50 manual accounts in all.
- The 100 previews, imports and undos an hour are shared with the other imports.
- None of these apps publishes a specification of its export. The columns come from each app's help pages, Actual's own source and files people have published, and each reader in `lib/import/apps/` names its sources and what they don't settle. Not confirmed against a real file: how Monarch, Lunch Money and ZeroSum separate tags (read as commas), the exact header of Lunch Money's file (its two spellings are both read) and its sign (shown and asked), YNAB's "Split (1/3)" marks and whether a flag you named is written by its color or its name, ZeroSum's dates (settled from the file, else asked) and how it writes a transfer's payee, and how Monarch and ZeroSum write split transactions. A line that can't be read is listed with its line number and why, never dropped silently.

### What the import keeps

Each account's import is kept as a file's is (the file's records and what became of each: [What is kept](#what-is-kept), [What an import keeps](#what-an-import-keeps)), and the import itself as one entry: the app, the file, each account's import and the app's accounts that went into it, what it made and the counts. All of it is in your [download](data-export.md#app-imports).

## Excluding a transaction

A one-off can swamp a month: a car bought outright, a deposit paid and returned, a work trip that was paid back. Tap the transaction on the Activity tab, then **Exclude from budgets and reports**. It stays in the list, marked "Excluded from budgets and reports", and is left out of every total that already leaves transfers and loan payments out: the month's money in, out and net, its chart, top categories, places and channels, its day's subtotal in the list, the budgets, the Home insights, recurring-bill detection and the Plan's spending, whose label says how many were left out. **Include in budgets and reports** puts it back exactly. It changes only your own totals: a transaction on an account you share is still shared, unmarked. It works on any posted transaction: a bank's, one entered by hand, or one imported from a file. A pending transaction can be excluded once it posts: the bank gives the posted one a new id, and the exclusion is kept by id. If you disconnect the institution and add it back, linking the new account to the old one excludes the same transactions again (see [Removing an institution and adding it back](#removing-an-institution-and-adding-it-back)).

Excluding changes nothing stored about the transaction itself, and never a balance, net worth or the history behind the chart: the money did move. If the bank later removes the transaction, the exclusion goes with it once the next sync saves the removal (as it goes with a transaction entered by hand that you delete), since it can never be shown again; nothing goes while one of your banks' stored transactions can't be read. Which transactions count in a total is decided in one place (`lib/spending.ts`), and the exclusion is kept in one record per transaction (`lib/txn-annotations.ts`), with its [tags and note](#tags-and-notes); an [approval](#reviewing-new-transactions) is kept apart, by the same id. Including one again is recorded too, so what you said about the transaction itself beats an exclusion carried across a re-link, and a [rule](#rules) that hides it. A transaction a rule hides is marked "Hidden by a rule" instead, and the month's summary counts those apart from the ones you left out.

A bank's fee (Plaid's "bank charge", a maintenance or overdraft fee) is spending, not a transfer: it counts in every total, the budgets and recurring-bill detection included, where a monthly fee worth getting rid of shows up.

## Categories and groups

Every transaction is filed under one of your categories, and every category is in a group: food and drink in Food, rent and utilities in Housing, and so on. A category has an id that never changes, so renaming one renames it everywhere at once: on its transactions, in the totals and budgets, and in the API.

### Where they come from

There is nothing to set up. The first time Nya reads your data after categories arrived, it makes them from what is already there: ten groups (Income, Food, Shopping, Housing, Transport, Travel and entertainment, Health and personal care, Services and fees, Other, and Transfers), a category for each of Plaid's sixteen, **other** for a transaction that says nothing about its category, and one for every category already on your data: one you chose for a transaction, one carried across a re-link, one on a transaction you entered or imported, and one a budget was saved under. Each is named as Nya showed it before (on screen, with a capital first letter), so no total, budget or API answer moves. After that, a category a bank or a file brings that you don't have yet is added the next time the Activity tab loads: to the category with the same words, if there is one, or as a new one in Transfers (words starting with "transfer"), Income ("income") or Other.

### How a transaction is filed

By one rule, everywhere: the Activity tab, the totals, budgets, insights, recurring bills, the Plan, the API and the download ([architecture.md](architecture.md#categories)). Most specific first:

1. **Yours**: the category you chose for the transaction, or for the same transaction under an earlier account you linked its new one to, or for a transaction you [imported into a linked account](#when-the-bank-sends-the-same-transactions) that the bank's own now stands for, or the one on a transaction you entered or imported.
2. **As you approved it**: a transaction you [approved](#reviewing-new-transactions) stays under the category it was approved under, whatever a rule, a merchant's default or the bank says later, which its detail offers instead, and when the bank changes its amount too (it then waits for review again, still there). One approved without a category takes what a rule or a default gives it.
3. **A [rule](#rules)** of yours: below a choice made on one transaction, above what the bank says.
4. **The merchant's [default](#merchant-defaults)**, for a merchant's new transactions: below your rules, since a rule says outright what a default works out from a few choices.
5. **Plaid's category** for it.
6. **The words the transaction carries**, for one stored before Plaid's categories were kept.
7. Otherwise the **uncategorized** category: other, unless you renamed it.

The name shown is always your category's, never one stored on the transaction.

### What a category counts as

A group is **spending**, **income** or **transfers**, and its categories count as that. Transfers are left out of the month's totals and the budgets. Seeded, Transfers holds transfer in, transfer out and loan payments, exactly what the totals left out before. Moving a category into a group of another kind changes how its transactions count, in every total and budget, and the screen says so before you move it: moving loan payments into a spending group counts them as spending. A group's kind is chosen when it is added and doesn't change.

A few rules read the words the bank or you gave a transaction rather than its category's name, so renaming or regrouping never moves them: recurring detection never takes a restaurant, a food shop, a way of getting about or a general shop for a yearly bill, and counts a loan payment as a bill whatever group holds it; the Plan finds loan payments, cash and refunds the same way, and counts income by its category's kind.

### Managing them

Under **Manage** on the Accounts tab, the **Categories** card counts your categories and groups, and **Edit categories** opens them, group by group, a screen at a time, for a phone. A change shows at once on your transactions and budgets.

- **Add** a category to a group, with an optional icon (an emoji). A name is up to 60 characters, and no two categories, or two groups, share one, whatever their capitals.
- **Rename** a category, give it an **icon**, or **move** it to another group.
- **Archive** one you no longer use: it leaves the lists you choose a category from, and stays on every transaction filed under it, which count as before. **Unarchive** brings it back.
- **Merge** one into another of the same kind: every transaction filed under it is then filed under the other, its budget is added to the other's, and it goes. Past months' totals stay as they were, since they are the same transactions, but a group's budget limit can change: the category's spending goes where it went, into another group too. Merging into another kind is refused, since it would change your totals: move it first. A merge can't be undone.
- **Delete** a category only when nothing uses it: no transaction filed under it (one you chose it for, entered or imported with it, into a manual account or a linked one, of any date, or a bank's from the last year, a hidden account's or one after its account [ended](#accounts-that-end) among them, since those are still stored), no budget, no [rule](#rules) that matches on it or files into it, no merchant's [default](#merchant-defaults), no transaction [approved](#reviewing-new-transactions) under it, and none of Plaid's categories. Otherwise the screen says what uses it, and offers a merge or archiving instead (or, for a rule or a default, changing the rule or clearing the default first). A rule and a default follow their category through a rename and a merge.
- The uncategorized category can be renamed, and moved to another spending group, but not archived, merged away or deleted.

Groups can be **added**, with their kind, **renamed**, moved **up** or **down** (the order they are listed in everywhere), and **deleted** once empty and without a budget of their own.

Choosing a category, for a transaction or for one you enter by hand, offers your categories by group, without the archived ones (but for the one it has).

### Starter sets

A starter set is a ready-made set of categories and groups to start from. **Start from a starter set**, on the Categories screen, offers three:

- **Simple**: about a dozen broad categories with plain names (Food, Shopping, Transport, Home and utilities, Health, Fees, Travel and others) in six groups: Income, Everyday, Home and bills, Fun, Other and Transfers. Personal care, home improvement and government and non profit are archived while nothing uses them.
- **Detailed**: the set Nya starts everyone with, one category for each of Plaid's, in ten groups, named as they were at the start. On a set still as Nya made it, it changes nothing. After Simple it brings the start back, archived ones shown again. After Freelancer it puts Nya's categories back as they were, and leaves the two business groups and their categories, which are yours then to keep or delete (or undo Freelancer instead, right after applying it).
- **Freelancer**: Nya's categories for your personal spending, and two groups of your own: Business income (Client payments, Other business income) and Business expenses (Software and subscriptions, Equipment, Office and supplies, Business travel, Business meals, Advertising, Professional services, Business fees, Taxes). Your income category is called Personal income. Plaid can't tell a business purchase from a personal one, so file them yourself, or with a [rule](#rules).

What a set can change, and nothing else:

- **Add** groups, and categories of its own. One you already have by that name (in any case) is used instead, moved into the set's group.
- **Rename** the categories Nya made at the start, and **move** them into the set's groups. Each is found by the key it was made with (Plaid's category, or the uncategorized one), so one you renamed is found too. One another was merged into is placed as what it is, never as what went into it, and the preview says where the merged one files.
- **Archive** one of those it has no place for, only while nothing uses it: no transaction of any date, no budget and no rule, and no category merged into it that the set keeps (so what Plaid files there never lands in an archived category). One in use stays shown, and the preview says why.
- **Show again** one it places that is archived.
- **Remove** a group it left empty that is one of a set's (by its name and kind) and has no budget of its own, now or in any past month Nya keeps (see [looking back at a month](#looking-back-at-a-month)), so no past month loses its budget. A group of yours, or one with a budget in a past month, stays, empty, and the preview says so.
- Put the groups in its order, its own first.

It never deletes or merges a category, never moves one into a group of another kind (one you moved into a spending group stays there, so nothing counts differently and no total moves), and never changes what a group with a budget of its own holds, so that budget covers what it did. A name another category has is left as it is. Your own categories (ones you made, or a file brought, even with one of Nya's merged into them) and your own groups stay where they are.

**See what Simple changes** (and the same for each set) shows, before anything changes, what the set would do to your categories as they are, worked out with your budgets, your budgets by month and what uses each category: the groups it adds, removes and keeps, each category it adds, renames, moves, archives or shows again, what it leaves as it is and why, and how your transactions and budgets file afterwards. Every transaction stays in its category (under its new name, counting in its new group), and every budget stays with its category, by id, wherever it moves. **Apply** is a second tap; if what it would do changed since the preview (your categories changed on another device, say), nothing changes and the preview is worked out again, so the next **Apply** applies what it shows. **Undo** is offered right after, until you leave the screen: names, groups and archiving go back, the groups it removed come back as the same groups, and the categories and groups it added go while nothing uses them. What it can't put back (a name another category has taken since, a category used since, a group whose name another group of another kind has taken) is kept, and said; so is anything changed since the apply, on another device, which stays as it is now. Applying a set twice changes nothing the second time.

While your categories are still the ones Nya made (nothing renamed, regrouped, archived or merged), the Budgets tab offers the starter sets once, in a card above the budgets. **See starter sets** or **Keep mine** ends the offer on that device; the sets stay on the Categories screen. The read-only API and the download see your categories as they are after a set, as after any change; neither applies one.

### Totals by group

The Activity tab's **Top spending** is by group: each group's total, which a tap opens to its categories'. The Budgets tab lists budgets by group the same way.

### Budgets on categories and groups

A budget can be on a category or on a group as a whole (**Add Budget** offers both, for spending: income and transfers never count against a budget, so they aren't offered, and a budget already on one says so). A group's own budget is a limit on everything in it, its categories without a budget included. A group without one shows its budgeted categories added up, budgets and spending alike. When a group has both, its limit is the smaller of its own amount and its categories' budgets added up, and a line under its meter says which applies:

- **Capped by the group's budget**: its categories' budgets add up to more than the group's.
- **Limited to its categories' budgets**: every category in it has a budget, and they add up to less. An archived category without a budget is left aside while it has no spending that month; archived, it still files what its bank or you give it, and with spending and no budget of its own, nothing limits it but the group's amount, which then stays the limit, as for any category without a budget.

Each group opens to its categories' meters and its own amount. The month's total counts each group once, at its limit. Home warns about a group nearing or over its limit as it does about a category, when the group has an amount of its own (without one, its categories' budgets warn on their own).

Budgets are kept by category id, so a renamed category keeps its budget, and a merged one's is added to where it went. A budget left on a category or group since deleted (on another device, say) is shown, and can be removed. The release before this one still reads the category budgets, by name ([operations.md](operations.md#category-ids-the-migration-and-rolling-back)).

### Suggestions from history

Editing a budget, or choosing a category or group to add one to, shows what was spent there on average over the last three complete months, rounded up to a round figure, with one tap to use it (**Use $415.00**, or **Add at $415.00**): "Suggested: $415.00, from the average of July, August and September ($412.37 a month)." It is counted by the rule every total follows, as the meters count spending: in the budgets' currency (what is in another is named under the budget it would have counted for, never added), without transfers, cash withdrawals, loan payments, what you excluded or what a [rule](#rules) hides, and with a refund as money in, never off spending. A category's is what is filed under it now, a group's everything in it now. A month with nothing spent counts as nothing. The average is to the cent, and the suggestion is that rounded up: to the unit under $100, then to $5, $50, $500 as it grows, from $100 up never more than 5% over.

Only months Nya's history reaches whole are averaged: never one before your first transaction, nor the month your history begins in (unless from its first day), nor one in which a bank's stored transactions begin after it began, which would count as spending nothing. With fewer than three, the suggestion says which months it is from and why the others aren't counted; with none, there is no suggestion yet, and it says why (or, before your first transaction, that there are none yet). A month averaged that may be missing transactions (a connection that stopped syncing, transactions that couldn't be read) is named, with why: the suggestion may be low. A category with nothing spent in those months has no suggestion, and says so (and, when what was spent there is in another currency, that too). With rollover on, the suggestion is still the plain monthly average: what rolled over isn't spending.

Suggestions are worked out on the server for this month only, from the stored transactions the Budgets tab reads for its months anyway, never from Plaid, and aren't stored. The read-only API doesn't offer them.

### In the API and the download

The read-only API gives each transaction's category id and group (`category_id`, `group_id`, `group`), lists your categories with their ids, groups and kinds and the groups themselves, finds one category's transactions by its id (`category_id`), and gives budgets and spending by group as well. A transaction's `category` is its category's name. Until the app has stored your categories (the first time it loads them after this release), the ids are `null`. The [download](data-export.md) has your categories (`categories`), your budgets with their ids and groups (`budgets`, `group_budgets`), and each transaction's category, its id and its group (`nya_category`, `nya_category_id`, `nya_group`). Sharing still shares no category.

## Tags and notes

Tags cut across categories: "reimbursable", "vacation 2026", "tax deductible", "gift". A transaction can carry several, and a note says what a category can't ("paid back by Sam", "the deposit, refunded in March").

- **On a transaction.** Tap it on the Activity tab: its detail shows its tags, each with an × to take it off, a field to add one (it offers the tags whose names hold what you type, and **Add** makes a new tag when none has that name, in any case: "Work" and "work" are one tag), and its note. Tags show as small chips after the merchant on its row, and a row with a note says "note" there (the note itself is in the detail). A pending transaction can be tagged and given a note once it posts, as it can be excluded: the bank gives the posted one a new id.
- **Any transaction**: a bank's, one entered by hand, imported from a file, or brought in from SimpleFIN. A note on a transaction you entered or imported into a manual account is the note written with it, the one its edit form shows, so a transaction never has two; a note on a bank's is kept beside it, and so is one on a SimpleFIN transaction, which stays as the bank gave it, and one on a transaction [imported into a linked account](#importing-history-into-a-linked-account), which stays as its file gave it: the file's memo shows as its note until you write your own, and clearing yours leaves none. When the bank later sends a transaction you imported, its row takes the imported one's tags and note, as it takes its category.
- **The Tags screen.** Under **Manage** on the Accounts tab, the **Tags** card opens your tags: make one, rename it (everywhere at once), give it another color (the arrow keys move between colors), or delete it. Deleting a tag takes it off every transaction it is on and out of every rule that adds it, after the screen says how many; one put on a transaction on another device while it was being deleted comes back off. When your tags can't be read, the screen says so rather than show none.
- **Search and filter.** The Activity tab's search finds tag names and note text too, and **All tags** filters the list to the transactions carrying one tag, whoever put it there.
- **Spending by tag**, beside top spending: what each tag's transactions spent that month, by the same rule as every other total (one currency, without transfers, loan payments, or what you excluded or a rule hides). A transaction adds to its tag exactly what it adds to its category, so the two agree; one with two tags counts under both, which the card says.
- **Kept with the transaction.** Tags and notes are kept by the transaction's id (`lib/txn-annotations.ts`, in the record its exclusion is kept in) and applied when it is read, so a resync changes nothing. If the bank removes the transaction, they go with it once the next sync saves the removal. If you disconnect the institution and add it back, linking the new account to the old one brings them back (see [Removing an institution and adding it back](#removing-an-institution-and-adding-it-back)); identical transactions tagged differently carry nothing rather than guess.
- **Private.** Sharing never shows a tag or a note, on any account at any level. The [API](#the-api-and-the-mcp-server) and the [download](data-export.md) have them: they are yours.

Limits: 250 tags, a name of 40 characters, 20 tags put on one transaction by hand and 100 taken off it, a note of 500 characters.

## Rules

A rule matches transactions and then files them under a category, gives them another name, adds tags, or hides them (leaves them out of budgets and reports, still listed). It changes every transaction that matches, past and future, at once: rules are applied each time transactions are read, never written into them, so turning one off puts every transaction back exactly as it was, and the rule is kept.

- **Conditions**, all of which must match: the **name** (contains, is, or starts with some text, in any case, with accents, extra spaces and the kind of apostrophe or quote ignored, so “Trader Joe’s” typed on a phone matches the bank's TRADER JOE'S; it reads the name before any rule renames it: the bank's, the one you gave the merchant and the bank's under it, and the merchant behind a payment processor such as PayPal), the **amount** (more than, less than, or between two amounts, as money out, money in, or either; amounts are sizes, in the transaction's own currency, with nothing converted), the **account** (and, through the links you made, an earlier account's transactions; never one whose earlier account is connected again; one that [ended](#accounts-that-end) is listed with its end, and a rule on it applies to its transactions from before then, the only ones listed or counted), the **institution**, the **category** it is under before any rule runs (so before a [merchant's default](#merchant-defaults) or an [approval](#reviewing-new-transactions) files it: the bank's, or one you chose), and the **source**: from a bank, entered by hand, imported from a file (into a manual account or [a linked one](#importing-history-into-a-linked-account) alike: the account tells them apart), or from SimpleFIN. Never a regular expression.
- **What it does**: file it under a category, show it under another name, add tags, and hide it.
- **What you set on one transaction always wins.** A category you choose for a transaction, a name you give its merchant, a tag you take off it, an exclusion you set or clear on it, and what was carried to it from an earlier account, or from a transaction you imported that it now stands for, all beat every rule. A transaction you entered by hand keeps the name you typed, and one you entered or imported keeps its own category when it has one: a rule files one without a category, tags it and hides it. An imported transaction's name came with it from a bank's file, and a SimpleFIN one's from the bank's feed, so a rule renames either as it renames a bank's. You can give one imported into a manual account a name of your own in its edit form, which no rule replaces, and one imported into a linked account by renaming its merchant, as a bank's; a SimpleFIN transaction stays as the bank gave it (its payee and note can't be edited, nor can a vendor rename apply to it), so a rule is the way to show it under another name: **Create a rule from this** starts one. Editing such a transaction starts from its own name and category, never a rule's, and saving it (to fix its note, say) never makes a rule's category or name its own, so turning the rule off still puts it back.
- **Order.** Rules run in the order the Rules screen lists them. When two rules that match one transaction both set its category, or its name, the higher one decides it; tags are added from every rule that matches, and any can hide it. A new rule goes first, so a rule made from a transaction applies to it. Rules never see each other's changes: a category condition reads the category a transaction has before any rule runs.
- **Making one.** Under **Manage** on the Accounts tab, the **Rules** card lists your rules in a sentence each ("If name contains “amzn” and money out more than 50: file under Shopping, tag Work."), with a switch to turn each off, **Up** and **Down** to reorder, and **Edit**; **New rule** makes one. On the Activity tab, a transaction's detail has **Create a rule from this**, which starts from its name (the one before any rule renamed it, which a rule matches) and its category, and lists the rules that match it. After you give a transaction another category, the tab offers to do the same for every transaction with its name, as a rule. Before a rule is saved, the drawer says how many of your stored transactions it matches, of any date ("Matches 43 of your stored transactions, of any date"), lists the newest few, says how many keep a category or name you chose yourself and how many you [approved](#reviewing-new-transactions) stay where you approved them, and, for a rule with others above it, that a rule above that also matches decides the category or the name.
- **Everywhere.** Rules apply wherever transactions are read: the Activity tab, every total and budget, Home's insights, recurring bills, the cash forecast and the Plan, reports, the API and the MCP server, and the download. Sharing shows none of what a rule does: what someone sees of an account you share is the bank's own name and amount.
- **Ids, not names.** A rule names a category and tags by id, so renaming either changes nothing, and a merged category is followed to where it went (the rule shows, and is edited with, the category it went into). A category a rule uses can't be deleted (the Categories screen says how many rules use it, to change first). If one is deleted anyway (from outside the app, or on another device while a rule naming it was being saved), the rule is marked broken and does nothing at all until it is changed: it never files anything somewhere else. A deleted tag comes out of every rule, even one saved on another device as the tag was deleted, and one whose rules couldn't be read then once they can be; meanwhile no rule shows it, and editing the rule simply leaves it out. A rule left with nothing to do says so.
- **When something can't be read.** Rules that can't be read apply to nothing, and the Activity tab says so. Categories that can't be read leave out every rule that names one, and the tab says that too; account links that can't be read leave a rule on an account to that account's own transactions, with a note.
- **SimpleFIN's transactions** are matched and changed as a bank's are: a rule renames, tags, hides or files them (`source` "from SimpleFIN" matches only them, as "from Lunch Flow" matches only Lunch Flow's, and **institution** reads their bank as the app names it). A pair Nya works out between two of your SimpleFIN accounts keeps the pair's category ([transfers and categories](#where-its-accounts-live)): a rule's category never splits one.
- **What a rule hides** is told apart from what you excluded everywhere it is counted: the Activity tab ("1 transaction a rule hides"), a report's totals and its appendix ("Not counted: hidden by a rule"), and the Plan.
- **Vendor renames stay as they were.** Renaming a merchant from a transaction's detail still renames every transaction from it, past and future, and it is your own choice, so it beats a rule's name. Renames are kept where they always were, untouched, so a rollback reads them exactly as before ([operations.md](operations.md#rules-tags-and-notes-and-rolling-back)).

Limits: 250 rules, 10 conditions in a rule, 10 tags a rule adds, text of 100 characters to match, and a name of 100 characters. Rules are kept as one encrypted value (`lib/rules.ts`); applying a hundred rules to five thousand transactions takes a few tens of milliseconds.

## Reviewing new transactions

Turn on the review queue and each new transaction waits for you to approve it: a quick look at each, one at a time or all at once, as YNAB's approve step and its Card Mode do. Once approved, a transaction keeps the category you approved it under until you change it.

- **Turning it on.** Under **Manage** on the Accounts tab, the **Review queue** card turns it on, from today, or from 7 or 30 days back to catch up on what came in lately, never further, so years of history never fill it. **Turn off** stops marking anything and keeps every approval. Turned on again, it starts from the new day.
- **What needs review**: a transaction from your bank, from SimpleFIN or from a file you import (into a manual account or a linked one), dated on or after the queue's first day, until you approve it. One you entered by hand is already as you meant it. A file of last year's transactions imported next month adds nothing to review, each being dated before the queue began. A pending transaction can be approved, and stays approved when it posts with its new id, as the same transaction; posted at another amount (a tip added), it comes back to review, saying what it was approved at. When the bank sends a transaction you [imported into a linked account](#when-the-bank-sends-the-same-transactions), the bank's takes the imported one's approval too, once it has posted: a pending one shown in its place waits for review meanwhile, since its amount can still change, as it takes no split or group either.
- **One transaction, once.** A split transaction is approved whole, its parts with it, and counted once; each transaction of a group is approved on its own, and the group's row says how many of its transactions wait. Counts are of transactions, as every count is.
- **On the Activity tab**, while the queue is on: how many wait ("12 to review"); **Needs review**, which filters the list as the search and the tag filter do; **Approve the 3 listed**, which approves what the list shows that needs it (the month, or what a cash flow pick lists, through the search and the filters), then says how many with **Undo**; and **Review one at a time**, which opens Card Mode. A row that waits says "to review", and its detail has **Approve**, or, once approved, when and **Review again**.
- **Card Mode** shows one transaction at a time, full screen, made for a phone: the merchant, the amount, the day, the account, the category, the note and the tags, and what to know first (the bank changed its amount since you approved it, a default filed it, it is split or in a group, it is a transaction you [imported](#when-the-bank-sends-the-same-transactions) whose amount the bank has changed since). Swipe right, or **Approve**, to approve it. Swipe left, or **Recategorize**, to choose another of your categories from the list and **Use** it (Enter on the list does too; moving through the list chooses nothing): it is approved with it, and the choice counts toward the merchant's [default](#merchant-defaults). **Skip** leaves it for later, **Split** and **Group** open the Activity tab's own sheets over the card (once it has posted; a group's transaction has **Edit group** and no Split, as on the Activity tab), and **Undo** takes back the last action, whatever it was, a category chosen included, exactly. It ends with **All caught up** and how many you approved, or, with some skipped, **Done for now** and **Review the skipped**, which deals them again and keeps the count, and the undo, of everything before. Every swipe has a button, and a quick flick swipes as a long drag does; a drag that goes up or down first scrolls instead; with reduced motion the cards don't fly; and a screen reader hears each card's merchant, amount and category before Enter approves it, then what each action did and how many are left.
- **Keyboard**, on a desktop. In Card Mode: → or A approves (Enter too, on the card), ← or C recategorizes (Enter in the category list uses the one shown), ↓ or S skips, U undoes, Esc closes, ? lists them. On the Activity tab while the queue is on: R opens Card Mode, N turns the Needs review filter on or off, Shift+A asks, then approves what is listed, U undoes that, and ? lists them, with **Use these keys on this device** to turn them off (for speech input, or keys pressed by mistake), a switch the Review queue card under Manage has too, so it is always within reach, with a keyboard or without; Card Mode's keys act only while it is open. Never with Ctrl, Alt or Command, so none takes a browser's shortcut, and none while you type in a field or choose from a list.

### What approving keeps

Approving says a transaction is right as it is: its amount, its category and its name, as you saw them. Following ZeroSum's rule, nothing you didn't do moves it to another category, and nothing changes it silently:

- **A rule added or changed later** doesn't move it: it stays in the category you approved it under, and its detail says what the rule would do ("Your rule would file it under Household"), with **Use Household** to take it. The rule's other matches follow it as always, and the rule drawer's preview says how many approved ones it leaves where they are. A **merchant's default** that forms later, and **the bank's own category** changing in a sync, are offered the same way. One you approved without a category takes the category a rule or a default gives it later: that fills an empty place, and moves nothing you chose.
- **The bank changing its amount** (a tip, part of it refunded) puts it back in the queue, saying what it was approved at, still under the category you approved it under, with what a rule, a default or the bank would file it under still offered: a dinner you filed under Restaurants while it was pending stays there when it posts with a tip. A split transaction shows the difference as a part of its own, as it always has, so nothing is spread silently over what you approved. So does a bank transaction that [stands for one you imported](#when-the-bank-sends-the-same-transactions) from the bank's file, which keeps standing for it once the bank changes its amount: approved at the file's amount (on the imported one, or on the bank's), it comes back to the queue, and since the note under it already says that the bank changed the file's amount, its detail says only that you approved it at that amount, and Card Mode says the two at once.
- **A name** the bank or a rule gives it since shows, with the name you approved it under in its detail: a name never changes a total, and a merchant's transactions stay named alike.
- **A rule's other actions** still apply to it, as to every transaction the rule matches: its name, its tags, and hiding it from totals (it then shows "Hidden by a rule"). Only its category waits for you.
- **Another app's history** imported into its account ([Switching from another app](#where-each-account-goes)) doesn't move it either: the app's tags and notes come onto it, never the app's category or split.
- **What you do** always applies: a category you choose for it (which its approval then holds), a split, a group, renaming its merchant, excluding it, and editing the amount or currency of one you entered or imported into a manual account, whose approval follows your edit rather than putting it back in the queue as if the bank had changed it.

Approvals are kept by transaction id, never by what a transaction says (`transaction-approvals`, `lib/review-store.ts`, encrypted, and in your [download](data-export.md#transaction-approvals)), and whether the queue is on, from which day, in `review-settings`. Nothing about them is written into a transaction: they are applied as transactions are read, everywhere a category is (the totals, budgets, reports, the API and the download file an approved transaction where you approved it). An approval goes with its transaction: when the bank removes it, when you delete a transaction you entered, when an import is undone. Like splits, approvals don't carry across a [re-link](#removing-an-institution-and-adding-it-back): a re-linked account's transactions are filed as unapproved ones are, by your rules, your defaults and the bank, so their categories may have changed; those since the queue began need review again (look at them before **Approve the N listed** takes them all at once), and older ones are filed so without entering the queue. An approval that can't be read leaves that transaction's review not known, never "to review", with a note, and the Review queue card offers to remove only the damaged ones, once you confirm. At most 50,000 approvals, and 500 at a time. Past that, approving is refused until you make room, which the Review queue card offers once approvals near it: forgetting the 10,000 oldest, by when you made them, once you confirm. Their transactions stay, filed as ones you never approved are, so some may show another category, and those dated from the queue's first day wait for review again.

## Merchant defaults

Give a merchant's transactions a category a couple of times and Nya learns it: the merchant's **default category** changes when two of your last three categorizations of it agree (as YNAB's payee defaults have since August 2026). The default then files the merchant's new transactions that nothing more specific gives a category.

- **What counts**: choosing a category for one of the merchant's transactions, on the Activity tab or in Card Mode. Splitting one, and a group's category, don't. A merchant is the one a [vendor rename](#rules) is kept for: a bank's transactions, and those imported into a linked account. Those kept on a manual account (entered by hand, imported into one, or from SimpleFIN) have none, so a default never files them.
- **Two of the last three.** Nya keeps the merchant's last three categorizations. Two that agree set the default, or move it; three that differ change nothing, so there is never a tie to break. A category merged since counts as the one it went into; one deleted agrees with nothing.
- **New transactions only.** A default files the merchant's transactions dated from the day it was set, so last year's totals never move because of one. When it moves to another category, the new one files what is dated from that day on, and what it filed before stays where it filed it, each earlier category with its days (the last twelve): a move never re-files the past. A transaction filed under an earlier one says so, "Filed by your default for Costco, in the category it had on this day (it has moved since)".
- **Its place**: below the category you choose for a transaction, what you approved and your rules, above the bank's ([How a transaction is filed](#how-a-transaction-is-filed)). A [rule](#rules)'s condition on the category reads the category before a default or an approval files the transaction (the bank's, or one you chose): "category is Groceries" doesn't match a transaction that only a default files under Groceries.
- **Seeing it.** A transaction a default filed says so in its detail, "Filed by your default for Costco (two of your last three)", with **Clear default**. Under **Manage** on the Accounts tab, **Defaults**, beside the rules, lists every merchant with one, its category, since when and how many of its last three agree, each with **Clear**. Clearing a default is yours to do, and takes back what it filed, before a move as since: those transactions go back to where your rules and the bank file them. It forgets its categorizations too, so two new ones set it afresh.
- **Its category** can't be deleted while it is one, or one it had still files days: clear the default first, or merge the category, which the default follows.
- **Kept** as one encrypted value (`vendor-defaults`, `lib/vendor-default-store.ts`, in your [download](data-export.md#vendor-defaults)), each merchant under a random id, its name inside the encryption, never in the database's keys. At most 1,000 merchants; past that, the one without a default categorized longest ago makes room (an undo in Card Mode doesn't bring that one back). When they can't be read, no default files anything, the Activity tab says so, and nothing is saved over them; damaged ones can be removed from the Defaults card, once you confirm.

## Savings goals

The Budgets tab's **Goals** card tracks a target against an account's balance: an emergency fund, a trip, a down payment. A goal has a name, a target and the account it counts, and can have a plan: part of that account's balance set aside for it (an **allocation**), a **monthly target** and a **target date**. **Add Goal** opens the editor, and tapping a goal edits it; both check every field as the server will, on the balances on screen.

### What a goal counts

An allocation is a claim on part of the balance, not a separate pot: the money stays in the account. Goals on one account never count more than it holds:

- A goal with an allocation counts it. What the allocations on an account leave is its **unallocated** remainder, shown above the goals for each account that has any.
- A goal without an allocation counts that remainder: the whole balance when nothing on the account is allocated, which is exactly what every goal counted before allocations existed, so goals saved before them show as they always did. Two goals without allocations on one account each count the same remainder, as they always did, and each says so, with how to split it.
- A save can't raise an account's allocations above its balance. The editor says how much is free, and the server checks every save against the newest balance it measured: one that would raise an account's allocations above it, or allocate anything from an account whose balance it doesn't know, is refused with the reason, which the editor shows. When a balance falls after the allocations were set, they are kept as set, so an unrelated edit still saves, and each goal shows its allocation scaled down to fit, in proportion and to the cent, with a note to lower them; the account's line says by how much they are over. An overdrawn account counts as nothing.
- Amounts are in the account's currency, no finer than its smallest unit (whole yen for a yen account), and nothing is converted. A manual account's balance is in its own currency: the one chosen for it (US dollars for one made before a currency could be chosen), or the one it was kept in from SimpleFIN or Lunch Flow.
- A credit card's or a loan's balance is money owed, not saved, so a goal can't start tracking one, nor take an allocation from it, and the editor doesn't offer them. A goal saved on one before this release shows no progress, and says why, rather than reading a debt as savings; the [debt payoff plan](#debt-payoff-plan) is for paying one down.
- A goal on a hidden account says **Tracked account is hidden**; one whose account is gone, **Linked account disconnected**. One whose account was reconnected under a new id and [linked to it](#removing-an-institution-and-adding-it-back) follows it, allocation and all, and says so.
- A goal on an account that [ended](#accounts-that-end) (closed, no longer yours, or no longer followed, from a day you chose, or assumed closed once its bank stopped returning it) says so, with the day, and an assumed closure says where to answer. From that day the account counts in nothing, as everywhere in Nya: the goal counts none of its balance, its allocation claims nothing, the account has no unallocated line, and the goal isn't projected, since nothing more goes into it. Its plan is kept as set: reopen the account and the goal counts again, allocation and all. The editor doesn't offer such an account for a goal, and nothing more can be set aside from one; an allocation already on it can be kept, or cleared.

### When it will be reached

The pace is what went into the account in each of the last three complete months:

- From its **transactions**, the net money in: deposits and transfers in, less what went out. Unlike spending, a transfer into savings counts here: it is the contribution. Pending transactions wait until they post, and interest and dividends are counted apart (they grow the balance, but nobody put them in): by your bank's label, unless you or a rule filed the transaction under another category, which wins, as it does everywhere, so a transfer the bank called interest that you filed as a transfer in is money put in. A transaction you [excluded](#excluding-a-transaction) from budgets and reports, or a [rule](#rules) hid from them, still counts here: the money moved all the same. The rows are read as the Activity tab lists them, rules applied: a rule's name changes nothing here, and a category a rule files a row under counts as one you chose would (a row filed as interest or dividends is counted apart, one filed under anything else isn't, whatever its bank called it).
- For an account whose transactions Nya doesn't have (an investment account, a connection that brings in balances only, a manual account with nothing entered), or couldn't read this time, the change in its **recorded balance**, from the last balance recorded in one month's last week to the next month's, from measured balances only, never an estimate. That includes interest and market moves, which can't be told apart from what you put in, and the goal says so, and says whose transactions couldn't be read when that is why.
- A month before Nya's data for the account begins, one that began after its connection's last good sync (a bank that needs you to sign in again, say), or one without a recorded balance at its end, is unknown, never zero; the pace averages the months it knows. Transactions [imported into the account from its bank's files](#importing-history-into-a-linked-account) count as the bank's do, and its data begins where they do, as far back as its files reach without a break to the bank's own: a month they don't cover to its first day says so. At a bank whose transactions don't come in (Plaid doesn't provide them, you didn't allow them, or it hasn't synced yet), only the files say which days they cover: the account's transactions count once its files reach, without a break, to the end of the last month the pace averages, from where they begin, and until then it is paced by its recorded balance, as before anything was imported.

The goal says what the pace means: **On track for March 2027**, from what went in, or **at your $250.00 monthly target** when there is no history to go by. With a target date it says what each month until then needs: **$140.00 a month short of June 2027; at this pace, reached in September 2027**, or **On track for March 2027, ahead of June 2027**. The projection counts whole monthly contributions from next month on. When nothing, or less than nothing, went in over those months, it says **No contributions in the last 3 months** and gives no date, whatever the monthly target says; but only when a month can say so: months that may all be short, or aren't known, say that what went in lately isn't known, and why, and the goal is projected at its monthly target if it has one. A goal reached says **Reached**, and a target date that passed says so, with when it will be reached. When several goals share an account, what goes into it isn't any one goal's, so each is projected at its own monthly target, or asks for one. A month that may be missing transactions (one its connection stopped syncing in, an institution still importing older ones, or the month the account's transactions begin in) makes the pace uncertain, and the goal says which and why. Each account is judged by its own connection, never by its institution's name, which two connections can share. The editor shows the outlook as you type.

None of it is stored. It is worked out each time the card is shown, from the balances on screen and the months the server reads from stored data (`GET /api/goals/progress`, which never asks Plaid), by the same code the API uses (`lib/goal-projection.ts`).

### Where you should be by now

With a target date, the meter has a lighter tick: where the goal would be on a straight line from what it held the day the date was set to the target on the date. The goal says the same under it, in words (**To reach it by Jun 30, 2027, it would have $2,400.00 by now**), and a screen reader is told it with the meter's figures. Moving the date keeps the line's start; moving the goal to another account starts it again, from what it holds there. A goal moved by a page from before plans, or by the release before after a rollback, has no line on its new account until it is next saved here, which starts one.

### Archiving

A goal reached is archived rather than deleted, so its record survives: archiving keeps its name, target, plan, the day (your own, as is the day a line to a target date starts) and what it held then, and releases its allocation, so the money is free for the others. Archived goals are in a list under the rest that opens to **Restore** one (without its allocation, which can be set again) or **Delete** it for good. A goal not reached can be archived or deleted. There can be 20 goals, archived ones included: the release before this one refuses to save more, so after a rollback it can still save the list.

### Where they are kept

A goal's name, target and account stay in the `goals` value, as they always have. Its plan is kept by goal id in the `goal-plans` store on the [storage seam](architecture.md#storage-seam), because the release before rewrites `goals` with those four fields only, and would drop anything else there on its first save. What a rollback sees, and the order a save writes the two in, is in [operations.md](operations.md#goals-plans-and-rolling-back). Goals or plans that can't be read pause the card, as goals that can't be read always have, rather than show goals without their plans and let the next save write over them. When it is plans whose bytes are damaged, the card says how many and offers to remove them, once you confirm: their goals then show again, with their names, targets and accounts, and no plan, to set again. A plan a newer version of Nya saved is never offered for removal, only counted: it waits for that version.

### In the API and the download

The read-only API's `goals` endpoint and the MCP tool `get_goals` give each goal as the card shows it, on the balances Nya last measured: what it counts, its allocation, monthly target and target date, the months behind its pace, its projection and the card's own sentences about it, with each account's unallocated remainder. A goal on an account that ended is `ended`, with the account's end (`account_lifecycle`). A goal on a hidden account says only that its account is hidden, with nothing of the account, unless hidden accounts are asked for, as everywhere in the API. The [download](data-export.md#budgets-and-goals) has each goal with its plan (`goals`) and the plans as stored (`goal-plans`). Goals are never shared.

## Budget rollover and income carryover

A budget can roll over: what is left of it at the end of a month is added to the next month's, and what is overspent is taken off it, so a quiet month for groceries leaves more for the next, and an expensive one is made up over the months after. Separately, someone whose income doesn't arrive every month can turn on income carryover, which keeps a running total of what is left to allocate. Both are worked out from your transactions each time and never stored, so recategorizing a transaction in a past month, excluding one, merging two categories or renaming one changes every carry it touches at once, and so do your [rules](#rules) and an [account's end](#accounts-that-end): a transaction a rule hides counts as an excluded one does, in neither spending nor income; one a rule files under another category counts there, in past months too; and an account that ended counts in no month from its day, until it is reopened, when everything comes back exactly.

### Rollover

Open a budget on the Budgets tab and choose **Turn on rollover**, counting from this month or an earlier one: any of the last 12 months, or further back as far as the first month with transactions, ten years at most, and never before the budget began. A start before the first month with transactions counts from that month, and says so. From then on its meter shows what you spent against what is **available**: its amount plus what rolled over into the month. The carry is said beneath the meter: "+$120.00 rolled over", or "$45.00 overspent, carried over". An overspent carry is kept, not forgiven: what is available can be less than the budget, or nothing at all, and the meter is then full and over until the months after climb out of it. Nothing available is said in words, never as a limit of zero or below: "$37.50 spent, nothing left", with how far over the month began ("Began the month $162.37 over"), and the same in the month's total, in a group's limit line and in Home's alert ("Over your Restaurants budget: overspending carried over left it $162.37 over before this month"). It is tagged over once something is spent, or when the month began over.

- **The month it starts** carries nothing yet: what is left of that month rolls into the next.
- **Reset carryover** sets the carry to zero from this month, once you confirm: this month starts from its budget alone. Earlier months keep theirs.
- **A refund** counts as money in, never off a budget's spending, as in the month's totals. So a purchase you returned stays in the carry, as overspending, in every month after it: a monthly budget only ever showed it for the one month. Reset the carry to clear it; the reset's confirmation says so.
- **Turn off rollover** drops its carry to zero at once, from this month; earlier months keep theirs. Turning it on again starts afresh from the month you choose (this month by default), so an earlier carry never comes back on its own. Choosing the month it ran from brings that carry back exactly, worked out again from the transactions, and for the rest of the month the editor says which month that is ("Turn it on again from March to bring its carry back").
- **Removing a budget** ends its rollover; adding it again starts without one.
- **Changing a budget's amount** changes it from this month on. The months before keep the amount they had, so their carry doesn't change. Nya keeps each budget's amount by month from the first time the app is opened after this release; for a month before that it uses the earliest amount it has, and says so ("Nya began keeping budgets by month in June 2026, so before then each budget counts its amount in June 2026").
- **A month without the budget** (before it was added, or while it was removed) ends the carry: it starts again from zero in the next month with the budget, and the editor says from when ("Rolling over since May, the first month with this budget since rollover began").
- **The first month with transactions** is the earliest a carry counts from: before it nothing is known, and a budget would otherwise roll over in full. A start before it counts from it, and says so ("Rolling over since March 2025, the first month with transactions"). Transactions imported from files count for it as the bank's do, so a file reaching years back lets a carry count from then.

A group's own budget can roll over too: what is left of the group's amount after everything spent in the group, by the categories in it now, so moving a category to another group moves its past spending, and both groups' carries, with it. The rule a group's limit follows ([Budgets on categories and groups](#budgets-on-categories-and-groups)) then works on what is available: the group's own amount plus its carry, against its categories' amounts plus theirs, added up. A group without an amount of its own rolls up what its categories have available. The month's total counts each group once, at its limit, so no carry is counted twice.

Merging a category moves its rollover with its budget, and adds its past amounts to the other's month by month, since their spending is now counted together. Deleting a category or group drops what was kept of it. An archived category's budget rolls over as any other.

### When a month may be incomplete

A carry is only as good as the months it counts. When one of them may be missing transactions, the carry says it may be off and which months ("+$120.00 rolled over (may be off: March is incomplete)"), and a note under the budgets says why, and for which months: a connection that stopped syncing before the month ended, a bank whose transactions couldn't be loaded or whose older ones are still importing, a bank whose stored history begins after the month began, a bank account or card whose transactions Plaid doesn't provide or you didn't allow, a manual account whose transactions couldn't be read, or transactions whose exclusion couldn't be read. An account's end is never one of them: you said nothing of it counts from that day. And a connection whose every account has ended leaves no month short from the month that day begins, however it is doing (a connection to a closed card that stopped syncing, say). Files you [imported into a linked account](#importing-history-into-a-linked-account) stand for the bank's own where it has none, as on a [report](#reports): a month before its stored history begins, at a bank whose transactions Plaid doesn't provide or you didn't allow, or at one that hasn't synced yet, is complete once files cover the whole month for every one of its bank accounts and cards (an account that ended only up to its end). A connection that stopped syncing after it had synced is still said to have stopped, whatever files cover. As in a month's own totals, a transaction in another currency than the budgets' is left out of what rolls over, and named under the budgets ("1 transaction in EUR isn't in what rolled over from earlier months, which is in USD").

### Looking back at a month

The month under the budgets' heading picks a past month, back to the first with transactions, ten years at most: its budgets as they were then, its spending, what rolled into each, and its own notes. Past months are read from everything Nya has stored, not only the year the dashboard loads. They are shown as they were, never edited. A month that can't be loaded says so, with **Try again**.

### Income carryover

For income that doesn't arrive every month (a freelancer's, a contractor's), **My income is irregular**, at the foot of the budgets, keeps a running pool: what is left of each month's income carries into the next. It is off by default, and off it changes nothing at all. Turned on, counting from this month or an earlier one, the Budgets tab shows **Available to allocate** above the meters: last month's leftover, plus this month's income so far, less this month's spending, with how it built up month by month (**Show how it built up**). Budgets work exactly as before: the pool answers whether you can afford something at all, the meters what each budget has left. It is not envelope budgeting: no money has to be assigned. Turning it off keeps the month it counted from, which is offered again.

Income is money in and spending money out, as the Activity tab totals a month: in the one currency the totals are in, without what you excluded, and with transfers between your own accounts, cash withdrawals and loan payments counted as neither, so moving money between your accounts never inflates the pool. A refund or a return is money in, as the Activity tab counts it, never taken off spending, just as a budget's meter doesn't go down when one arrives. The pool at the start of a month is exactly the months' net flows (money in less money out) added up, to the cent. It counts from the first month with transactions at the earliest, and says when a month it counts may be incomplete.

### Where it shows

`/api/v1/budgets` and the MCP tool `get_budgets` answer each budget's amount in the month asked about, what rolled over into it, what is available, the months it counts that may be incomplete, and the income pool when it is on, from the same read and the same rules as the Budgets tab, so the two agree ([The API and the MCP server](#the-api-and-the-mcp-server)). Their notes, and the MCP tool's sentence, say when the month asked about may be incomplete, and why, or comes before any transaction stored, so nothing is counted as spent in it, and when a carry or the pool counts a month that may be incomplete; a bank account or card whose transactions Plaid doesn't provide or you didn't allow is named only when your files don't cover the month for every one of its connection's bank accounts and cards, as under the Budgets tab's month. A month still to come has its budgets and nothing rolled into it yet: nothing is counted past this month. Home's budget alerts weigh what is available, as the meters do. The [download](data-export.md) has each budget's rollover into the month it is made, and the settings themselves (`budget-months`, `income-carryover`). Budgets aren't part of sharing, which shares accounts ([sharing](authentication.md#sharing-between-people)), so none of this is shared.

## Splitting a transaction

One Costco charge can be groceries, household things and a tire. Tap the transaction on the Activity tab, then **Split**: give each part an amount and a category (the picker every transaction uses), and a note if you like. The sheet says how much is left to split as you type, **Put the rest here** puts it on a part, and **Save split** stays off until the parts add up to the transaction exactly, to the cent (the server checks it again): to the currency's own unit, whole yen for yen, and eight places for a currency without an ISO code, such as a cryptocurrency. Amounts are typed without a sign: the parts of money out are money out, and the parts of a refund are money in. A split changed on another device while the sheet was open isn't saved over: the sheet says so, and opening it again shows the change.

- **In the list** the transaction stays one row, as the bank has it, with "Split into 3" where its category was and its own amount shown quieter, and its parts beneath it, each with its category and amount. A search that finds one part lists the transaction with all of them. A tap on a part changes its category, or leaves that part alone out of budgets and reports while the rest count; **Edit split** changes the parts, and **Unsplit**, after asking, puts the transaction back whole under its own category, exactly as it was. The Budgets calendar lists it once too, as one charge, "split into 3".
- **Every total counts the parts**, never the transaction: the month's money in, out and net, top spending, the [cash flow](#cash-flow), the budgets, Home's insights, spending by tag, the Plan's spending, reports, and the API's spending and categories. A split never changes a month's total, only where it goes. A part has its transaction's day, merchant (as shown, after renames and rules), account and currency. Wherever transactions are counted (the Activity tab's list, a report, the Plan, the API's counts), a split one is one transaction, in all and in each of its categories, never one per part.
- **What still sees one transaction**: recurring-bill detection (a monthly bill you split is the same bill, at its whole amount), Home's biggest purchase of the month, the API's list of transactions (each once, its parts in `splits`), an OFX statement (one transaction, its parts in the memo when they fit), matching a pending charge to its posted one, and a share.
- **The transaction's own settings reach its parts.** Excluding it, or a rule hiding it, leaves every part out. Its tags are on every part, so spending by tag counts each part once and the transaction in all. Its note stays on the transaction (each part has a note of its own). A rule's category never lands on a part, whose category you chose; a rule's name is the parts' name too. A rule's conditions read the transaction, never its parts: a rule on a category matches the category the transaction is under before any rule, not a part's, so "category is groceries" doesn't match the groceries part of a Costco run, and what the rule does (its tags, a hide) reaches every part. A part filed under a category since deleted (on another device, say) is uncategorized, with a note, until you edit the split, and one since merged is filed where it went.
- **When the bank changes the amount** (a tip added, part of it refunded), the difference becomes a part of its own, under the transaction's category, marked "The bank changed this amount since you split it", so every total stays the bank's; the split sheet shows it as left to split until you place it. Editing the amount of one you entered or imported does the same ("This amount changed since you split it"). A transaction [imported into a linked account](#when-the-bank-sends-the-same-transactions) that you split while the bank's transaction stood for it adds up to the bank's amount; if the bank then removes its transaction, the imported one counts at the file's amount again, its difference a part of its own, marked "You split this at the amount of the bank's transaction that stood for it", until you edit the split. Until you edit the split, its parts count as you made them: when the amount went down, or turned into money in, the difference is money in, so a budget can show more spent than the bank's figure. The Budgets tab says so while one of the month's split transactions is like that.
- **Refused:** a pending transaction (the bank gives the posted one a new id: split it once it posts), and one in a [group](#grouping-transactions), which counts it whole. A bank's transaction that stands for one you [imported from a file](#when-the-bank-sends-the-same-transactions) shows that one's split, or counts in its group: a split of its own would hide them, so it is refused, saying to change that split (**Edit split** does) or to take it out of the group first.
- **Any transaction**: a bank's, one entered by hand or imported into a manual account, one SimpleFIN brought, or one [imported into a linked account](#importing-history-into-a-linked-account) from its bank's files. When the bank later sends one you imported, its split carries to the bank's row, as what you said about it does ([When the bank sends the same transactions](#when-the-bank-sends-the-same-transactions)): counted once, as the parts, and edited from the bank's row.
- **Gone with its transaction.** If the bank removes the transaction, its split goes once the next sync saves the removal, as one you delete, or an import you undo, takes its own, and forgetting the earlier account a file was imported into takes those of its imported rows; nothing goes while a store of transactions can't be read. Splits don't carry across a [re-link](#removing-an-institution-and-adding-it-back), but for those on rows imported from files, which stay with them as the rows do.
- **Beside the rest.** A split transaction on an account that [ended](#accounts-that-end), dated from its end, is left out with the account's other rows, parts and all, and a report counts it once among what it leaves out. A [goal](#savings-goals) counts what went into an account, so a split transaction counts once there, its parts adding up to it. [Rollover](#budget-rollover-and-income-carryover) counts each part in its own category, so splitting a transaction in a past month changes a carry at once.
- **When something can't be read.** A split that can't be read (or that a later version of Nya saved) shows its transaction whole, with a note, and nothing saves over it; one whose transaction is in another currency now shows it whole too, until you split it again.
- **Categories.** A category a part is filed under is in use: it can't be deleted until the part moves, and merging it moves the part with everything else.
- **Private.** Sharing never shows a part, its note or its category: what someone sees of an account you share is each transaction as the bank has it.

Limits: 2 to 20 parts, 5,000 split transactions, and a part's note of 500 characters.

## Grouping transactions

Some transactions belong together: a $120 dinner and the $60 a friend paid you back are a $60 dinner, and a work trip and its reimbursement come to nothing. Tap one on the Activity tab, then **Group with others**: the sheet offers your other posted transactions in its currency, nearest in date first, with a search (one you ticked stays listed while the search hides it). Tick the ones that belong, give the group a name and a category, and **Group them**; the sheet says what they come to before you do. Until you pick a category, it is the one of the largest money out among those ticked that isn't a transfer (the dinner's, not the payback's, which a bank files as a transfer in), and a transfer category only when every one is a transfer; a group under a transfer category counts in no total, and the sheet says so.

- **In the list** the group is one row, on its earliest transaction's day and account, saying "Group of 2" and its category, at its net, with its transactions beneath it, each at its own amount. Its category is changed from its row as a transaction's is. **Edit group** renames it, refiles it, and adds transactions or takes them out (a tap on one beneath it takes it out too), and saves only a change; **Ungroup**, after asking, puts them back on their own, each under its own category, exactly as before.
- **Every total counts the net once**, in the group's category, on the group's day: $60 of restaurants, never $120 out and $60 in (the [cash flow](#cash-flow) draws no payer for the $60). The API lists each of its transactions with the group beside it (`transaction_group`, with its `net`); recurring-bill detection reads each on its own, and an OFX statement lists each, its memo naming the group.
- **What its transactions' settings do.** An exclusion, a rule's hide or a rule's category on one of them doesn't change the net: grouping them says how they count, and the sheet and the group's list say so beside one you excluded or a rule hides. The group carries their tags, so spending by tag counts its net under each. Where transactions are counted, a group is as many as it holds.
- **Refused:** fewer than two transactions, or more than 50; a pending one; a split one (its parts count on their own, so a group would count it twice: unsplit it first) or a part; one in another group; transactions in different currencies (a net of two currencies is no amount); and transactions more than a year apart. A bank's transaction that stands for one you imported from a file is split, or in a group, as that one is, and refused as such.
- **A transaction that goes** (the bank removed it, you deleted it, its institution was disconnected, the import that brought it was undone) leaves the group, and the group says one isn't there any more, and why where Nya knows: one you imported from a file whose bank transaction is still pending "is pending at the bank, so it counts on its own until it posts", and one that counts in another group names it; one on an account you hide stays in it, left out of its net while the account is hidden, and said the same way. A group with none of its transactions left goes. A group counts on its day: once that day is older than the year the Activity tab reads, the group counts nowhere in it, as a report for a later period counts none of it, never at part of its net. Its transactions still inside the year are listed, each saying which group it is in and the day the group counts on, with **Ungroup**, its amount dimmed as an excluded one's is, and count in no total (the API gives their group with a `net` of `null`).
- **When its transactions' currencies differ** (one you entered has another currency now), the group no longer applies: its transactions count on their own, each saying which group it is in, with **Ungroup**.
- **Beside the rest.** One on an account that [ended](#accounts-that-end), dated from its end, is left out of the group's net, as the account's other rows are left out, and the group says one of its transactions isn't there; a report says so too. A [goal](#savings-goals) counts each of a group's transactions on its own account at its own amount (the payback into checking, never the group's net on the card). [Rollover](#budget-rollover-and-income-carryover) and the income pool count the group's net in its category, so grouping in a past month changes a carry at once. One [imported into a linked account](#importing-history-into-a-linked-account) that the bank later sends counts through the bank's row, which takes its place in the group, unless the bank's is in a group of its own: then the group says one of its transactions isn't there.
- **Private.** Sharing never shows a group or its name: each transaction is shown as the bank has it.

Limits: 2 to 50 transactions in a group, 2,000 groups, and a name of 60 characters.

**In the API and the download.** The [API](#the-api-and-the-mcp-server) gives a split transaction's parts in `splits` and a grouped one's group in `transaction_group`. The [download](data-export.md) has both, as `transaction-splits` and `transaction-groups`; its transactions CSV lists every transaction once with its own `amount`, and its parts and a group's net as rows of their own, with `nya_amount` the amount the app counts, so adding up either column counts nothing twice.

## Recurring bills and the cash forecast

The Budgets tab finds the bills and income that repeat, says when each is next expected, and carries your checking and savings forward over the next 30, 60 or 90 days, with a calendar of what posted and what is coming. Home's upcoming bills come from the same detection, which the dashboard runs once for both.

### What counts as recurring

A charge or a deposit repeats on one of these cadences: weekly, every 2 weeks, twice a month (two days of the month, like the 1st and the 15th), every 4 weeks, monthly, every 2 months, every 3 months, twice a year and yearly. Each cadence is a schedule: a fixed step in days, or one or two days of the month in every first, second, third, sixth or twelfth month. Deposits are found the same way as charges, so payroll every other Friday or on the 15th and the last day shows as income, apart from the bills.

Charges are grouped by account (its name at its institution), merchant and currency: the same subscription on two cards is two series, even at one bank, and no amount adds up two currencies. A merchant's charges are recurring on a cadence when they fit its schedule (`lib/recurring.ts`):

- **On time, within a few days.** A charge may land up to 2 days from its scheduled date for weekly, 3 for every 2 or 4 weeks and twice a month, 4 for monthly, and up to 14 for yearly, since weekends and holidays move a bank's posting and a yearly renewal drifts a little.
- **Most of them fit.** At least 75% of the merchant's charges fit, one per scheduled date, and at least 75% of the scheduled dates between the first and the last have one. A gym that also sells smoothies still has its membership found, and a skipped month is allowed; a store visited on no schedule fits nothing.
- **Their amounts agree, with the evidence that takes.** The *same amount* (the largest and smallest of the last year's charges within 2% of their median, or 50 cents: a subscription's price, a rent) needs four charges for weekly and twice a month and three for the rest, and at those fewest they must be exactly the same, to the cent. A *similar amount* (within 25%, or 5 units of the currency: a utility bill, a phone bill with its taxes) needs one charge more, since a shop's spend can be similar by chance. The one charge farthest out is forgiven among five or more (a prorated first bill, a bonus paycheck). This is what keeps a grocery store you visit every week from being called a bill: its timing may be weekly, its amounts are not.
- **Twice a year and yearly** need the same amount and three charges, or two at exactly the same amount with nothing else from the merchant in between, at a merchant that isn't a restaurant, a food shop or a way of getting about: two visits a year apart to a restaurant are not a bill. A renewal whose price moves each time (a six-month car policy, a domain) is found from three charges at similar amounts, in a category known to bill (not food, transport or general shopping), at its latest price.

Evidence is taken from a window before the merchant's latest charge: half a year for weekly, a year for most cadences, longer for twice a year and yearly. When a due date moves (from the 5th to the 20th, say) or a payroll changes cadence, the charges since the change are judged on their own, but only if those before it were a series by the same rules, so a restaurant visited at random whose last few visits happen to line up is not a bill.

A merchant billing several things under one name (Apple's iCloud and a music plan, Amazon's Prime among your orders) is split by amount, and each run of exactly one amount, to the cent, is judged on its own: a subscription repeats its price exactly, a store's trips almost never do. Such a run needs four charges or more, and is never twice a year or yearly, since at a merchant you buy from often a few charges that line up are what chance gives. Measured on simulated accounts (four hundred restaurants or shops visited a few times each, a household's daily shopping, a very busy account of fifteen charges a day), this took the bills found where there were none from as many as 28 in an account to none, or one in eight accounts at most, while every real subscription in them is still found (`test/recurring-phantoms.test.ts`).

**A price change** keeps the series going. Charges at one amount and then, on the same schedule, at another (a promotion ending, a plan upgraded) are one series at the new amount from its second charge, and the card says what it was before. Until that second charge, a charge at a new amount still counts for its date, so a bill that came is never "not in yet" and never counted twice.

**Pay that varies** (an hourly wage) is found when it keeps its schedule closely, six deposits or more on nearly every date: its amounts may then vary more, and its typical amount is the average of the last half year (leaving out any far from the rest, such as a bonus), which moves little from one paycheck to the next and comes to what was paid even when two amounts alternate. A raise to a new fixed amount is followed as a price change is. Pay that keeps its schedule but varies more than that (its largest and smallest more than 60% of the median apart, once a tenth of them at the ends are left out) is listed as varying, kept out of the forecast, and named there. Two deposits a date from one payer, such as two people paid by one employer into a joint account, are split by amount and each found as pay; when one of them stops, it ends, and never takes over the other's deposits.

**Early, extra and pending charges.** A charge at the series' same amount paid early, up to half a period ahead (and never more than a month), stands for the date it was paid for, so rent paid ten days early isn't expected again on top of itself. An extra charge from the same merchant at another amount (a repair fee beside a water bill) is not taken for the next bill. A charge still pending counts for its date when its amount is near the bill's, as the forecast takes it off the balance it starts from; a smoothie pending at the gym is not the membership. A deposit still pending doesn't count, as the forecast doesn't add it. A charge one series of a merchant kept is never taken by another, so when a phone line is cancelled its series ends, rather than take over the line that goes on, and a new price goes to the series whose amount it is nearest.

What never counts: money moved between your accounts, cash taken out, a transaction you [excluded](#excluding-a-transaction), and money in on a card or a loan (a payment received, a refund, however an imported file names it), which is never income. Loan payments and bank fees are bills, as before. A payment that pays a card off, by Plaid's detail or because the card's side shows it received at its amount within a few days (so imported files are matched too), is marked with the card it pays, and isn't in the monthly total of bills, where the card's own bills already are. Only a loan payment, or a row an imported file gives no category, is matched so: a bill in another category (an HOA's 200) is never taken for a card's payment, however its amount matches.

Each series shows its account, cadence, how many times it was seen, its typical amount and the next date its schedule names. A bill on the 31st is expected on the 30th of a 30-day month and the 28th of February (the 29th in a leap year), and on the 31st again after; a yearly charge on February 29th falls on the 28th in other years. One that is late but within its days is "not in yet" and expected today; past them, that date is taken as skipped. Two scheduled dates gone by with nothing arriving (one, for cadences longer than a month) and the series "may have ended": it is listed last and left out of the monthly figures, the forecast and the calendar.

A yearly charge is seen twice only in two years, and the Activity tab loads one. So `/api/transactions` also sends, beside the year, the rows from up to 800 days back that a yearly or twice-yearly charge needs (`recurring_history`): from merchants charged this year only once or twice, at an amount charged this year too, at most three times before, compact, with your categories, renames, exclusions and hidden accounts applied as to every row. A wider set is cached with Plaid's part, and each answer narrows it once your exclusions are applied, so an excluded row never keeps the others from being sent. Only recurring detection reads them, in the app and in the API's recurring bills (`lib/activity.ts`).

The earlier detection found roughly monthly charges only, at a consistent amount over three months, with no check of their timing. The monthly bills it found are still found, with their cadence now. Charges at no regular interval (a copay a few times a year) were listed then and aren't now, and a bill whose due date moved is judged on its new date once it has charged there a few times.

Tap a series for **Not recurring**: it leaves the list, the forecast, the calendar and Home's upcoming bills. Those marked are listed under the rest, where **Restore** brings each back. A dismissal is saved as the series was (its account, merchant and amount) and applies to the series of that account and merchant nearest that amount, or the price it had before a change. Where one merchant charges two subscriptions, told apart by their amounts (iCloud and Apple Music), it applies only at exactly that amount, or that price before a change; otherwise within 25%, or 50 cents. Two paychecks from one payer on the same day (two people paid into one joint account) are told apart by amount too, the lower and the higher, but pay moves a little each payday: a dismissed one holds for its own side while the amount saved is within its own deposits, lowest to highest, and never reaches the other person's, even after raises bring their pay to that amount. One raised past what was saved is listed again, to dismiss again. So it holds as new charges come in and a bill's amount moves, and it never moves to another subscription from the same merchant (iCloud dismissed is not Apple Music dismissed, nor Apple Arcade later). The buttons wait for your planned items to load, and the card says so when they couldn't be.

### Planned items

Expenses and income you know are coming (a car registration, a tax refund, rent from a room you let) are added under **Planned** on the Budgets tab: a name, money out or in, the amount and its currency, the date, and whether it repeats (weekly, every 2 weeks, monthly, every 3 months, every 6 months or yearly). Twice a month is two monthly items, so neither date is a guess. A monthly item on the 31st falls on each shorter month's last day. Planned items count in the forecast and the calendar only: budgets, totals and history don't change.

They are kept with the series marked not recurring and the forecast's warning, as one encrypted value per account on the storage seam (`lib/planned-store.ts`, `app/api/planned-items`), saved whole like budgets and goals, and checked field by field on every save: at most 100 items, each with a name of one line up to 60 characters, a positive amount in its currency's smallest unit, a known three-letter currency and a real date between 2000 and 2100. They are in the [download of your data](data-export.md#planned-items).

### The cash forecast

The forecast counts only money that leaves or reaches your cash accounts: checking, savings and cash on hand (Plaid's depository accounts, and manual ones of that type), not hidden ones. It starts from their balance as the Accounts tab shows it, less the charges still pending on them (a bank's balance leaves those out, but the money is spent; a pending deposit isn't added, and a transfer between two of them pending on both sides moves nothing), and says so: "Starts from $X: $Y in Checking at Chase, less $Z still pending." It says when the balances are from, and when they are the ones this device saved on an earlier visit, shown until a load replaces them, it says that too: anything since isn't in it. It is in one currency, the one most of those accounts are in; accounts in another, and any without a balance, are left out and named, and so is a charge pending in another currency (with a [home currency](#a-home-currency), one with no rate), which isn't taken off.

From there it moves day by day through each detected bill (out) and income (in) on a cash account, on the dates its schedule names, and each planned item, through today and the next 30, 60 or 90 days, in whole minor units of its currency (cents, yen, fils). A card's own charges aren't in it: a subscription billed to a card leaves your checking only inside the card's payment, so counting both would take it out twice. The card's payment from checking counts when it is a detected bill (a steady autopay); one that changes every month isn't in the forecast, and for a card whose payment falls due within the forecast, the notes name it with its due date and what is left of its statement, from Plaid's payment details, so you can add it as a planned expense. They don't when the statement is paid already (a payment made on or after the day it was issued, in full), when you have planned an expense near that amount within a few days of the due date, or when a steady card payment Nya can't tie to a card is near that amount, as it may be that card's. A planned item says nothing of which account it is paid from or for, so only one whose name names the card itself counts as its minimum payment or as part of its payment: a word of the card's own name that tells it apart beside a word that says it is the card or its payment ("Sapphire payment" for Chase Sapphire Preferred, "Delta card", "Costco Visa payment"; never "card" or "preferred" alone), its last four digits ("Card 4821"), or its bank's name with "card" ("Chase card"). The card's word alone isn't enough, as it is often a merchant's or a payee's ("Costco", "Delta Dental", "Southwest Gas"), nor is the bank's name alone, as it names the bank's loans and savings too, and a $45 gym is not a $40 minimum. When what such items come to within a few days of the due date (each time one falls there) is within a quarter of what is owed, the note isn't said; when it is less, the note says what you planned may be part of it, and gives the rest as what isn't in the forecast. Other bills planned that week, rent or a phone bill, change nothing. On a day with money both out and in, the money out is counted first, since which comes first isn't known, and the chart draws that dip. It gives the lowest point and its day, and warns in red when the balance is expected to drop below zero, or in amber below a figure you set. That figure starts at 100 in the forecast's currency, the figure Home's low-balance alert uses, and is saved with its currency, so if most of your cash comes to be in another currency the forecast says so rather than read the old figure as the new currency's (one saved before currencies were kept reads in the forecast's).

It is an estimate, drawn dashed and labelled so, and every date in it is an expectation ("around Oct 15"). Everyday spending isn't in it, nor money moved between your accounts: between two cash accounts it nets out anyway, but a regular transfer to a brokerage doesn't lower the forecast, so add it as a planned item if it matters. It says what it may be missing, as the Home total does: balances recovered from an earlier day, an institution that couldn't be reached, checking or savings a bank couldn't show or that stopped reporting (named when the bank remembers them), a connection that stopped syncing or whose transactions couldn't be loaded or are still arriving, a bank whose transactions Plaid doesn't provide or you didn't allow, pay that stopped coming or varies too much to forecast, a series on an account whose type isn't known, a typed balance not updated for a week, and anything left out for its currency. When transactions couldn't be loaded at all it says that, rather than show a forecast with nothing coming. It is worked out in the browser from what the dashboard already loaded, and never stored or sent anywhere: it never becomes history. Day zero is your own day, not the server's or UTC's.

**What if I buy…** asks about one purchase: an amount and a day within the forecast's range. The drawer shows the new expected lowest point beside the old one, whether the balance would drop below zero or the warning, and the forecast with and without the purchase. Nothing is saved.

The card ends with a link to the [runway](#runway), which carries the same cash further, with your everyday spending in it: how long it would last with your income and without it.

### The calendar

A month a page, Sunday first, from a year back to a year ahead. Each day carries a figure and a dot for each kind of thing on it; in the cell the figure is in whole units, a thousand and up shortened ("-$143", "+$3.5K"), so it fits a phone's narrow column, and the day's list has the cents. Days gone by show what posted: money in less money out, as the Activity tab's day heading counts it (every transaction but those you excluded, in the totals' currency). From today on they show what is expected, counted as the forecast counts it: bills and income on your cash accounts, and planned items. A card's own charges, pay that varies too much to forecast and a series on an account of unknown type are listed on their day, with why, but not added. The two figures are never added together, since one is the bank's record and the other an estimate. A card's or loan's payment due is marked on its day with its minimum, from Plaid's payment details, but never added: it may already be a detected bill, and what you will pay is yours to decide. Tap a day for its list. While transactions load, or when they couldn't be loaded, the days gone by say so instead of "Nothing posted".

## Cash flow

The **Cash flow** card at the top of the Activity tab draws where money came from and where it went, as a Sankey diagram: who paid you on the left, flowing into everything counted, which flows out to your category groups and on to their categories, with what was left over beside the groups. Tap a part of it to list its transactions.

- **Periods.** This month, last month, the last 3 months, the year to date and the last 12 months, each made of whole months on your own calendar (this month runs to its last day, as the Activity tab counts a month), so each month in it is the Activity tab's month, to the cent. The card names the months. The Activity tab loads the last 365 days of transactions; a period that reaches before them says its first days aren't in it, and one that starts before the earliest transaction there is, when that is more than a week into those 365 days (your history itself begins later, not a quiet few days), says it may be missing what came before. A transaction you imported from files is one there is, so months only your files reach don't begin late.
- **The month's own figures.** The chart is built from the rows the Activity tab totals, by the rule the month's totals and the budgets follow: in one currency, the one most of your transactions are in, with transfers between your accounts, cash withdrawals and loan payments left out, what you excluded and what a rule of yours hides left out, and transactions in another currency named, never converted. So its money in is the Activity tab's money in for those months, and its spending by category is the Budgets tab's and the API's. The card says what it left out, as the month's totals do. Transactions [imported into a linked account](#importing-history-into-a-linked-account) from files (your bank's, or another app's export) count as the bank's own do, each once: a deposit's payer is its name as the list shows it, a purchase is in the category it is filed under, and one the bank has sent since counts as the bank's, with the category and exclusion you gave the imported one ([When the bank sends the same transactions](#when-the-bank-sends-the-same-transactions)). The card says how many of the period's transactions were imported, as each month's totals say it of the month.
- **Money in, by who paid it.** A payer is the name on its transactions (the merchant's, or your name for it), the same whatever its capitals. Plaid has no payroll code (its transaction codes are kinds of payment, such as a transfer or a purchase), so pay is told apart by who paid it: one employer is one source, however its deposits are filed. The largest payers are drawn on their own, each at least 3% of the period's flow, six at most (or seven, when the seventh would be the only one left to fold); the rest are folded into **Other income**. A refund counts as money in, as in the month's totals and the budgets, never as less spending (the Plan's spending, which takes refunds off, is the exception): a shop that refunded you is among the payers, marked a refund in the table when its transactions are filed under a spending category. Nothing is drawn as a source that isn't on your transactions.
- **Money out, by group and category.** Each group you spent in (Food, Housing...), then its categories, filed as the Budgets tab files them. A group's categories under 3% of the flow are folded into **Other**, in that group; a group under 3%, or whose categories would all fold, ends at the group. The groups' kinds decide what counts: a transfer group's transactions are never money out, and money out filed under an income category (a paycheck taken back) counts, under its group, as it does in the totals.
- **Split and grouped transactions.** A [split](#splitting-a-transaction) transaction flows as its parts, each to its own category, so one Costco charge can feed Food and Housing, and what the bank changed its amount by since is a part of its own (more is spending in the transaction's category, less is money back from its payer). A [group](#grouping-transactions) flows once, at its net, under its name and its category: the $60 a friend paid back for dinner is no payer, and the dinner is $60 of restaurants; a group that comes to money in is a payer of its name (a refund, in a spending category), and one that comes to nothing draws nothing. Every count is of transactions, as the totals' are: a split one is one however many nodes its parts reach, on the table's totals and in a fold too, and a group is as many as it holds.
- **More out than in.** A period that spent more than came in draws the difference as **Savings or debt** beside the payers (money already in your accounts, or a card's balance growing), never as a flow running backwards. One that spent less draws **Left over** beside the groups: what came in and wasn't spent, wherever it went (moved to savings, paying off a loan, or still in checking). A period with nothing counted says so instead of an empty chart.
- **What may be missing** is said beside the chart: first what the load couldn't read, as the list says it (a manual account's transactions, your SimpleFIN accounts, your rules or your categories; never your tags or the review queue's settings, which no figure here reads, and which only the list says), then, in the words the month's totals use, a connection whose transactions couldn't be loaded or are still importing, a connection that stopped syncing (with the months of the period it may leave short), a bank account or card whose transactions Plaid doesn't provide or you didn't allow, and transactions from SimpleFIN with no category, which count as spending or income. As under each month's totals, a bank account or card whose transactions Plaid doesn't provide or you didn't allow is named only for the months of the period your files don't cover for every one of its connection's bank accounts and cards, as a [report](#reports) and what [rolls over](#budget-rollover-and-income-carryover) count them; any other connection is named whatever your files cover; and imported transactions that couldn't be read are named, as on the list.
- **Tap to see the transactions.** Tapping a payer, a group, a category or a fold lists its transactions in the Activity list below, from every month of the period (a split transaction whole, with its parts in what you tapped under it, still saying how many parts it has and edited from all of them; a group's row with its transactions), with the search and the tag filter on top and a line saying what the list shows, where focus goes too, and the chart marks what was tapped (for a payer or a category it folds, picked from the table, the node that holds it); tapping it again, **Back to the month**, or choosing a month puts the month back. A reload of your transactions keeps the period and what the list shows. The list is worked out again from your transactions as they are, so one you recategorize or exclude meanwhile leaves it.
- **Without the chart.** To a screen reader the chart is one image, described in a sentence; **Show as a table** under it lists every flow with its amount and how many transactions it sums, each folded payer and category on its own line, and each name is a button that lists its transactions.
- **On a phone.** Inline SVG, no chart library, in the app's own colors: money in and what was left over in green, spending in blue, savings or debt in red. Labels sit beside the thin nodes, shortened to fit (a tall node's on two lines); the table and each node's tooltip have every full name and cent.
- **For programs.** `/api/v1/cash-flow` and the MCP tool `get_cash_flow` answer the same flows, with every payer and category listed, for a month or any dates, and their notes say how many of the transactions were imported from files, as the card does, and as `/api/v1/spending`'s (and `get_spending`'s) do ([The API and the MCP server](#the-api-and-the-mcp-server)). Their notes say what may be missing as the chart does: never your tags (spending's do, since it answers by tag too), and a bank account or card whose transactions Plaid doesn't provide or you didn't allow only when your files don't cover the dates asked for, for every one of its connection's bank accounts and cards; so do the transactions' notes.

## Reports

**Reports**, under the transactions on the Activity tab and under **Manage** on the Accounts tab, opens `/reports`, with three reports to choose from at the top: **For your accountant**, a summary of a tax year or a report on any dates, to print or save as a PDF; the [runway](#runway), how long your cash would last; and [year over year](#year-over-year). `?report=runway` or `?report=year-over-year` in the address opens one. The accountant's report is the offline version of sharing with one: nothing is shared, and you decide who gets the file.

- **Two kinds.** A tax year is a calendar year, from 2000 to this one; this year runs to today, and the report says so. A date range is any days from 2000 to today, at most two years (731 days).
- **What is in it.** Money in and money out by category, each with how many transactions it counts; a table by month; the categories you marked for taxes, as a group of their own, with an appendix listing their transactions (the first 500, oldest first, and a note saying so when there are more; one the totals don't count says why: you excluded it, it is a transfer or loan payment, or it is in another currency); and where the data comes from. It covers bank and card transactions and the ones you entered or imported: activity inside investment accounts (trades, dividends, interest) and inside loans never comes in as transactions, and when an account here holds either, the first page says so.
- **The app's own rules.** A report reads the Activity tab's transactions through the same assembly as the app and the API (`lib/activity.ts`) and totals them with the same functions (`lib/totals.ts`), so a month in a report is the same month on the Activity tab: transfers, cash withdrawals and loan payments (paying a card off among them) never count as money in or out, what you excluded is left out and counted, hidden accounts are left out, and a transaction entered by hand or imported into a manual account counts like a bank's, the appendix saying where each came from, as does one [imported into a linked account](#importing-history-into-a-linked-account) from its bank's files. A [split](#splitting-a-transaction) transaction counts as its parts and a [group](#grouping-transactions) as its net, as on the Activity tab: the appendix lists a part as "Part 1 of 3 of a $150.00 transaction", and a group's net as such. One currency per report, the one most of the period's transactions are in, or another of theirs you pick; the rest are named and left out, never converted. Money in includes a refund, under the category of what was refunded. A report reads as far back as its period, past the Activity tab's year: Nya keeps every transaction it has stored.
- **Never complete without saying so.** The first page says, before any figure, whether anything may be missing and why, in the words the app uses elsewhere: a connection that hasn't synced since a day (a period's transactions count as all in once a connection has synced three days after its last day, since banks post late, or today), one whose transactions couldn't be loaded (one never synced, only from the days its history can reach; one whose stored transactions can't be read, from every day, since it may have been linked before Nya first saw it), one still importing older transactions, one whose transactions in Nya begin after the period starts, or, with none stored, whose history can go back only to a day after the period starts (a link brings at most two years before it was first seen; either way, if its accounts were open before then, the earlier ones aren't there; never said of a connection whose every bank account and card you hid (an investment account or a loan beside them brings no transactions), none of whose transactions a report reads), a bank account or card whose transactions Plaid doesn't provide or you didn't allow, an account a connection stopped reporting (if it is still open, its later transactions aren't there), a connection removed since, whether a connection was removed when that couldn't be read, your own categories or the exclusions you carried across a reconnected account when they couldn't be read (the totals then use the bank's categories, or may count what you excluded), and any other transactions that couldn't be read (a manual account's, say). What couldn't be read of how your rules, categories, splits, groups, merchants' defaults or approvals apply leaves no transaction out, so it is said among the report's caveats, in the words the Activity tab uses, and makes no month incomplete. A removed connection's transactions went with it unless every one of its accounts is back: linked by you to an account connected now, or matched to one at the same institution by its last four digits and type; one that isn't is named, so a second login at the same bank, or the same login added back with fewer accounts, never passes for the one removed. Each month says which connections it may be missing (a sync, or an account last seen, within three days after a month ends still leaves it short), and every table of figures says they may be incomplete. A period with no transactions says so rather than showing zeros. The data's as-of time is the oldest of the connections' last syncs, and is said to be not known when one of them couldn't be loaded or doesn't say. The first page also lists every connection with its state, when its transactions were last brought in and how far back they reach, each manual account with its transactions in the period (or that they couldn't be read) and when its balance was last updated (a manual account's transactions are what you entered or imported, so Nya can't tell whether any are missing), and the connections removed since, with the accounts not back. A connection that holds only investment accounts, or no bank account or card, is listed with what that leaves out, which is not a gap.
- **History imported from files.** A period before a connection's own transactions begin is covered where files you imported into its accounts reach ([Importing history into a linked account](#importing-history-into-a-linked-account)): its transactions in the period are the files' rows, and if the files cover every one of its bank accounts and cards for the whole period, nothing is missing from it. Where they cover only some, the gap names those accounts and how far back each one's files reach, and says when the rest begin ("Chase's transactions in Nya begin on Oct 10, 2024 (Checking's on Jan 1, 2019, imported from its files)"); where every account's files reach back, but not to the period's start, it says that day ("begin, with those imported from its files, on ..."). A file with lines that couldn't be read covers its period only when you said those lines aren't transactions, as for the estimated history. A bank whose transactions Plaid doesn't provide, or you didn't allow, or that never synced, is short of nothing in a period your files cover for every one of its bank accounts and cards. An account that [ended](#accounts-that-end) needs its files only up to its end, whether or not its bank still returns it, and one that ended before the period none of it. Where the data comes from says how far back each account's files reach.
- **Accounts that ended.** An account that [ended](#accounts-that-end) counts as it does on the Activity tab: on every day before its end, and on none from it. Each one whose end the period has something to say of is listed with the sources: closed, no longer yours or not followed, from its day, through which day it is counted, and how many of its transactions in the period are dated from then, in Nya and left out (the totals' note counts them too). That is any whose end falls in the period, and, from before it, one only assumed closed, one you stopped following, or one with transactions in it. Closed and no longer yours are never gaps: nothing of yours is missing. An assumed closure is (if the account is still open, its transactions from then aren't there), and so is an account you stopped following, which is still yours, and the days an account was missing from its bank's answer before it came back; none of them for an investment account or a loan, whose own activity is never in a report. A manual account that ended before the period is not one of its sources; a connection whose every account had ended before it is listed as such, and isn't a gap however long ago it last synced; and an account of a removed connection that ended before it had nothing in it to go with the connection, so it is no gap either. Closures that can't be read stop the report, as they stop the Activity tab, rather than count an account you closed.
- **SimpleFIN in a report.** Its rows count as on the Activity tab: a card payment, a loan payment or a move between your own accounts that Nya pairs between two of your SimpleFIN accounts is left out like a bank's, or counted like one once you move its category into a spending group ([transfers and categories](#where-its-accounts-live)), and every other row counts until you categorize it. Each bank a SimpleFIN connection reaches is listed as "My Bank (through SimpleFIN)" (and one a Lunch Flow connection reaches as "My Bank (through Lunch Flow)", the same way), with the server it reaches, its state in the words of its card and when it last synced cleanly. What may be missing is said as for any connection: older history still arriving (only when the period starts before what has come, and only for those earlier months; once it is all in, where it begins), and a bank that hasn't synced since its last clean sync when a request failed, SimpleFIN reported a problem with it, or the bank's own balance there is days old. An account you stopped tracking or disconnected during the period is said too: removed, its transactions went with it (unless you track it again, when its own history says how far back it reaches); kept as a manual account, from that day its transactions are only the ones you enter or import, and if its connection's history has reached back further since, the report says you stopped tracking it before its transactions from those earlier days came in, as its OFX statement does. Nya keeps each such account's name, bank and dates for this; **Accounts you stopped tracking**, on the Reports page, lists them, and **Forget** deletes one, after which reports no longer mention it (its past balances stay in your net-worth history). A card or a loan waiting for your answer about its sign is named, and is not a gap: that holds its balance in the app, never its transactions. An account that [ended](#accounts-that-end) holds nothing at its bank, so its bank's history moves on when SimpleFIN leaves it out of an answer (a card closed at the bank, say); the days that answer covered may be missing its transactions, until an answer holds it again and brings them, and the report says so for the days it counts on, as days it was missing from its bank's answer. Its OFX statement says so too.
- **Days and times.** A transaction's date is its bank's own day and is never converted. Today, and every time a report shows (when it was made, when each connection last synced), are read in your browser's time zone, which the report names. The CSV writes its times in UTC (ISO 8601), and says so.
- **From what is stored.** A report reads what Nya has stored, as the read-only API does, and never calls Plaid: it costs nothing there, works while a bank or Plaid is down, and says as of when each connection's transactions are. Open Nya first to bring your connections up to date.
- **Printing.** **Print or save as PDF** opens the browser's print window; choose Save as PDF there. The print styles make a clean A4 or Letter page in black and white with no navigation or buttons: the first page holds the period, what may be missing, the bottom line and where the data comes from (on one page with half a dozen connections), and the totals by category, the months, the marked categories and the appendix each start a page of their own, a long table's header repeating on every page. Every page's footer names the report, its period and the page's number. No PDF library is involved. **Download as CSV** gives the same totals, months and notes as one table for a spreadsheet, written like the data download's CSV files, formula guard included (`lib/report/csv.ts`).
- **Categories marked for taxes.** **Mark categories** picks the ones that matter to you, from the categories in the report's period; they show as a group of their own in every report. A mark is kept as the category's words, which a rename never changes, so it follows your [categories](#categories-and-groups): a category you rename stays marked, under its new name, and one you merge into another takes its mark there, with its transactions. Words no category has any more stay marked, matched as Nya files a category (any case, spaces collapsed). The report's totals by category are by your categories too, named as they are now, as the Activity tab's are. Nya never marks a category, suggests one or says how any category is taxed: that is tax advice, and for you and your accountant. Your choice is saved as your own setting (`report-settings`, on the storage seam, and in your data download; see [data-export.md](data-export.md#report-settings)); a setting that can't be read leaves the group out, says so, and is never saved over.

The page asks `GET /api/reports` (`kind=year&year=2025`, or `kind=range&start=2026-01-01&end=2026-03-31`, with `tz`, and optionally `currency` and `format=csv`), which checks every parameter and the period's bounds before reading anything (`lib/report/`). `GET` and `PUT /api/report-settings` read and save the marked categories.

### Runway

How long your cash would last at your recent spending, with your income and without it, the day it would run out, and a chart of the cash going forward. An estimate, labelled as one, with what it is worked out from listed under it and what it can't see said first. The **Cash forecast** on the Budgets tab links to it.

- **What cash is.** Where the [cash forecast](#recurring-bills-and-the-cash-forecast) starts: your checking, savings and cash on hand (bank accounts, and manual accounts of that kind), not hidden and not ended, at their newest balances, less the charges still pending on them, in one currency (the one most of them are in). Never an investment account, the cash inside one included (a brokerage's settlement or money market fund is money waiting to be invested, days from being spent, and the page says it isn't counted), never a health savings account (money for health costs, named on the page as not counted), and never a card or a loan. What your cards owe is said beside it: their charges are in your spending as you make them, but paying them off comes out of this cash, which the runway doesn't take off. With no checking, savings or cash on hand in Nya there is nothing to start from, and the runway says so instead of a figure.
- **What spending is.** The money out the Activity tab counts, over the last 3, 6 (the default) or 12 full calendar months before this one (this month is never whole yet): transfers between your accounts, cash withdrawals, loan and card payments and what you [excluded](#excluding-a-transaction) aren't in it, and transactions in another currency are left out and named. Then what a runway must see that the Activity tab rightly doesn't, each listed under the figures:
  - **Refunds** (money in under a spending category, such as a return) lower the spending, and aren't income: they don't stop when pay does. Income is the rest of the money in: pay and other money in.
  - **Payments to a card Nya doesn't see** are spending. A payment from your checking or savings that the bank marks as a card's payment, and that no card in Nya shows arriving within 5 days at its amount, pays for charges Nya never saw, so it counts, and the page names it. If that card is in Nya after all (under another login, say), it is counted twice, and the page says that too. A payment that lands on a card in Nya stays out: that card's charges are in the spending already. Each payment the card shows (pending ones too, never a refund) is the other side of one payment only, the nearest in days. A payment whose receipt isn't there is still that card's when it repeats payments that have been landing on a card in Nya: a missed month, or one paid in the last days whose receipt isn't in yet.
  - **Bills less often than monthly** (every two months, quarterly, twice a year, yearly, such as car insurance) are taken out of the average and added on their dates instead, as the cash forecast adds them, and listed: averaged, a yearly bill would weigh nothing in a window it didn't fall in, and four times its weight in a three-month window it did.
  - **Transfers out and cash taken out** (rent paid by Zelle, a move to an account Nya doesn't hold, an ATM withdrawal) aren't spending on the Activity tab, but they do leave your cash: they are added up and named, and the what-if can count them. A move to another of your checking or savings accounts whose other side Nya sees within a week (an ACH can take five business days; pending counts) is neither. A move to one of your accounts in Nya that isn't cash here, such as a health savings account or an investment account, does leave the cash, and is named as such. Where Nya can't tell (a savings account you typed by hand with no transactions, a bank whose transactions may be missing that month, a transfer whose other side may not be in yet), the page says the money may have arrived in your own checking or savings.

  A day's spending is those months' spending over their days, and a month is an average month (365.25 / 12 days). **One-offs are said, never hidden**: an outflow of at least half an average month at a merchant with nothing of a similar size in those months and not a detected bill (a car repair, a laptop) is listed, and so is money in of at least half an average month from a payer with nothing like it (a bonus, a tax refund, a car sold). They stay in unless you leave the one-offs out in the what-if.
- **Other currencies.** Without a [home currency](#a-home-currency), cash, transactions and expected payments in another currency than the runway's are left out and named. Once you choose one, the runway still keeps its currency, your cash accounts' (what a what-if is typed in), and what is in another is converted into it: each transaction at its own day's rate, so the months' spending is their transactions converted, to the cent, and a cash account's balance, what is pending on it, and each loan payment, bill and planned item at the newest rates, each listed with what it was. A bill is still matched to its transactions, and a move out to its other side, by the amount and currency each was in. Only what has no rate is left out, and named with why; the page says what was converted, never an amount.
- **What the forecast changes.** Bills Nya detected that your spending already counts (rent, utilities, subscriptions) are in the average, so they aren't added again and their dates don't move it. A bill on your checking or savings that the spending leaves out, a loan's payment (a mortgage, a car loan), which is a transfer to every total yet leaves your cash each month, is added on its expected dates, as the forecast adds it, and listed. A card's payment never is: the card's charges are in the spending already. A loan payment you filed under a spending category counts in the spending instead. Bills less often than monthly are added on their dates (see above). What you planned on the Budgets tab is added on its days, an expense always and income only while income comes; one in another currency is left out and named.
- **The arithmetic.** In whole cents, as the forecast's: today is the balance now, with what is expected today; from tomorrow each day takes a day's spending, adds a day's income while it comes, and what is expected that day. The cash runs out on the first day it would be below zero, and lasts the whole days before it; months are those days over an average month, to one decimal, rounded down. It looks ten years ahead: one that hasn't run out by then lasts more than ten years, or doesn't run out at all when it is no lower then than now. With no cash to start from, or no transactions in the window to go by, there is no runway to give: the page says which, and draws no chart.
- **A what-if, never stored.** Above the report: the months the spending is read over, spending lower by an amount a month, the day income stops (the line with income then has it only through that day, and once that day has gone it says income stopped), what you planned in or out, the one-offs in or out, and transfers out and cash taken out counted as spending or not. The one-offs and the money moved out can be ticked only when the months chosen have some. It is worked out on the page from what the report read, and nothing about it is sent or saved. **Save as a scenario** keeps one by name, its settings and never its figures (opened again, it is worked out from your data that day), on the storage seam, at most 20, which saves sent at the same moment never go past; **Apply** brings one back and **Delete** removes it. Lower spending saved in another currency than the runway's isn't applied, and the page says so. A saved scenario that can't be read is listed, and can be deleted once you confirm it; one a newer version of Nya saved is only counted, and is never deleted from here.
- **What it can't see**, said before any figure: no transactions to go by, or no cash to start from; months of the window that may be short of transactions (a bank that hasn't synced since a day, one still importing, one removed, and the rest of what a report says may be missing, by the report's own rule, over the months chosen), each payment to a card Nya doesn't see, the transfers out and cash taken out, accounts or balances that couldn't be read, a bank account or card whose transactions Plaid doesn't provide or you didn't allow, cash accounts and expected payments in another currency, a manual cash account with no transactions in the last 12 months (what you spend from it isn't known), a balance you typed and haven't updated for a week, planned items that couldn't be read (then none are in it, and bills you marked not recurring may be), and hidden accounts.
- **From what is stored**, as the other reports, in your browser's time zone: open Nya first to bring your connections up to date. Printed, it has its figures, its what-if in words, and what it is worked out from.

### Year over year

This year so far against the same days of last year (January 1 through today, against January 1 through the same day a year ago; February 29 is the 28th a year away), this month so far against the same days a year ago, each month of this year against the same month last year (whole, but this one), and money in and money out by category group and category, each with this year, last year, the change, and the change in percent ("new" when last year had none, never an infinite rise).

- **The Activity tab's rule.** Every figure is the Activity tab's: a month here is that month on the Activity tab, to the cent, and the year is its months added up. Transfers, cash withdrawals, loan payments and what you excluded aren't in either year, both years are kept in one currency (the one most of their transactions are in, or one you pick), and the rest is left out and named. Once you choose a [home currency](#a-home-currency), both years are in it (or in one you pick), each transaction in another converted at its own day's rate, so each figure is its transactions converted, to the cent; only one with no rate is still left out, and named with why. Each transaction is filed under its category as the Activity tab files it, named as it is now and in the group it is in now, so a category you renamed or moved since last year is compared where it is today.
- **Months that may be short** are marked in words, with the institutions behind them, on the month and on every total that adds one up, by the same rule as the accountant's report; the gaps themselves are said on the first page.
- **Printing** works as for the other reports: the comparison and the months on their own pages, and the groups after them.

Both are in the API and the MCP server too: `/api/v1/runway` (with `months`, and the what-if as `spending_lower_by`, `income_until`, `include_planned`, `leave_out_one_offs` and `count_moved_out`, which change only that answer) and `/api/v1/year-over-year` (with `currency`), read on UTC days, and the tools `get_runway` and `get_year_over_year`. Their version 1 fields convert nothing; once you choose a home currency, `base` gives the figures as the page shows them, the runway's in its cash accounts' currency and year over year's in the home currency. The page asks `GET /api/reports/runway` (`tz` only) and `GET /api/reports/year-over-year` (`tz`, and optionally `currency`), and the scenarios are `GET`, `POST` and `DELETE /api/runway-scenarios`; they are in your data download ([`runway-scenarios`](data-export.md#runway-scenarios)).

## The weekly summary

Off until you turn it on. The **Weekly summary** card under **Manage** on the Accounts tab turns on one short email a week about the week before, on the day you choose (Monday unless you pick another), counted in your time zone: the browser's when you turn it on, which you can change. The card's sheet shows this week's summary: what the email would say if it went now, for the day and zone it shows, saved or not. The one that goes is worked out when it goes, after your transactions are brought up to date, so it can say more, and its link to turn it off is made for each email. **Send me one now** sends you that, at most three times a day. It is built in `lib/weekly-summary/` ([architecture.md](architecture.md#the-weekly-summary)).

- **What it says, in words.** Your spending this week against a usual week: much more, more, about the same, less or much less. A usual week is the middle one (the median) of the eight weeks before, so one big purchase doesn't make every other week look small, counted as every total counts spending: in the currency most of your transactions are in (the others named and left out), without transfers, cash withdrawals or loan and card payments, without what you excluded, and with hidden accounts and accounts that ended left out as the app leaves them out. Then, only those with something to say: the budgets over, or close to their limit (85% of it, the Budgets tab's amber, while the month runs), in the month the week ended in, rollover included, by name; the bills due in the next seven days and the expenses you planned, by payee and day, never their amounts, and a bill expected and not in yet; the goals that reached their target since the last summary (one that stays there is told of once), and those behind their target date, every week while they are; the bank connections that need you, in the words of the [email notices](#email-notices), but for one that can't be repaired, which the summary says just so, since it can't tell, as a notice can, whether connecting it again would help; the months whose transactions may be incomplete, and why, as the Budgets tab says it; and how many of the week's transactions from SimpleFIN or Lunch Flow have no category, naming only the services they came through. A week with nothing to look at says "Nothing needs your attention this week."
- **Never a figure.** No balance, amount, percentage or account number, in the subject or the text. A percentage reveals no amount alone, but beside any figure you know it reveals the rest, and the words say enough. The only numbers are days of the month (and a year where the week crosses into another) and counts of things ("2 bills due", "over 99" from a hundred). For the figures, the email links to the app (`APP_URL`). The names are yours: your budgets and categories, your goals, your planned expenses, and the merchants your bills are from, as the app names them (your renames included), and your banks. Those are personal, and `/privacy` says the email service sees them, and what of them. Any name of yours can spell out an amount, so one is counted instead of named ("Groceries and 1 other") when it has a digit of any script, a currency or percent sign, a number or a currency written as an English word (in any case, plural or run together: "Fifty dollars a week", "Ten thousand for the car", "fivehundred"), a number written as a Spanish, Portuguese, French, Italian or German word ("Diez mil para el coche", "Zehntausend"; not their words for one, which are their word for "a" too, nor due, once, sept, seize, elf or null, which are English words as well), or a letter outside the Latin alphabet, since no list of words can read every script. The list is wide on purpose: a name refused is only counted. "Real" and "won" aren't in it, as common words that say no amount alone ("Real estate"). A bank's name comes from Plaid or SimpleFIN, never from you, so it is named unless it has a digit or a currency or percent sign: "Capital One" is named. Before it goes, the whole email is checked again, against what the summary itself writes: its links, its dates (a weekday that is that date's, a year only near the day it comes), its counts, its own words, and the banks' names and currency codes it wrote ("Transactions in BTC aren't counted in that") aside, anything left that is a digit, a currency or percent sign, a number or currency word, or a letter outside the Latin alphabet stops it, and the log says so.
- **The subject** names the two most pressing things: a connection that needs you, a budget over, bills due, a goal behind, more or less spending than usual, or no transactions ("Your week in Nya: 2 bills due, Groceries over budget"); "a quiet week" when none is; and the week's dates when its spending couldn't be compared.
- **Never a guess.** It reads with the functions the app shows the same things with, so the email never says what the app contradicts. Spending isn't compared while the week's transactions aren't all in (a connection that stopped syncing, or that hasn't come in since the week ended, named with the day it last did), while older transactions are still arriving, with fewer than four earlier weeks of transactions to go by, or when none of your connections bring in transactions; the email says which. A week with no transactions says so. A part whose stored data can't be read is left out, and the email says "Some of your data couldn't be read, so part of this summary is left out. Open Nya to see it all."
- **When it comes.** The daily snapshot sends it, after that day's snapshot, at the run that falls on your day on your calendar: the week is the seven days on your calendar before it, so sent on a Monday it covers Monday to Sunday, and midnight is yours. That run is at 1 p.m. UTC for everyone, so the hour it comes is your time zone's: about 9 a.m. in New York in summer (8 a.m. in winter), 2 p.m. in London in summer (1 p.m. in winter), 6:30 p.m. in India, 10 p.m. in Tokyo, 11 p.m. in Brisbane, and, in Sydney's summer, in Auckland and in Kiritimati, in the first hours of your day, as it begins (it is the run of the day before in UTC). Every calendar has one run a day, so it comes on the day you chose, never the day after; the card and the sheet say the hour for the zone you picked. The catch-up run two hours later sends only what its day's run should have, when that one was held up for you: from UTC+9 to UTC+11 it is already the next day there, and judging the day at its own hour would send the summary there just after midnight, from a run that brings no SimpleFIN bank in. Before it reads, it brings your bank transactions up to date the way opening the app does (one sync per connection, which Plaid doesn't bill per call); if that fails, the summary says how fresh they are. On a day the job couldn't reach you (no time left, a snapshot that failed) it goes with the catch-up run two hours later, or a day or two after. Turning it on, or moving its day, never brings a late one at once: the first comes on the next day you chose. Nor does a move send most of a week again: a summary goes only when at least four of its seven days weren't in the last one, so moving Monday to Tuesday just after Monday's waits for the Tuesday after.
- **Once a week, never twice.** The week is recorded once the email service accepts the summary, and each one carries an idempotency key built from your account's container and the week, so a run again (the catch-up, a cron delivered twice) sends nothing new, and a retry after an answer that never arrived isn't delivered twice. A summary you asked for with **Send me one now** while it is on, for its own day and time zone, counts as that week's, when it could say how the week's spending went: one asked for before a bank's transactions are in (a SimpleFIN bank comes in with the daily run) leaves the week's own to come. Its footer says when the next comes from what you saved, never from a day the sheet shows but you haven't saved. A summary whose answer from the email service never came may have gone: the record marks it, and only after such a try does a refusal of the week's key for a changed email count as sent (the email service's docs don't say a key is kept only for emails it took).
- **Who gets it:** whom the connection notices go to. With Clerk, the primary email address of the account that owns the data, once Clerk has verified it, and only while the account is still allowed in; with the shared password, `NOTIFY_EMAIL`, and only for the deployment's own data. Never a demo account, and never the people you share with.
- **Turning it off.** In the card, or with the link at the bottom of each email, without signing in. Opening the link only shows a page with one button, since mail apps and their security scanners open links on their own; a mail app that offers one-click unsubscribe (`List-Unsubscribe`, RFC 8058) uses it directly. A link works for 90 days, can do nothing but turn off that summary, and stops working once the summary is turned on again after being off. A link that doesn't work says so the same way whatever the reason. Deleting your account deletes the setting and stops the emails.
- **Where it is kept.** The setting is a store of its own, encrypted, and in your data download ([`weekly-summary`](data-export.md#weekly-summary)). Beside it, not in the download: the key its links are signed with, the last week sent (and one that got no answer), and which goals it last said were at their target.

It needs email set up and `APP_URL`, for its links ([deployment.md](deployment.md#email-notices)): without them the card says so, and the preview still shows what it would say. Like the notices, it tells only of Plaid connections; a bank SimpleFIN or Lunch Flow feeds is in its spending and months, not under its connections. Nothing about it is in the read-only API or the MCP server: version 1 is unchanged.

## Keeping Plaid costs down

Plaid bills per linked institution (Item) per month for Transactions, Investments and Liabilities, and per request for its live-balance call. Nya is built to stay on the cheap side of that:

- **One connection per login.** Adding accounts at an institution you already have goes through Plaid's account picker on that connection rather than creating a second one (see [More accounts at an institution you already have](#more-accounts-at-an-institution-you-already-have)).
- **Transactions only where it is used.** A first call for an Item's transactions starts Plaid's Transactions product on an Item that doesn't have it (Plaid's reference counts "calling `/transactions/get` or `/transactions/sync`" as initializing it), and Plaid then bills it monthly until the Item is removed. A brokerage or retirement connection lists Transactions only under Link's `additional_consented_products`, which Plaid's reference says "will not be billed until you start using them by calling the relevant endpoints", so whether it ever starts on one is Nya's call alone. Nya makes that call only for a connection that holds a bank (depository) account, such as checking, savings, money market or cash management, or a card: the accounts whose transactions Nya reads. Plaid's reference says Transactions "supports `credit`, `depository`, and some `loan`-type accounts (only those with account subtype `student` or `mortgage`)", and sends investment accounts to Investments. A loan alone doesn't start it either: its balance and terms come without it, and its payments show on the account they are paid from. If Plaid refuses that first call (it doesn't provide Transactions for those accounts, or you didn't consent to sharing them), Nya remembers that for 30 days rather than asking again on every load. A bank account or card added since is asked about at once, and so is the connection after update mode succeeds on it. For a refused consent, the connection's card offers **Allow transactions**: it opens Plaid's update mode with Transactions listed in `additional_consented_products`, asking for that consent and nothing more (if Plaid refuses a link token with that field, the button opens a plain update instead, once). That update mode collects the consent this way is what the code expects, to be confirmed in Sandbox before the brokerage option is turned on ([deployment.md](deployment.md#brokerage-and-retirement-connections)). A **Reconnect** only repairs a sign-in, and never asks for it. Nya learns whether Plaid already bills Transactions on a connection when you link it (from Plaid's own record of the connection); connections linked before this was recorded all came from the bank option, which always included it.
- **Balances come from `/accounts/get`**, the balances Plaid already holds, not the billed live-balance call. They are as fresh as Plaid's last update of the connection, about daily for a healthy one whether it carries Transactions or only Investments, which is plenty for a daily net-worth snapshot. The cost is that the header's Refresh button re-reads from Plaid but cannot pull a truly live balance.
- **Webhooks** (optional). Set `PLAID_WEBHOOK_URL` to the public URL of `/api/plaid/webhook` on your deployment and Plaid tells Nya when new data is ready. Nya then serves dashboard loads from its own stored data for up to six hours instead of fifteen minutes, and drops that stored copy the moment Plaid says something changed. Refresh still goes to Plaid. Webhooks are verified against Plaid's signature; an unsigned request does nothing. Items linked before you set it are registered by the daily check below, or by reconnecting them.
- **Storage across accounts (admin only).** Under Manage, beside Your data, the admin's **Storage across accounts** (`/admin/storage`) shows every account on the deployment by its random id: its Plaid connections, each with its stored transactions and investment transactions against the size ceiling (`MAX_TXN_BLOB_CHARS`), whether a save was refused for its size or failed, and what the daily check last found (refused by Plaid, or every account hidden, since when); its connections to SimpleFIN or Lunch Flow, and how many still hold their access; what it stores in all and its largest single value; and its last daily snapshot. Sortable and read-only, and nothing of anyone's data is decrypted for it: beside sizes and counts it shows what is kept as plain text (banks' names, Plaid's ids for the connections, the daily check's dates and the snapshot's outcomes) ([operations.md](operations.md#storage-across-accounts)). Anyone else gets the 404 a path that does not exist gets, and its route a 404 too.
- **Unused connections (admin only).** A daily check (`/api/plaid/check-items`) flags a connection, in any account, once Plaid has been unable to read it for 60 days (login expired, consent withdrawn) or every account on it has been hidden for 60 days. **It never removes anything.** The admin, meaning whoever owns the deployment's own account (normally the first to sign in with Clerk, or the password holder), sees the flagged ones under Manage on the Accounts tab, labelled by owner. Other accounts see nothing and the route answers them with a 404. Review and disconnect asks for the institution's name, and the server checks the connection again first: if its owner has reconnected it or unhidden an account, or Plaid does not answer, nothing is removed. Any success starts the count again; an outage or timeout counts for nothing. Change the period with `PLAID_UNUSED_DAYS` (minimum 14).

Liabilities and Investments are paid Plaid products: free in `sandbox`, but billed per Item per month in `production`, so enabling payment details or linking brokerages on many institutions has a running cost. A brokerage or retirement connection is billed for Investments from the day it is linked, since that is the product it asks for, as a bank connection is for Transactions. Transactions starts on it only if Nya makes that first call, for a bank account or card on it.

## The API and the MCP server

Programs you choose can read your data: a script, a spreadsheet, a dashboard of your own, or an AI assistant. Make a token in the **API tokens** card under **Manage** on the Accounts tab; it is shown once, with a copy button and a configuration to paste into an MCP client. The public **Developers** page (`/developers`) documents everything: each endpoint with an example request and answer and every field, the limits, the conventions, and how to add the MCP server to a client. How tokens are made, kept, checked and revoked is in [authentication.md](authentication.md#api-tokens).

- **A read-only REST API** under `/api/v1`: who the token is (`me`), accounts, net worth and its history, transactions (searchable, a page at a time, by tag too, each with its tags, note and the rules that match it, its parts or its group when you [split](#splitting-a-transaction) or [grouped](#grouping-transactions) it, and whether it waits for your [review](#reviewing-new-transactions), `needs_review`, or when you approved it, `reviewed_at`), categories and their groups, tags, rules, budgets, spending by category, group and tag, cash flow (money in by payer, out by group and category), recurring bills, savings goals, holdings, the [runway](#runway) (with a what-if, if you ask for one) and [year over year](#year-over-year). A token is sent as `Authorization: Bearer nya_...`; errors have one shape, `{"error":{"code","message"}}`.
- **An MCP server** at `/api/mcp`, over MCP's streamable HTTP transport (JSON-RPC 2.0, one message per request, plain JSON answers, no sessions), with tools for the same reads: accounts, net worth now and over time, searching transactions, spending by category, cash flow, budgets, recurring bills, categories, tags, rules, savings goals, the runway, year over year and holdings. Each tool answers with a short summary and the same JSON the REST endpoint gives. What came from a bank or was typed in (merchant names, descriptions, account names) is only ever inside that JSON, and the server tells the assistant it is data, not instructions.
- **Stored data only.** Nothing either one does calls Plaid: it reads what Nya has already saved, so asking often adds no Plaid calls. Answers say how fresh that is: each balance's date, when each institution's transactions were last brought in (for transactions and the totals built on them), and whether an account's connection needs you. What can't be read is named and the rest answered: accounts and net worth say `complete: false` and list what they are short of (`missing_accounts`), so a total is never passed off as whole. Hidden accounts are left out unless asked for (`include_hidden=true`). Accounts and transactions SimpleFIN keeps up to date are served beside the rest with `source` `simplefin`: each balance as of the day the bank gave it, none to count for a card or a loan SimpleFIN reports as money held until you answer (net worth then says `complete: false`), each bank's link as Connection health shows it, and each bank among the `sources`, with whether its older history is all in. An account kept as a manual one when you disconnected keeps its own currency. Transactions are the app's own last 365 days, from banks, SimpleFIN, typed in and imported from files alike (each says which in `source`; [rows imported into a linked account](#importing-history-into-a-linked-account) are listed where the bank's own don't stand for them), built exactly as the Activity tab builds them (`lib/activity.ts`), and every total is the one the Budgets tab shows (`lib/totals.ts`): in one currency, without transfers, ATM withdrawals or loan payments, and without what you excluded, which each answer counts and names. Recurring bills are the ones the Budgets tab lists: detected the same way, from the older rows a yearly bill needs too, each with how often it comes, never one you marked not recurring, and added up to a month as the tab adds them, without those that may have ended or that pay a card off. Estimated history points are marked, and the stretch of them walked back through imported transactions is named (`from_imports`). A connection that brings in no transactions (one holding only investment accounts, or no bank account or card, and a bank account or card whose transactions Plaid doesn't provide or you didn't allow) is named in each answer's `sources`, with why, and its notes say what that leaves out, as the Activity and Budgets tabs do, so an empty list or a zero is never passed off as no spending.
- **Accounts that ended** ([above](#accounts-that-end)) are listed with when and how (`lifecycle`: closed, no longer yours or not followed, the day, the note, and whether Nya assumed it), and from that day count in nothing, as in the app: net worth now leaves them out, its history from their end day, and their transactions from it. An end that can't be read fails the answer rather than count an account you closed.
- **A home currency** ([above](#a-home-currency)): every total is in one currency, as it always was, naming what it left out (an account in a cryptocurrency counts under its own code, BTC say). Once you choose a home currency, `me` says it (`base_currency`) and your own rates, accounts give each balance in it (`to_base`), net worth gives `base` (each currency's balances converted once at the newest rates, as Home shows it, with each currency's part, what was left out and why, the rates' day and your own rates used), and balance history gives each point in it (`to_base`), each day at its own rate. An account converted on its own can be a cent per currency away from the total, which converts each currency once. Spending and cash flow give `base`, the same totals over the transactions converted at each one's day's rate, with what was converted, the days of the rates and what was left out and why; each transaction and part gives `to_base`, a split transaction's being what its parts' come to, as totals count it; recurring bills give each bill's `to_base` and their month in `base`, at today's rates; goals give each one's target and saved amount in `to_base`. Budgets give `base`, the same budgets as the Budgets tab counts them, in their own currency with spending in another converted into it, and what was converted. Every version 1 field keeps its meaning: nothing in them is converted. The MCP server's sentences say the converted totals, and what they left out.
- **Plaid's sign convention**, the one Nya keeps: a positive transaction amount is money leaving the account, and what a credit card or loan owes is a positive balance.
- **Version 1 is stable.** Fields may be added, but none removed, renamed or given a new meaning; a change that would do that comes as `/api/v2`, with version 1 kept beside it for at least six months.

Each token may make 100 requests a minute, and one person may have 10 tokens; an address that sends too many tokens that don't work is turned away for a while. Restoring a backup ends every token (backups leave them out), and the demo accounts can't make any. Writing (adding a transaction, changing a budget), signing in with OAuth instead of a token, webhooks, live balances and calls from web pages on other sites (no CORS) are not part of version 1.

## Sharing with someone

With accounts (Clerk), people who connect by an invite link can share parts of their finances with each other, read-only, each choosing what the other sees and for how long, and seeing exactly what that is and each time it was shown. How connecting, ends, previews and the records of showings work is in [authentication.md](authentication.md#sharing-between-people).

What can be shared, each on its own, and none of it until it is chosen:

- **Accounts**, one by one: that it exists, its balance, or its balance and the last 30 days of transactions.
- **Net worth**: the total now, or the total and its history (your chart, a point a week, estimates marked), always as amounts, with or without the accounts in it whose balances you share above, each with its balance. The total is your accounts' newest balances added up, as on the Accounts tab, in the currency most of them are in; an account you share only as existing, or not at all, is in the total and never listed.
- **Spending**: what went out each month, this month so far and the five before, by category group, or by category too, as percentages or amounts, by the same rule as every total in the app. Never money in, and never a transaction, a merchant, a note, a tag, a split's parts, a group of transactions or how many there were: what another currency left out is named by its currency alone.
- **Holdings**: your investments by asset class and by tax bucket, as the Plan tab's allocation counts them with your own buckets and splits, as percentages or amounts, with or without the positions. Never an account's name or number.
- **Budgets**: this month's and last month's, by group, or by category too, as how much of each is spent or as amounts, with what rolled over, as the Budgets tab counts them.
- **Goals** you choose: each one's progress and when it is projected to be reached, as percentages or amounts, never the monthly rate behind the projection.

Hidden accounts are in none of it, and an account that ended counts from its day in nothing, as everywhere else in the app. "This month" is yours: the month in the time zone your weekly summary keeps, else by the UTC day. A kind whose data can't be read just now says so, on their card and in your preview, never a figure short of what it can't read; spending that counts a transaction whose exclusion couldn't be read, as your Activity tab does, says that too, and spending and budgets say when some of how you file transactions couldn't be read (your own categories, your merchants' default categories or what you approved in the review queue), so some of it may be under another category. Spending says it may be incomplete while a bank account or card's transactions don't come in (Plaid doesn't provide them, or you didn't allow them), unless the files you imported into its connection's bank accounts and cards cover every month shown, through today, as your Activity tab counts them. Percentages read as your own screens write them: money that is there is never 0%, and a share short of the whole never 100%.

**What they could work out.** Shares can give away more together than one by one: a net worth beside an account's balance, or beside your holdings in amounts, gives what the rest add up to; spending beside net worth gives an estimate of income; a goal's progress and when it will be reached give what goes into it each month; holdings as percentages beside one investment account's balance, or beside a net worth with every other balance shown, give their amounts; budgets as percentages beside spending in amounts give the budgets. Before you save, under your choices, Sharing says plainly which of these combinations what you share with someone makes, and "See what they see" says it again from what they are shown (`lib/share-inference.ts`). It never stops a save, and it says that no list is complete: any total says something about what is in it, and these are only the combinations Nya knows.

**Joint budgets** (two people keeping one budget together) aren't built: sharing is read-only, always, and whether a joint container is in scope is the owner's decision (decision 8 of the plan).

## Downloading your data

Under **Manage** on the Accounts tab, **Download my data** gives you everything Nya stores about you, decrypted, after a fresh sign-in, five times an hour. Field by field, with what is left out and why, in [data-export.md](data-export.md).

- **Formats.** Everything as one JSON file; your transactions, or your balance history, as a CSV file for a spreadsheet; or one bank account's or card's posted transactions as an OFX statement (`lib/ofx-export.ts`), the file money apps import (GnuCash, Actual Budget and YNAB take it; Quicken takes such files only from banks it works with, and Monarch takes CSV). Each transaction's FITID is its id in Nya, so importing the same file twice, into Nya's own import or an app that honours FITIDs, adds nothing; signs are OFX's, days are the bank's, an excluded transaction is listed and marked in its memo, and the balance is the latest Nya recorded, with its day where you are, or none. A linked account's statement holds the history you [imported into it](#importing-history-into-a-linked-account) from its bank's files too, each transaction once. An account SimpleFIN keeps up to date is offered too: its transactions as the app shows them, a payment or a move between two of your SimpleFIN accounts typed as a bank's file types it (`PAYMENT`, `XFER`), and the balance SimpleFIN last gave, as of the bank's own day, or none while Nya holds it. Loans and investment accounts aren't offered as OFX, and the card says why.
- **A passphrase.** Any format can be protected with a passphrase of at least 12 characters, typed twice. The file is then encrypted as it is sent, in the [age](https://age-encryption.org) format, and saved with `.age` at the end of its name. It opens on the public **Open a protected download** page (`/open-download`), in the browser, without signing in and without the file leaving the device, or with the age app (`age -d`). Nya never keeps the passphrase, so a lost one can't be recovered.
- **An email each time.** With email set up, each download emails you when it happened and which format, never what is in it, with what to do if it wasn't you; so does making an API token. The download never waits for the email or fails because of it.

## Your data

Under **Manage** on the Accounts tab, beside Download my data, **Your data** (`/your-data`) lists every kind of thing Nya keeps about you: the data map the privacy page promises, for your own account. It is built from the same lists the download and the key inventory walk (`lib/inventory.ts`: every key family stored the old way and every store declared on the storage seam), so a store added later is listed by being declared, and a test fails until it has an entry.

Each kind, under a heading (banks and accounts, balances and history, transactions, imports, budgets and goals, settings, sharing, sign-in and keys, and the app's own records), says:

- **How much**: how many entries are stored (a day of history, an account, a bank connection's transactions) and their size, as stored.
- **Encrypted**: whether it is, and with which key: your account's own, or one the deployment shares between accounts (the original single key, or a shared data key, until the [migration pass](operations.md#moving-existing-data-to-keys-of-its-own-the-migration-pass) moves your data). Each value's key is read from what the value says of itself, its first bytes, never by opening it; a value in an encrypted kind that doesn't look encrypted at all is counted as one that can't be read.
- **Plain text**: what is stored around it unencrypted, said as such: the days with a balance as field names, account and transaction ids, the merchant names you renamed, Plaid's ids in the names transactions are kept under, and the kinds that hold no ciphertext at all.
- **In your download**, from the download's own lists, with where in the file.
- **In the nightly backups**, from the backup's own lists (`lib/export.ts`), where the deployment takes backups.
- **How long it is kept**, as the row of the privacy page's retention table it is kept by, and for a count that limits how often, its window.
- **Who else can see it**: Plaid, SimpleFIN or Lunch Flow where it came from; people you share with, programs you give an API token to and the email service, wherever what they are shown is read or worked out from it (a setting that decides what a share shows counts, for that share). These three are held to the code: `test/inventory-seers.test.ts` stores something of every kind first (an account link and what it carries, a bank brought back and linked, files imported into a manual and a linked account, an account let go from SimpleFIN; only locks, counts and a few records only their own job reads are excused, each with why), then records every family read while every API endpoint and MCP tool answers, while everything that can be shared is shown at its widest, and while the weekly summary and the connection notices are gathered, and fails when a kind's list says otherwise. A path opened only by a particular value, rather than by a kind holding something, is held as far as that test's data goes. Whoever runs the deployment can read all of it, and the page says once at the top what the database and the host hold.

The few kinds kept for the whole deployment rather than in your account are counted for you where that is cheap and plain: your account's record, which sign-in it belongs to, your sharing connections and your data key. What is about nobody by name (failed sign-ins by address, the backups' last outcome, the ECB's rates) is listed apart and not counted.

**Numbers only.** Nothing is decrypted and no stored value leaves the database: your account is measured through the storage seam (`measureContainer` in `lib/repo.ts`), which answers sizes, counts and each value's claimed key. Hide amounts leaves the page as it is, since counts and sizes are no amounts. A kind that can't be read just now says so in place of its figures, never "0"; if your account can't be read at all, every kind says so; and a key in your account that no kind names is counted as such, never left out. The page reads nothing it could change: it is a list, with no button that deletes. Each look walks the database's keyspace, as the restore's look at whether an account is empty does, so it is counted the same way: thirty an hour (`lib/inventory-limit.ts`). Each value's key is read from its first bytes inside the database, a few megabytes of values at most each step, so a value past that is said to be not looked at, for its size, and the database is never held up for long.

## Bringing back a download

A JSON download brings everything in it back into another copy of Nya, or into a new account after you deleted yours: **Manage**, then **Bring back a download**, or on the first screen of an account with nothing in it yet. Where it is offered is the operator's choice (`RESTORE_ENABLED`, [operations.md](operations.md#turning-on-restores)). In full, with what is checked and why, in [data-export.md](data-export.md#bringing-it-back).

- **Into an empty account only**, since it brings back a whole copy; settings alone are allowed, and the file's replace them. An account that has data can be emptied first (a fresh sign-in, and typing `empty my account`), refused while a bank, SimpleFIN or Lunch Flow is connected.
- **The file stays on your device.** It is read and checked in the page, a few megabytes at a time, a protected one opened with its passphrase there; anything this version doesn't know refuses the whole file. The page says what comes back and what doesn't before anything is written, and a damaged entry the file names is left out only once you have seen which transactions it affects.
- **Everything you made comes back with its ids**: categories, tags, rules, manual accounts and their transactions, imports, what you said about transactions, links, budgets and their months, goals and their plans, the Plan, holdings history, every layer of balance history, settings. A connection to SimpleFIN or Lunch Flow comes back waiting for a new Setup Token or API key.
- **A bank connected through Plaid** comes back as earlier accounts with its posted transactions held, not counted, until you connect it again and link each account to its new one ([Removing an institution and adding it back](#removing-an-institution-and-adding-it-back)); then they count once. Or stop following it. Until then no net worth total is recorded, and the total says why.
- **Never**: pending transactions, credentials (bank access, the SimpleFIN Access URL, the Lunch Flow API key, API tokens), sharing and the app's machinery. Investment transactions are kept for your next download, not shown yet.
- **Unfinished**, every tab says so, no total is recorded, the API answers 503, shares show nothing and the weekly summary waits; go on with the same file, or undo. **Undo** empties the account again, offered while unfinished and for 7 days after, with the download offered first. Starts, undos and emptyings are limited to 5 a day, and each emails you.

## Planning

The **Plan** tab answers two questions: how much you need to be financially independent (FI), and how a retirement on that money would have lasted through US market history. Everything is in today's dollars. The results are hypothetical: they come from US history, or sequences resampled from it, and are not a prediction and not advice. The tab says so beside every result, with the assumptions behind it.

### The FI figures

- **FI number**: your annual spending divided by the withdrawal rate (4% by default), grossed up for the tax on withdrawals: `spending / (1 - tax) / rate`. Spending $40,000 at 4% with no tax needs $1,000,000.
- **Years to FI**: how long your invested assets take to reach the FI number, adding your annual savings at the end of each year and earning a steady real return (5% a year after inflation by default). It says "Not at this rate" when savings and growth never get there.
- **Coast FI**: what you need invested today for it to grow to the FI number by your target age with nothing more added, at the same return.
- **Barista FI**: the FI number when part-time income (after tax) covers part of your spending, so the portfolio covers only the rest.
- **At your target age**: the balance projected at the steady return. Real markets don't move steadily, which is what the simulation is for.

### Where the inputs come from

Each input says where it came from, over what dates, and what may be missing from it, and any of them can be typed over. A typed figure stays until you switch back to Nya's.

- **Annual spending**: the last 365 days of money out, with the first and last day shown beside it. It starts from the Activity tab's rule, which leaves out transfers between your own accounts and card payments (paying a card off settles purchases already counted on the card), and then counts what that rule leaves out for reasons that don't hold for planning (`lib/fire/inputs.ts`; the Activity tab's own totals are unchanged):
  - **Loan payments** that Plaid's detail says are on a mortgage, car, student or personal loan, and payments Nya pairs from a SimpleFIN checking or savings account into one you said is a loan there ([transfers and categories](#bring-your-own-sync)). Their principal is really saving, but it is spending until the loan ends, which is what a plan starting today has to fund. The label says how much of the figure they are; if a loan ends before you stop working, type your own figure. The loan account's side of a payment (money in) is never counted, so a payment from one linked account to another counts once.
  - **Cash withdrawals** (at an ATM or a teller): cash taken out is spent. If you enter what you spend in cash by hand (or import it), on a manual account marked **Cash on hand**, the withdrawals and those transactions are the same money, so over the year only the larger counts, never both: the cash spending you entered always, and the withdrawals only for what is beyond it. Over the whole year, so cash taken out at the end of one month and spent in the next isn't counted twice, and if you have only just begun entering cash, the withdrawals before that still count. Only an account marked so: spending entered on a manual checking account at a bank Plaid can't reach is not cash, and never cancels a withdrawal. The label says which it did, and on which accounts; with withdrawals counted beside spending entered on an account not marked as cash, it says how to mark one.
  - **Refunds**: money back in a spending category (food, shopping, travel, bills and the like) is taken off spending rather than counted as income, which would overstate both. Money in under income, a transfer or "other" is never a refund. The total and the largest refund are shown beside the figure, so an odd large one (a deposit returned, an insurance payout) can be seen and typed over.

  A transaction you [excluded](#excluding-a-transaction) counts in none of the figures, and the label says how many were left out. Paying a card off never counts, whatever the row was recategorized as: it settles purchases already counted on the card. Anything else filed under loan payments (no detail, Plaid's "other payment", which can be a store card, or a row recategorized there whose detail says something else, a card payoff among them) can't be told from a card payment, so it is left out and its total is named beside the figure, never guessed: Plaid's apart from the ones you entered, imported or categorized there yourself, which have no detail at all. A pending charge whose posted version has arrived was already dropped. With less than a year of history the total is scaled up to a year, and the label says from how many days or months. Under four weeks gives no figure.
- **Invested assets**: your investment accounts at their balances when the dashboard last loaded them (the time is shown), without hidden ones, plus checking and savings if you tick the box. Debts are not subtracted. Every investment account counts, a 529 or an HSA included: [Allocation](#allocation) shows each account's tax bucket, but the plan doesn't model them, so type your own total to leave one out.
- **Annual savings**: an estimate. A year of income minus spending from bank data, plus what went into workplace retirement plans over the year: a 401(k), 403(b), 457(b), TSP, SIMPLE IRA and similar, by the account's type, from Nya's verified investment transactions, an employer's match included where the plan reports it as a contribution. Payroll contributions never pass through a bank account, so bank data alone misses them. Nothing is counted twice. Under Edit, say how each plan is paid into, and the choice is saved with your plan:
  - **Payroll**: every contribution is added.
  - **My bank** (a Solo 401(k) you fund from checking, say): none is, since the transfers that paid for them already count as saved.
  - **Not set**, the default: a contribution is added unless a transfer that Plaid files as going to investment and retirement funds paid for it (the same amount within a dollar or 1%, within five days; several contributions on one day, a deferral and an employer share, by their sum). A move to savings or to another person never counts as one.

  Contributions to brokerages and IRAs are not added: they are usually paid from a bank account, where income minus spending has already counted them. The label says, for each plan, how many contributions were added and the largest, so you can see what counted, what matched a transfer and wasn't added, and which plans aren't set. A plan whose verified record covers less than the year is named with the day it starts; the server decides that on its own days, so it never depends on your time zone. When the institution's own activity for a plan starts inside the year (it may keep less history than Nya asked for), the label says "Nya has activity for it from" that day. With no workplace plan linked, the label says pre-tax contributions and a match are missing.
- **Ages and assumptions**: typed by you. Your age places Social Security, pensions and one-off expenses in the plan. The target age can't be before your age: if you have already stopped working, use your age. A pension that doesn't rise with inflation is entered in today's dollars, not the amount quoted for the year it starts.

A figure that may be short says so beside it, and the FI number and the progress under it repeat the warning for spending and invested assets. In particular:

- An institution whose transactions couldn't all be read (it needs reconnecting, is still syncing, or its stored history couldn't be read) is named beside spending and savings, and so are the transactions of a manual account, or imported into a linked account from files, that couldn't be read. If the transactions couldn't be loaded at all, spending says that rather than "not enough transactions". The Activity tab's other notes (a split or a group that couldn't be read, rules or categories that couldn't be applied) don't: every transaction is there, so nothing is missing from the figures.
- A bank account or card that Plaid doesn't provide transactions for, or whose transactions you didn't allow, is named beside spending, which may be low without it, and savings, which may be off, and the FI number repeats the warning. When no connection brings in spending at all (investment accounts only, say) and there are no transactions entered by hand or imported either, spending and savings say why and ask you to type them, rather than waiting for transactions that won't come. With transactions entered by hand or imported, spending and savings are measured from them as usual, and the connections that bring in none are named beside them.
- An institution that couldn't be reached is named beside invested assets: not counted when nothing was recovered from it, or counted at its last-known balances, with their date. Accounts it is known to have that couldn't be shown, and accounts with no balance, are counted in the same notes.
- A workplace plan whose activity couldn't be read is named beside savings.
- Without a [home currency](#a-home-currency), Nya doesn't convert currencies. Spending and savings add up the currency most of the year's transactions are in, and invested assets the one most of the accounts are in; what either left out in others is named beside it. Investments in one currency and spending in another are said beside the figures, since the FI number and the assets can't then be compared. With one, the Plan stays in the currency most of its accounts are in, which what you typed in it is in, and converts the rest into it: the year's transactions each at its day's rate, the accounts at the newest rates. One note says what was converted, and only what has no rate is left out.

### Will it last?

The simulation runs a retirement through market history, withdrawing by a rule each year, for the length you choose: to age 95 by default, or 30 years when no age is set. Where it starts sets what the first year spends, and the card says which:

- **Your FI number at your target age** (the default): the first year withdraws your withdrawal rate of it, which after tax is your spending, since that is how the FI number was set.
- **Your invested assets today**, or **a balance you type**: the first year withdraws your spending (grossed up for tax), at whatever rate that implies, so the result answers whether what you spend now would have lasted from there. The card shows the rate and says when it is above the one your FI number assumes. The grid adds it as a row up to 20%.

VPW is the exception: it sets its own spending from the balance and the years left, so the card compares its first year with what you spend.

There are two methods:

- **Historical cycles** start the plan in every month from January 1871 that leaves its whole length inside the data (1,470 starts for 30 years) and live through what actually followed, the idea behind cFIREsim and FIRECalc, on monthly rather than annual data.
- **Monte Carlo** runs 5,000 sequences, each built from five-year blocks of consecutive months drawn at random from the same history (a circular block bootstrap). A block keeps the months together, so stocks, bonds and inflation stay matched, as do runs of good or bad years shorter than a block; longer cycles are cut at block edges, so the results spread wider than the history's. The draws are seeded, so the same plan always gives the same answer.

While a new answer is worked out, the last one keeps its place but is hidden, since it answered other assumptions.

**Success** means the portfolio never ran out: every year's withdrawal was paid in full to the end of the plan. The success rate is the share of starts (or runs) that succeeded. A flexible rule (all but the constant one) lasts by cutting spending when markets fall, so its success rate comes with a second figure: the lowest year's spending as a share of the first year's, in the worst start (or run) that lasted. The fan chart shows the balance each year at the 10th, 25th, 50th, 75th and 90th percentiles, with a table of the same figures. The worst starting years are those that ran out soonest, or, when none did, came closest (the lowest spending, then the lowest balance left), listed once per calendar year.

**The grid** repeats the plan at 3% to 5% and 20 to 50 years, plus your own rate and length, with everything else as in the plan. For a flexible rule each cell carries the second figure too, and VPW, which has no rate to vary, gets one row. In historical cycles each length has its own starts, every month that leaves the whole length inside the data: a 30-year plan can start up to June 1993, a 50-year one only up to June 1973. Each column says where its starts end, and the note under the grid gives each one's count and span. A longer column leaves out the latest starts, so it can read safer than a shorter one: at 4%, 60 years can beat 50 by missing the late-1960s retirements. Monte Carlo columns share the same runs, each extended to the longer lengths, so for a rule that ignores the length (constant, percent of portfolio, floor and ceiling) a longer column never reads safer; guardrails and VPW withdraw by the years left, so theirs can differ slightly either way. A one-off dated after the plan ends is shown as such: only the grid's longer columns reach it.

Each plan year, in order:

1. The rule sets the year's withdrawal from the balance at the start of the year.
2. Other income (Social Security, a pension, after tax) pays part of that year's spending, so the portfolio withdraws less; income beyond the spending is invested. One-off expenses are added. What the portfolio pays is grossed up for the flat tax.
3. If that is more than the balance, the plan has run out that year.
4. The rest earns the next twelve months' returns, at your mix, rebalanced every year by default (every month, or never, are the options).
5. Fund fees are charged (0.1% a year by default).

Withdrawals are taken once a year at the start of the year, the convention of the annual studies, slightly conservative against spreading them out. Returns compound monthly. Cash keeps up with inflation and earns nothing more: Shiller's data has no short-term rate, and the 10-year yield would credit cash with a bond's returns.

### The withdrawal rules

- **Constant (the 4% rule)**: the first year withdraws the rate times the starting balance, and every later year the same amount in today's dollars, whatever the market does.
- **Percent of portfolio**: every year withdraws the rate times the balance at the start of that year. It cannot run out; spending moves with the market, and the result shows how far it fell.
- **Guardrails (Guyton-Klinger)**: starts at the rate, then keeps the amount steady in today's dollars, except that after a year the portfolio lost money the raise for inflation is skipped while the withdrawal is above the starting rate (of the current balance); above 120% of that rate it is cut by 10% (not in the last 15 years), and below 80% it is raised by 10%.
- **VPW (variable percentage withdrawal)**: each year withdraws the share an annuity would pay over the years left, at an expected real return from your mix (5% for stocks, 2% for bonds, 0% for cash). It spends the portfolio down to zero at the end, on purpose.
- **Floor and ceiling**: each year withdraws the rate times the balance, kept between a floor and a ceiling set as shares of the first year's withdrawal (90% and 125% by default).

The exact definitions are in `lib/fire/rules.ts`.

### The data, and its license

The history is Robert Shiller's monthly US data (the price and dividends of the S&P Composite, which is the S&P 500 since 1957, the consumer price index and the long-term government bond yield, the 10-year Treasury's since 1953), January 1871 to June 2023, as packaged by [datasets/s-and-p-500](https://github.com/datasets/s-and-p-500). The package extends the price past June 2023 without the other columns, so the history stops there. The packaging is licensed under the ODC Public Domain Dedication and License (ODC-PDDL-1.0), but **Shiller's own page states no license terms** beyond a disclaimer. Check before any paid use.

`scripts/fire-data.ts` turns the package's CSV into `lib/fire/history-data.ts`: monthly real stock returns, real bond returns and inflation, in millionths. It is reproducible, and the module records the source URL (a pinned commit), the SHA-256 of the file and a SHA-256 of the rows used:

```bash
curl -sSLo /tmp/sp500.csv https://raw.githubusercontent.com/datasets/s-and-p-500/07b81e6af68239acd65b901a11844d6d95db6ead/data/data.csv
bun run fire-data /tmp/sp500.csv
```

The derivations are in `lib/fire/derive.ts`:

- **Stocks**: total return over a month is `(next price + next dividend / 12) / price - 1`. Shiller's dividends are annualized, so a month earns a twelfth.
- **Bonds**: a 10-year bond bought at par at this month's yield and sold a month later, with 9 years 11 months left, at next month's yield, plus a month of coupon: the standard constant-maturity approximation. Shiller's long rate is the 10-year Treasury yield from 1953 and a long-term government bond yield before, so the early bonds are an approximation too.
- **Real returns**: `(1 + nominal) / (1 + inflation) - 1`, from the CPI. Inflation is applied this once; the engine never adjusts a withdrawal for it again.

### How it is checked

The engine is pure functions (`lib/fire/`), run in the browser. Property tests check that no value appears from nowhere (with no returns, the balance is the start plus deposits minus withdrawals), that inflation is applied exactly once, that a higher withdrawal rate never raises the success rate, that a seed reproduces a Monte Carlo run, and that random plans give no NaN or negative balance. The flexible rules have their own: VPW never runs out and ends at exactly zero at the end of any plan, percent of portfolio never runs out or reaches zero at any rate, mix or method, and guardrails never cut in the final 15 years.

Golden tests check that the results are consistent with the figures published studies report, as ranges, not against the same inputs run through other tools: the studies used annual data, other periods and other bonds, which move the answer by a few points. 4% over 30 years succeeds in 96% to 97.5% of the historical starts with half or more in stocks, consistent with the roughly 95% or more that the Trinity study, Bengen, FIRECalc and cFIREsim report for the classic case; bond-heavy mixes do much worse, as Trinity found; longer plans need lower rates; and the failures and near misses all start in the known bad periods (around the 1929 crash, the mid-1960s, and the early 1900s). Nothing here was compared with FIRECalc, cFIREsim or Early Retirement Now on identical settings. The tests name each source.

### Limits

- **US history only**, in US inflation terms. Accounts in other currencies are shown in their own currency but simulated on US data, and nothing is converted between currencies.
- **Taxes are one flat rate** on withdrawals: no brackets, capital gains, account types (taxable, tax-deferred, Roth: [Allocation](#allocation) shows them, the simulation doesn't use them), required minimum distributions or subsidies.
- **No fund look-through.** Nya knows the mix of the broad index funds on its list and of a security that is one class by its type; any other fund (a target-date fund, whose mix moves every year, or an actively managed balanced fund such as Wellington) is unclassified until you split it under [Allocation](#allocation). A balanced fund whose index or written policy fixes its mix (Vanguard Balanced Index, the LifeStrategy funds, the iShares Core allocation ETFs) is on the list. The simulation's mix is what you set, or what you take from your allocation.
- **One history.** Monte Carlo reshuffles it in five-year blocks, so it can string bad stretches together, but every month in it is a month that happened.
- **Spending from bank data** misses anything paid from accounts you haven't connected, and last year's spending may not be what retirement costs.

The tab saves only your assumptions (one encrypted value per account, the `fire-plan` store on the storage seam, `lib/fire-plan.ts`), never a result; everything else is worked out again from your data each time it opens. A plan saved under an earlier release always reads back: reading checks only its shape, and fills in any field added since, while today's ranges apply when it is saved again. If a saved value is one this release doesn't take (a range narrowed since, say), the tab says which, uses the default in its place (or leaves out the income or one-off it belongs to, named by its label or, without one, by its amount and age), and offers to save the plan that way. Nothing else saves it: editing is paused until you choose to, so nothing is dropped by accident, and the tab never stops working over it. A plan a later release saved, which this one doesn't understand, is reported as such and left untouched. Its code, with the engine and the market history, loads only when the tab is first opened. If that fails (offline before it was ever fetched, or a flaky connection), the tab says so and offers to try again, and the other tabs work as before. The simulation runs in a Web Worker, and the grid there one cell at a time, so the page stays responsive while they are worked out. The jobs wait on the page and go to the worker one at a time, so when the plan changes again before they run, the old ones are dropped and the new answer never waits behind them. Where a worker can't start, the same work runs on the page, one piece at a time between paints. If neither can run (the connection dropped before the engine's code arrived), the card says the simulation couldn't be loaded and offers to try again.

### Allocation

On the Plan tab, under the FI figures: what your investment accounts hold, by asset class and by tax bucket, against a target if you set one, and over time. It is on the Plan tab because it is what the simulation's mix comes from, and the tab's code loads only when it opens, so the classifier and the history read cost the other tabs nothing (`components/AllocationCard.tsx`, `lib/allocation/`).

- **Asset class**, for each position, from the first of these that speaks for it:
  1. a split you set for its ticker, or, for a security with no ticker (a 401(k)'s collective trust, say), for its name;
  2. the rule the uninvested-cash badge uses: Plaid's cash-equivalent flag, its cash type, the big brokerages' settlement funds, a money market fund's name;
  3. Nya's list of broad index funds and ETFs, each with the split its mandate fixes: VTI and VOO 100% US stocks, VXUS 100% international, BND 100% bonds, VNQ 100% real estate, VBIAX 60% US stocks and 40% bonds, the Vanguard LifeStrategy and iShares Core allocation funds by their stock and bond split. Never a target-date fund, whose mix moves every year (`lib/allocation/funds.ts`);
  4. Plaid's security type, where the security is itself one class: a company's stock, a bond or CD, a coin, an option (as "other").

  A stock's region is a class of its own when it isn't known. Plaid's equity type covers domestic and foreign companies alike, and a world fund's split between US and international moves with markets, so these are **stocks, region unknown**: counted as stocks in the plan's mix, but never called US or international.
- **Unclassified**, never a guess: a fund the list doesn't have, a leveraged or inverse fund (known by its ticker, or by words in a fund's name: a multiple such as "2x", "3X" or "-1x", Direxion Daily, UltraPro, UltraShort, Inverse, Leveraged, Ultra, Short, Bull or Bear, though not a fund of short-dated bonds or of leveraged loans; a company whose name has such a word, 10x Genomics say, is a stock, and a plain fund from an issuer that also makes leveraged ones, ProShares' NOBL say, is a fund like any other), even when Plaid types it as a stock, since its return is a multiple of its index's or the opposite, a security Plaid gives no type for, money in an account that no position it lists explains (often cash an institution doesn't list as a position), an account that lists no positions at all (a manual investment account, or one at a connection that offers none), an account whose positions didn't come this time (the holdings call failed for it, or its answer left it out: `/api/net-worth` marks it `holdings_unanswered`), which is unclassified whole and never split, since a split is for money beyond positions and what it holds isn't known, and an account whose institution couldn't be reached (its balance may be recovered, but not what it holds). It is shown as its own share, part by part, each with **Classify**: a split for a security, or for an account's money that no position explains. A split that doesn't add up to 100% is never used. Positions worth more than their account's balance (a margin loan the institution nets out, or prices from another time) are counted as listed, and named.
- **Tax bucket**, for each investment account, from Plaid's account subtype: taxable, tax-deferred, Roth, HSA or education, or unclassified where the subtype doesn't settle it (a "retirement" account of no named kind, a non-taxable brokerage account, a mutual fund account, a GIC, an insurance policy). Every investment subtype Plaid lists is decided, with a test each (`lib/allocation/buckets.ts`); Canadian and UK accounts go by how they are taxed, so a TFSA or an ISA is with Roth, an RRSP or a SIPP with tax-deferred. A 401(k) can hold Roth money and its subtype won't say so: change any account's bucket.
- **One currency**, the Plan's. Without a home currency, an account or position in another is left out and named with its amount; nothing is converted. With one, each balance and position in another is converted into the Plan's at the newest rates, and only one with no rate is left out; the allocation over time doesn't convert yet, and says so. Hidden accounts are left out. The line under the title says when the balances were loaded.
- **Shares** are whole percents that add up to 100 (the largest remainder), except that money that is there never reads 0% ("<1%") and a share short of the whole never reads 100% (">99%"). Less than half a cent is what adding and taking away fractions leaves behind, not money: it has no row and no share. A short position or cash borrowed on margin counts as the negative amount it is: a leveraged account shows stocks above 100% and cash below zero, and the bar leaves the negative part out and says so.
- **Target and drift.** Set the allocation you aim for by class, in percents that add up to 100, giving stocks as one share or as US and international (not both). Drift is each class's share of what is classified against its target, in percentage points and in the money it would take to get back to it. Unclassified money is left out of both sides, and said. Under a target split by region, stocks of an unknown region have a row with no target; under a target with no stocks at all, every stock is held against 0%, whatever its region. Shares there follow the shares' rule at a tenth of a point ("<0.1%", ">99.9%"), and while your settings load, the target says it is loading rather than that none is set.
- **For your plan.** The mix the simulation can take: stocks of every region together, bonds and cash, with checking and savings as cash when the plan counts them as invested assets. Unclassified money is never counted as stocks or bonds, and real estate, crypto and "other", which the simulation has no history for, are left out too; each is named with its amount. **Use my allocation in the plan** opens a confirmation with the plan's mix now, the one it would take and what that leaves out, and nothing changes until you confirm it; the plan then keeps that mix until you change it. The simulation's own form offers to fill it in, saved only with Save. With cash below zero, none is offered: a mix of shares can't hold it.
- **Over time.** The mix on each day [holdings history](architecture.md#holdings-history) recorded, of the accounts the allocation above shows, worked out by the allocation itself (`allocate`) from what was recorded that day: the positions, classified with your splits as they are now, and the balance measured beside them, so money no position explains is unclassified (or split, as you set it), a manual investment account counts by its balance, an account whose positions didn't come that day (its institution answers for it on other days) counts its whole balance as unclassified and never by its split, an account in another currency is left out whole, and a position with no currency takes its account's, exactly as today's figures do; the readout and the table say how much of each day's figure no position explains, and the notes name the accounts it was in. It starts on the first recorded day ("Recorded from" it, and "shown from" the first day of the year it reads, when recording began earlier) and is never drawn before it, with a day nothing was recorded on left as a gap. An account with neither positions nor a balance recorded on a day (its institution couldn't be reached) is missing from it: the day is drawn faint and marked, and the readout and the table name the account and why ("not recorded since Oct 2, 2026", "first recorded on Oct 4, 2026"). An account still linked is expected on every day after it was first recorded, and after the day the account directory first knew it: the accounts the allocation shows, those whose institution the dashboard can't show at all (an outage past the 35 days balances are recovered for, or an account missing from the last snapshot), which the Plan sends too, and any other the server still remembers for a connection still stored or finds missing pending confirmation. So an institution that stops answering, however long for, or was failing when recording began, never leaves those days drawn as if complete; the notes under the chart say which accounts the latest days leave out, and since when, and repeat the allocation's own caveats ("Fidelity couldn't be reached and isn't counted, so the latest days may be short"). An account whose connection is gone (removed, or the account closed) is counted from its positions alone on the days it was recorded (its currency isn't known, so its balance can't be set against them, and a position of its with no currency is left out), expected only between its first and last recorded days, and named as not linked now. Up to a year, from `POST /api/allocation-history` with the accounts the dashboard knows, which reads one month at a time and answers 409, never an empty series, when what is stored can't be read; a day whose balances can't be read is left out and said. A table of every day sits behind "Show as a table".

What you choose (buckets, splits, the target) is saved as one encrypted value, the `allocation-settings` store on the storage seam (`lib/allocation-settings.ts`), through `/api/allocation-settings`, which checks every field: a ticker as Plaid sends it (up to 40 characters) or a name (up to 200), with no control characters (Classify is offered only for a security whose ticker or name a save takes), percents with at most one decimal adding up to 100, no account, ticker or name twice, and at most 200 buckets, 300 fund splits and 200 account splits. While the settings load, or if they can't be read, the figures leave them out and say so, and editing waits. They are in your [data download](data-export.md#allocation-settings).

### FI progress on Home

A small card on Home with the Plan's FI number, years to FI and progress, and your savings rate over the last 12 months: what was saved as a share of income, with contributions to workplace plans counted on both sides, since they are income that never reached a bank. It is the year as the Plan counts it: a transaction you [excluded](#excluding-a-transaction) is on neither side, nor is one in another currency, and the card names them in the Plan's own words, as it names invested accounts in another currency. It reads your saved plan and measures from the same inputs with the same arithmetic as the Plan tab (`lib/fire/progress.ts`, `components/plan-inputs.ts`), so the two always agree, and the Plan tab's savings line shows the same rate: with your savings typed into the plan, it is still the rate your transactions measure, which the Plan's line gives beside the savings you typed and Home says it is. Every figure is labelled an estimate, with the Plan's note that pre-tax contributions to a plan Nya can't see, and an employer's match, aren't in bank data, and a figure that may be short says so as the Plan's does, by the same rule: transactions that couldn't be read, or a bank account or card whose transactions Plaid doesn't provide or you didn't allow. With only investment connections and transactions you entered by hand, it names the connections that bring in none, as the Plan does; with no transactions from anywhere, there is no spending to work from, so Home shows no card and the Plan tab says why.

With no plan saved it shows what the Plan's defaults give (a 4% withdrawal rate and a 5% real return), says so, and links to the Plan tab: your spending over a named rate is worth seeing before anything is set, and nothing in it is a guess about you. It shows nothing while what it needs loads, without spending to work from, or when your plan can't be read (the Plan tab says why). It sits below the insights, so appearing once its figures are in moves nothing above it. It runs no simulation, and its code loads after Home paints, as the Plan tab's does (`components/FiProgressLoader.tsx`); if that fails, Home works as before without it.
